## Three-Level Scope Framework

The three-tiered work does not simply scale up or down the same concept. The Coordinated Research Area focuses on innovation chains, climate resilience, and regional coordination within approximately 43.6 square kilometers; the Overall Design Area focuses on Urban Renewal structures, Public Spaces, transportation infrastructure, and industrial bearing within approximately 11.4 square kilometers; the three key areas together cover about 368.4 hectares, which are responsible for detailed scenarios that can be tested. The above scale is derived from the task materials, and the specific geometry will be finalized for official release. [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [source:PROCESSED-FACT-PACK] This proposal aims to cascade the strategic, structural, and place-based elements through a [depth:three_level_scope_framework], ensuring a hierarchical transmission of these components. Examine each layer to ensure that there are clear spatial anchors [[depth:overall_spatial_structure]], operational mechanisms, and review interfaces.

The overall structure is summarized as "one spine, three cool islands, two wings, six cool passage entrances, and twelve recallable scenes." "One spine" refers to the climate commons spine organized along the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park and its north-south connections: it is not a new statutory road or landscape boundary, but a continuous experience that ties together shaded pedestrian paths, rain gardens, displays of low-carbon energy, public services, and open knowledge. "Three cool islands" correspond to the Zhongzhiyuan, AI Origin community, and Dazhongsi three key areas, addressing green AI validation, open research and living, and low-carbon AI-Native commercial issues, respectively. "Two wings" are the knowledge–life collaboration wing facing the university community and the industry–service collaboration wing facing the enterprise station-city. "Six cool passage entrances" are conceptual comfortable nodes spanning major traffic interfaces, entering the park and key areas, with specific locations to be calibrated through road, property, and traffic flow surveys.

The theme of this structure is "Urban Heat Budget." Any AI pilot project proposed to enter the Public Space must first outline its computational energy consumption, water usage, equipment and materials, data collection, and maintenance responsibilities, and then specify how much shading, cooling, rainwater retention, accessibility support, or open knowledge it returns to the public. The heat budget is not a quantified standard already determined in this round, but rather a common ledger for establishing baselines, setting thresholds, and public review and reconciliation. The overall boundary area is read from [metric:site_area_sqm], and the number of three areas is read from [metric:key_area_count]; both are subject to the precision of the Provisional Boundary. The three-layer framework thus forms a closed loop of "regional collaborative problem identification—overall structure allocation of public returns—key areas validating scenarios—reassessment for continued implementation, adjustment, or exit."

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The proposal does not equate "AI Industry Belt" with merely more servers, screens, or exhibition centers, but rather understands it as a resource contract that can be reviewed by the public: when computational and data facilities consume urban resources, they should return to the city through energy-saving verification, heat utilization, open interfaces, talent sharing, and comfortable Public Spaces. The value chain consists of "basic research—green AI full-stack validation—open-source collaboration—product trials—public impact assessment—scaling or exit"; the spatial chain is carried out by different stages involving Zhongzhiyuan, the AI Origin community, Dazhongsi, and the associated universities, communities, parks, and stations. Thus, corporate services are no longer just a recruitment front desk, and public spaces are no longer just a backdrop for industrial image; instead, they become a shared venue for standard validation, social feedback, and risk exposure.

Six international cases provide methods rather than directly replicable templates. The open digital platform of the Singapore Ang Mo Kio Digital District signals that cross-system interconnectivity should be premised on open interfaces, tiered permissions, and energy consumption monitoring; this proposal translates it into a scenario access checklist, without replicating its governance and climate conditions. [source:PDD-JTC] The Fortum and data center waste heat recovery case in Finland indicates that computational heat is only a resource when the supply and return water temperatures, seasonal loads, distance, and responsibility boundaries are all in place; the Beijing solution only lists waste heat cascade utilization as an engineering verification option, without pre-setting feasibility. [source:FORTUM-HEAT] The agile pilot and digital twin experience in Helsinki Kalasatama support "small steps testing—public evaluation—continue or stop," but cannot replace resident consultation and statutory procedures. [source:KALASATAMA]

The Paris Oasis campus transforms the hot environment through shading, permeable surfaces, vegetation, and shared use, inspiring the "Cool Walkway" that begins with comfort perceived by children, seniors, and caregivers, while requiring clear arrangements for winter and summer vacations, as well as nighttime openings and maintenance. [source:PARIS-OASIS] Medellín's Green Corridor demonstrates that continuous vegetation, walking, and biodiversity can collectively reduce heat exposure. Transplanting to Beijing must use locally adapted plants, assess winter sunlight, water conservation, and freeze-thaw maintenance, and should not adopt tropical green density standards. [source:MEDELLIN-GREEN] The controversy surrounding Toronto's Quayside project provides a counterpoint: digital infrastructure should be implemented on streets only after data charters, oversight, appeals, and exit mechanisms are in place, and should not be used to trade away ambiguous authorization for the sake of technological convenience. [source:QUAYSIDE-GOVERNANCE]

Collaboration is facilitated through a matrix of "Resources—Roles—Public Benefits—Verification Methods," all of which are collaborative suggestions rather than binding commitments: universities and research institutions can contribute methods and open courses, with the public benefit being reproducible experiments and talent training; companies can submit green AI products and energy consumption statements, with the public benefit being controlled experimental windows; communities and social organizations can propose real-world issues and participate in evaluations, with the public benefit being non-commercial public services and space improvements; park, park district, and site operators can propose safety and maintenance conditions, with the public benefit being clearer fault closure loops; if government professional departments later participate, they are responsible for rules, approvals, and public accountability, without vouching for a single technology. The three industrial validation scenarios must simultaneously pass through performance, energy consumption, public impact, and exit criteria, and those that do not meet the standards shall not be solidified under the guise of "demonstrations."

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

Organize the overall design with a "Climate Commons Spine" to ensure a continuous north-south connection, integrating pedestrian alleys to stitch together communities, universities, parks, and transit stations. The land structure is not a claim on the current or statutory plan but a conceptual zoning framework for testing proportions, connectivity, and scenario capacity: an ecological and shading backbone [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-GREEN], a slow-moving and necessary transportation interface [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-ROAD], a northern green AI-verified zone [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-NORTH], a midsection open research and living zone [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-MID], a southern low-carbon commercial public interface [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-SOUTH], and a cultural public unit that bears cross-zone shared services [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-CIVIC]. These areas collectively cover the Provisional Boundary, used for [depth:land_use_layout] topological checks, and do not represent that land use approvals have been granted.

The spatial sequence is controlled by the principles of "prioritize public realm, then context; prioritize adaptive reuse, then incremental construction; prioritize reversible facilities, then permanent works." The climate spine prioritizes the placement of shaded segments, drinking water and resting points, permeable surfaces, rain gardens, and low-visibility signage; scene nodes are integrated into the Public Space after its capacity is clearly defined. At this stage, the Building Footprint is merely a conceptual test footprint, with [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001] used to validate the building footprint area and spatial density, but not indicating actual buildings or constituting a demolition and construction plan. The road spine [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-SPINE] only indicates proposed pedestrian and service connections, with specific alignments, sections, and intersection treatments to be determined in subsequent transportation specialties and road right-of-way.

[depth:development_intensity_controls] Adopt the principle of "unknown values left blank." Where formal control plans and current survey data are not available, the Floor Area Ratio, total building scale, Building Height, setback distances, parking provision, and standards for public service facilities will be left as pending confirmation; the proposal will not be based on visual models to generate pseudo-precise metrics. Only the area and proportions resulting from this conceptual geometry can be recalculated, and these should be annotated as temporary attributes in the charts. The update actions are categorized into light intervention in Public Spaces, adaptive reuse of existing buildings, specialized arguments for station-city integration, and permanent constructions requiring legal procedures, thus avoiding the misinterpretation of a single Urban Design plan as an implementation permit.

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

Three "cool islands" adopt the same public interest agreement but do not replicate the same type of spatial product. [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design] requires each to answer on functional combinations, spatial organization, traffic access, building adaptation, Public Space, AI scenarios, operation, and exit strategies, but their dominant values are clearly different. The geometric boundaries of the key areas are all temporary rough boundaries: Zhongzhiyuan corresponds to [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001], AI Origin community corresponds to [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002], and Dazhongsi corresponds to [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003]. During formal deepening, these must be replaced with surveying, property rights, control planning, roads, fire safety, municipal services, heritage, and operational conditions one by one, but at present, no approval conclusions or investment arrangements are advocated.

**Zhongzhiyuan: Green AI Full-Stack Validation Island.** The northern area places models, chips, edge devices, robots, and park systems under a single resource ledger for testing. Spatially, it is recommended to form a low-carbon validation corridor, a reservable test courtyard, an energy consumption and heat release public station, an ecological buffer towards Qinghe, and a public observation interface. The industry team submits performance, energy consumption, water consumption, equipment recycling, and safety descriptions; third parties and the public can view the aggregated results but cannot access trade secrets or personal data. Failed validation equipment will be removed, and the ground will be restored to avoid temporary experiments permanently occupying public land.

**AI Origin Community: Open Research and Climate Living Experiment Cool Island.** The central area is oriented towards university students, faculty, researchers, nearby residents, student families, and those with mobility challenges, integrating open-source releases, climate courses, community co-creation, barrier-free transportation, and quiet study within a walkable range. It is recommended to prioritize the use of adaptable ground floors and courtyards to set up open research lounges, cool learning corridors, and community maintenance stations, without predetermining the retention or demolition of buildings. Research outcomes should be explained in understandable language regarding training data, resource consumption, and social impact; living experiments must provide non-AI equivalent services, and resident participation should not affect the use of Public Spaces.

**Dazhongsi: Low-Carbon AI-Native Commercial Public Interface Cool Island.** The southern area does not create a "sense of the future" through large-scale electronic displays, but rather establishes a credible commercial interface through comfortable station-city transfers, open ground floors, safe nighttime conditions, low-carbon goods and services, and transparent digital rights. It is recommended to set up shaded waiting areas, continuous accessible surfaces, product energy consumption labels, returnable trial cabinets, and human service counters in the direction of the rail transfer. Commercial recommendations must not rely on facial recognition or covert profiling. Business pitches, cultural tours, and consumer experiences must comply with public passage and quiet periods, and must not convert open spaces into exclusive exhibition venues.

"Six Cool Gates" are set in pairs according to their functions: two gates in the north serve the park area—riverside and testing—public transition, two gates in the middle serve the campus area—community and research—lifestyle transition, and two gates in the south serve the transit area—neighborhood and commerce—park transition. The minimum configuration for each gate includes continuous shade, seating, basic nighttime lighting, accessible detours, emergency contact, artificial signage, and shelter from the rain; AI navigation and environmental sensing are only provided as an enhancement layer. Their specific numbers and locations are Conceptual Recommendations, to be refined by professional teams after traffic surveys and public consultations.

## AI Innovation Ecosystem, Personas, and AI+ Scenarios

Scene design is based on six types of personas rather than a list of equipment. The first type is the green AI R&D engineer, who requires reproducibility of experiments, energy consumption comparisons, and clear intellectual property boundaries. The second type is startup teams, who need low-threshold testing, compliance guidance, and alignment with real-world needs. The third type is university students and young researchers, who require open learning opportunities, nighttime safety, and interdisciplinary collaboration. The fourth type is nearby elderly residents, who need clear, stable, and staffed services. The fifth type is wheelchair users, the visually impaired, and caregivers, who require continuous accessibility and predictable routes. The sixth type is park visitors, night shift workers, and short-term visitors, who require shade, drinking water, resting areas, and the basic right to not be tracked. The number of personas is recorded by [metric:persona_count], but no persona should be converted into a personal label or used for commercial targeting.

Twelve "reversible scenario cards" share seven fields: public issue, spatial carrier, minimum data, human responsibility, evaluation metrics, pause threshold, and recovery method. The first three are industry validations: **01 Green AI Full Stack Efficiency Sandbox**, which compares performance, energy consumption, and heat emissions for the same task; **02 Edge Inference Interoperability Testing**, which verifies multi-vendor device interfaces, offline capabilities, and fault degradation; **03 Low-Speed Robot Thermal Coexistence Testing**, which checks for occlusions, noise, right of way, and high-temperature endurance. These must not form exclusive procurement or long-term occupancy before validation is complete, and the validation count is reviewed by [metric:test_scenario_count].

The remaining nine services cater to public life: **04 Shadow-Priority Accessible Navigation**, which only uses road and environmental information without saving personal trajectories; **05 Heat Warning and Cool Spot Guidance**, providing paper maps and manual announcements; **06 Rain Garden Maintenance Assistant**, which assists in generating inspection lists, confirmed by horticultural staff; **07 Tree and Water Usage Public Ledger**, which publishes aggregated maintenance information; **08 Accessible Companion Assistant**, which is activated by the user and can be deleted at any time; **09 Jing-Zhang Cultural Tour Guide**, which clearly identifies historical sources and uncertainties; **10 Business Green Compliance and Public Policy Assistant**, which serves only as a material navigation tool and does not replace legal or approval opinions; **11 Open Learning Studio**, which publishes courses, codes, and replication experiments; **12 Activity Resource Budget and Safety Review**, which reviews electricity, waste, noise, evacuation, and heat risks before events. The total number of scenarios is recorded by [metric:scenario_count].

Three recognizable but not pursuit-of-gigantism public landmarks carry a shared narrative. The first, "Heat Budget Station," uses physical scales and local offline screens to publicly display energy, cooling, and maintenance status; the second, "Open Source Shade Walkway," combines removable shading elements, rain gardens, and open courses; the third, "Jing-Zhang Climate Milestone Wall," juxtaposes railway history, urban climate, and AI public contributions, with content updated after source verification. The number of landmarks is reviewed by [metric:landmark_count]. All scenarios implement data minimization, clear purposes, Human Review, accessible appeal mechanisms, non-AI equivalent services, regular re-evaluation, and one-click pause and exit; conclusions involving safety, healthcare, law, planning approval, or resource allocation must be made by responsible personnel.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

Land-use representation adopts comparable classification codes in accordance with [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE], but the existing layers are conceptually complete zones, not a statutory Land-Use Plan. Green commons, road connectivity, and the three composite functional categories and cultural public spaces are verified by [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-GREEN], [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-ROAD], [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-NORTH], [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-MID], [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-SOUTH], and [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-CIVIC]. The layers must seamlessly cover the temporary overall boundary without overlapping and require a topological check and area reconciliation upon the substitution of the formal boundary.

The architectural strategy follows the "Preserve what can be preserved first, renovate what can be renovated first, test demolition and assembly first, and then decide on permanent construction." Due to the lack of detailed records of the age, structural safety, ownership, rental, fire safety, cultural heritage protection, and usage efficiency of each building, this proposal does not determine any building as either mandatory for demolition or retention. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish] The subsequent investigation will be divided into five steps: archival and ownership verification, structural and fire safety inspection, comparison of carbon emissions and adaptation costs, public interest evaluation, and stakeholder negotiation. Only after these five steps are completed and enter the legal process can a decision on the Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy be formed. Currently, [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001] and the footprints on the same level are only used for testing the scale of the neighborhood, the ground floor interface, and the Building Footprint indicators.

[depth:height_massing_character] Climate and Public Space characteristics should replace any formal styling: control the continuous and accessible first-floor public interface along the climate spine; evaluate solar and wind conditions and tree canopies near the park; and study mixed-use opportunities around the site after ensuring safety and capacity. Both the Building Footprint area [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm] and the concept building density [metric:building_density] can be recalculated from a test footprint, but they do not describe the current state and do not directly provide development rights. The Floor Area Ratio [metric:floor_area_ratio] and the total floor area [metric:total_floor_area_sqm] remain unknown and will be calculated after formal zoning, building height, and survey data are complete; all height, setback, floor area ratio, and total quantity recommendations also require professional and specialized argumentation under [depth:development_intensity_controls]. (Building Coverage Ratio)

The architectural adaptation priorities vary for the three areas: Zhongzhiyuan prioritizes checking the variability of experimental spaces, equipment cooling, loading and unloading, and public observation safety; AI Origin community prioritizes checking the openness of the existing ground floor, quiet study areas, aging-friendly design, and barrier-free access; Dazhongsi prioritizes checking the continuity of the station-city ground floor, nighttime facades, and commercial logistics. The roofs are not pre-set for full greenery or the deployment of computing equipment, but are selected for each building based on structural loads, fire safety, maintenance, glare, noise, and carbon emissions over the entire lifecycle. Any claims of "zero carbon," "energy savings," or investment returns must be based on measured baselines and third-party verification, and no promises are made in this round.

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The top priority for the transportation strategy is the continuous passage of people during extreme weather and daily commutes. The concept main spine [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-SPINE] connects the Three Cool Islands and the Public Space, but it only expresses the intent for pedestrian and service use, without replacing the road right-of-way and engineering alignment. [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking] will break down subsequent work into six overlapping maps: rail connections, pedestrian continuity, bicycle parking, bus connections, motor vehicle and loading/unloading, and low-speed robots. The six Cool Travel Gates will first assess cross-street distances, wait times, slopes, tactile paths, winter icing, summer sun exposure, lighting, and emergency access, before deciding whether to integrate signal optimization or AI navigation.

Road area [metric:road_area_sqm] and ratio [metric:road_ratio] are recalculated for this round of conceptual land use zoning and should not be interpreted as the current road ratio or the controlled plan road ratio. Low-speed robots must be limited in time, speed, and range, yielding to pedestrians, wheelchairs, and guide dogs; they must safely stop if the system is out of contact, and operations should be suspended if complaints are concentrated or conflict thresholds are exceeded. Shadow priority navigation uses open roadways and aggregated environmental data without tracking individual paths; users without smartphones can still complete their journeys using continuous signage, tactile paving, paper maps, and human inquiries. Specific transfer renovations around rail stations require review by operating entities, traffic management, fire services, and municipal units, and this proposal does not claim to have reached coordination.

At the municipal level, the sequence is established as [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]. First, reduce demand by shading, natural ventilation, water conservation, and load management. Then, research the coupling of photovoltaic, storage, waste heat, and recycled water systems. Finally, configure sensing and algorithms. The utilization of computational waste heat must be argued through temperature grades, supply and demand timing, distance, pipeline networks, equipment reliability, and lifecycle economics. If not validated, it should be withdrawn. Environmental sensing should prioritize the collection of non-identifiable data such as temperature, humidity, soil moisture, and equipment status, with clear retention periods. The current [data:geometry/constraints.geojson] is a placeholder for data gaps, containing no actual features and not indicating any existing statutory constraints; official control lines, municipal, and protection data must replace and re-run spatial verification upon availability. Public services such as drinking water, restrooms, seating, emergency care, charging, and shelter from rain should be marked with physical identifiers and subject to manual inspection, ensuring they do not disappear due to digital system failures.

![Pedestrian and Rail Access with Blue-Green Urban Municipal Composite Network](assets/figures/mobility-bluegreen.png)

The implementation of transportation and utilities must preserve the engineering prerequisites: road right-of-way, underground utilities, drainage and flood prevention, fire rescue, track protection, tree relocation, construction rerouting, and accessibility review. Each pilot should establish a "space lead—equipment lead—data lead—public contact" quadruple role; faults should be identified with a phone number and offline entry provided at the site, rather than solely relying on QR code scanning. This ensures that so-called smart facilities remain part of a maintainable urban service, rather than isolated endpoints without accountability.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The goal of the blue-green system is not to pursue a high green coverage image, but to reduce heat exposure for the most vulnerable populations while improving flood management, ecology, and pedestrian experiences. The conceptual green commons [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-COMMONS] connects the Three Cool Islands, Six Cool Gates, and along-line open spaces; the public activity commons [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-COMMONS] hosts rest, learning, display, community services, and temporary experiments. Both are cross-referenced with the land green skeleton, but due to the lack of current vegetation, soil, underground space, and hydrological data, they only express system relationships. [depth:blue_green_public_space] requires subsequent evaluation through canopy projection, continuous shadow duration, seat accessibility, permeable performance, flood risk, habitat quality, and annual maintenance costs, rather than just counting flat color blocks.

The recalculated green area [metric:green_area_sqm], green ratio [metric:green_ratio], Public Space area [metric:public_space_area_sqm], and public space ratio [metric:public_space_ratio] are all conceptual geometric indicators that must be updated after formal surveying. Tree species selection is based on Beijing's suitability, drought resistance, cold resistance, and low allergenicity, while also considering winter sunlight. Rain gardens require soil permeability and pipeline surveys; not all low points should be treated as sponge facilities. The Paris campus cooling and Medellín green corridor provide inspiration for "perceptible comfort and continuous networks," but climate, maintenance, and governance must be localized. [source:PARIS-OASIS] [source:MEDELLIN-GREEN]

Public Space establishes three temporal sequences: during daily hours, the priority is for passage, rest, and neighborhood use; during test hours, the principle is to have minimal barriers, transparent information, and the ability to stop at any time; during event hours, capacity, noise, electricity, waste, evacuation, and heat thresholds are set. The three landmarks, Open Shade Canopy, Thermal Budget Station, and Jing-Zhang Climate Milestone Wall, use repairable components, local fonts, and clear graphics and text, without relying on highlighted screens to create a sense of technology. Urban Character is formed by the depth of railway heritage, the open spirit of Haidian's innovative culture, and the simple materials that adapt to the climate; any actions involving the physical body of cultural relics, their protection zones, and scenic view corridors require official departmental documentation and specialized assessment, not AI speculation.

Maintenance is integral to the design, not an afterthought. Tree plantings, gardens, paving, shading structures, drinking fountains, and sensor equipment are individually specified for inspection frequency, seasonal tasks, failure degradation, and removal methods; in the event of a data system outage, basic shading, seating, wayfinding, and drainage remain functional. It is recommended that annual canopy growth, survival rates, failure response times, accessibility complaints, and public satisfaction be used as public operational metrics, but only after establishing a baseline and identifying responsible parties. This round does not fabricate maintenance funding or commit to specific institutions; rather, it proposes a template for roles that can be negotiated by management, operators, and the community.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Update the project list to prioritize "public return first, heavy assets last." [depth:renewal_project_list] The suggested projects are categorized into six packages: A Climate Commons Spine and Six Cool Gates; B Zhongzhiyuan Green AI Validation Field; C AI Origin Open Research and Living Room; D Dazhongsi Low-Carbon Commercial Public Interface; E Three Public Landmarks and Open Knowledge Initiative; F Data Governance, Evaluation, and Maintenance Capabilities. Each package must include location, target population, spatial conditions, minimum public return, data boundaries, operational responsibilities, retraction costs, and professional approvals. The "smartification" factor should not be a default priority reason.

[depth:phasing_implementation] Adopt a three-phase reversible implementation. The first phase, "Reversible Pilot," corresponds to [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-1], establishing a baseline with temporary shading, signage, open courses, and validation of three sectors, with the area recalculated as [metric:phase_1_area_sqm]; the second phase, "Network Weaving," corresponds to [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-2], enhancing pedestrian and cycling infrastructure, blue-green spaces, and adaptive reuse of existing ground floors after evaluation and conditions are met, with the area recalculated as [metric:phase_2_area_sqm]; the third phase, "Regime Solidification," corresponds to [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-3], incorporating proven commons agreements, maintenance standards, and data rules into subsequent professional plans, with the area recalculated as [metric:phase_3_area_sqm]. These three phased areas are conceptual implementation zones and do not equate to annual construction plans, fiscal arrangements, or land supply timelines.

Each phase will have four gates: **Admission Gate** to verify origin, spatial safety, data minimization, and non-AI equivalence; **Operation Gate** to monitor comfort, energy consumption, faults, complaints, and Public Space occupancy; **Review Gate** to be judged by professionals, users, and independent observers; **Exit Gate** to specify equipment removal, data deletion, site restoration, and experience disclosure. Thresholds are not automatically expanded; they still require corresponding approval.
