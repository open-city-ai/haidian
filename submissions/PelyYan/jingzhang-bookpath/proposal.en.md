## 1. Overall Concept

### 1.1 Naming and Core Narrative

**Jingzhang Bookpath (京张书径)** — A city-scale knowledge corridor that uses independent bookstores as cultural anchors and AI technology as its neural network.

The century-old Jing-Zhang Railway corridor (Wudaokou → Zhichun Road → Dazhongsi) happens to be China's highest-density knowledge belt — surrounded by Tsinghua, Peking University, CAS, and Zhongguancun. The independent bookstores that grew along this corridor over 30 years (All Sages Bookstore, Douban Bookstore, Wind Through Pines, Zhongshuge...) are the **living proof** of that knowledge density.

**AI innovation and humanistic inquiry are not in conflict — they are symbiotic.**

This is not a slogan. The world's strongest AI labs (DeepMind, OpenAI, Anthropic) were all born at the intersection of university towns and humanistic neighborhoods, not in industrial parks. The reason is simple: AI's deepest problems — alignment, values, creativity, meaning — are fundamentally humanistic problems. An "AI innovation belt" without bookstores is like a tree without roots.

**When other proposals define this as a pure "AI industry belt," we see a deeper structure:**
- DeepMind's papers cite philosopher Wittgenstein
- GPT-4's alignment training requires value judgments from humanities scholars
- Zhongguancun's first generation of entrepreneurs (Wang Xuan, Liu Chuanzhi) found inspiration in bookstores along this very corridor
- Tsinghua's Yao Class students browse All Sages Bookstore in their free time — cross-disciplinary collision needs physical space

**The real bottleneck for AI innovation is not compute, but imagination — and imagination grows in humanistic soil.**

### 1.2 Urgency: Bookstores Are Disappearing

In September 2025, **Douban Bookstore** — which stood on Chengfu Road for 19 years — permanently closed. Before it: Wind Through Pines (2014), Guolinfeng, Third Pole, and Wild Grass Bookshop all vanished or relocated.

Independent bookstores along this corridor are disappearing at a rate of one every 2-3 years. If the Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt does not build survival structures for humanistic spaces into its design, in 10 years only chain stores and co-working spaces will remain — and the belt will lose the one thing that makes it different from any tech park in the world: **a living humanistic tradition**.

This proposal is not nostalgia. It is a concrete spatial strategy: **ensuring AI startups and humanistic bookstores coexist on the same corridor, rather than the former displacing the latter.**

### 1.3 Design Philosophy: Three-Layer Overlay

```
Layer 3: AI Neural Layer      — Agent networks, data flows, compute nodes
Layer 2: Knowledge Exchange   — Bookstores, academic spaces, open-source communities
Layer 1: Railway Heritage     — Century-old Jing-Zhang physical remains, pedestrian systems
```

The three layers are not separate: bookstores (L2) are the **interface layer** connecting railway heritage (L1) with AI innovation (L3).

## 3. Symbiosis Mechanism Design

### How AI Startups Can Coexist Without Displacing Bookstores

**Root cause diagnosis:** Bookstore death is not caused by "people not reading" but by:
1. Rent pressure: tech companies tolerate 5-10× the per-sqm cost bookstores can bear
2. Traffic shift: online purchase drains transactional footfall, leaving only experiential
3. No policy tools: no "cultural anchor protection" in urban renewal frameworks

**Three mechanisms:**

**A. Cultural Anchor Rent Lock** — Embed "cultural use retention clauses" in land-transfer conditions. 30% of ground-floor area along bookstore trail restricted to cultural use, rent locked at cultural-rate for 10 years.

**B. AI Company × Bookstore Shared Lease** — For every 1000sqm office space leased, AI companies fund 50sqm bookstore space. Forms: rent subsidy / lobby bookstore / joint events.

**C. Bookstores as AI Testbeds** — Independent bookstores become real-world testing environments for AI scenarios. AI companies gain authentic user feedback; bookstores gain technology and revenue.

**Why this works better than pure subsidy:** Not charity but **structural embedding** — bookstores are functional nodes in the innovation ecosystem, not protected weaklings. AI needs humanistic feedback (alignment, cross-disciplinary thinking); bookstores provide exactly that.
