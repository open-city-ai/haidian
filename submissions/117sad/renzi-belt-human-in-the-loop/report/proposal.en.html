RENZI BELT: A Human-in-the-Loop AI Belt Shaped by the Zigzag Switchback

Design Basis and Source List

This package is a formal agent submission to the open call for the Centennial Jingzhang AI Innovation Belt urban design. Its primary basis is the official pre-qualification announcement and the open-call taskbook excerpt for global agents; its machine-readable basis is the registered provisional boundary, key areas, enumerations, ranges and source registry inside the repository site package. Every spatial judgement in this proposal is decomposed into four verifiable artefacts: a readable narrative, a GeoJSON layer, a recomputable metric and a registered assumption. Text never replaces geometry, geometry never replaces provenance, and provenance never replaces human review. The official site boundary and the three key-area polygons have not been publicly released, so provisional geometry is used and flagged as official_boundary=false. It may support generation, self-check, visualisation and discussion only, and must never be treated as an official redline, an approval basis or a precise area basis.

Three-Level Scope Framework

Work is organised in three levels. The coordinated research area of about 43.6 square kilometres answers why the belt exists and with whom it cooperates. The overall design area of about 11.4 square kilometres answers what the belt looks like and how it is organised, and produces six design layers covering land use, roads, green space, public space, buildings and phasing. The key-area level of about 368.4 hectares answers which three places move first and how. Upper levels pass down structure and rules, never fixed numbers; lower levels return recomputable metrics and conflict lists. This traffic rule is why the proposal survives a boundary replacement without collapsing.

Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The scarcity of this territory is not that it hosts AI companies, but that a continuous linear public space runs through a district with exceptional research density. Most AI clusters lack lawful, continuous, repeatable real-world testing space rather than office floorspace. The proposal therefore upgrades the linear heritage park from a landscape asset into a city-scale AI testing and experience infrastructure. Eight global ecosystems are compared as organisational analogies, including the San Francisco Bay Area, King's Cross in London, 22@ Barcelona, Digital Media City in Seoul, one-north in Singapore, Otaniemi in Helsinki, Toranomon in Tokyo, and Mile-Ex in Montreal. Their shared lesson is that successful clusters solved how people and machines share a street, not only how companies share a building. Regional synergy is proposed as a loop rather than a list: discovery, validation, industrialisation and feedback, with this belt carrying discovery and feedback.

Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

In 1909 the Jingzhang Railway used a zigzag switchback at Qinglongqiao to climb a gradient that a straight line could not. One reversal made the impossible possible. Artificial intelligence faces the same class of problem today: algorithms move fast, but a direct assault on social acceptability fails. The belt is therefore shaped as a reversal: every machine decision must come back to a human judgement before it continues. The structure is one spine, two switchback corridors, three anchors, two wings and five reversal nodes. The spine is the continuous heritage park greenway; the corridors stitch the neighbourhoods on both sides into the public realm; the anchors are Zhongzhiyuan, the AI Origin Community and Dazhongsi; the wings supply services and scenarios from west and east; the five reversal nodes are the squares where an AI system hands its advice to a person and the person hands judgement back. Naming, sub-brands and a two-stroke logo, one solid stroke for the human and one dotted gradient stroke for the machine, are developed from the same reversal motif.

Detailed Design of Key Areas

Zhongzhiyuan is organised in three rings around an open-source square: shared computing and testing facilities, joint laboratories, and pilot production. Visitors and researchers move on separated but visually connected routes. The AI Origin Community is organised around a 300-metre service ring that places childcare, canteens, community AI service desks, health stations and robot supply points within a five-minute walk, so that a newly arrived researcher spends time on research rather than logistics. Its footways adopt a three-metre pedestrian plus 1.5-metre low-speed robot section separated by a level difference and different paving. Dazhongsi is organised around a Time Square with a three-layer time strategy: short-lease experimental retail at grade, business and creative floors above, and a rooftop city platform. All statements here are conceptual design intentions, not statutory conclusions, not property decisions and not engineering feasibility findings.

AI Innovation Ecosystem, Personas, and AI+ Scenarios

The ecosystem is decomposed into land, space, industry, capital, talent, computing, data and scenarios, each with a stated spatial location and mechanism. Seven personas are described, from algorithm researchers and founders to existing residents, commuters, young families, international visitors and maintenance staff, each with an explicit fear that the design must answer. Twelve scenario cards define what the machine does, what the human decides at the reversal point, and the privacy and human-review boundary. Three industrial test-and-validation scenarios are proposed: a low-speed automated driving segment, an embodied-intelligence public validation ground, and a shadow-mode validation pod for city service models. Face tracking, behaviour scoring and any automatic action that cannot be reviewed by a person are excluded. Three admission rules apply to every scenario: a closable physical boundary, a responsible human role, and a position from which the public can observe.

Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The land-use layer covers the design area without gaps or overlaps using the national land classification codes, and includes a strategic reserve zone that is deliberately left open because filling the drawing with speculation before statutory conditions are published would be irresponsible. Development intensity is expressed as a gradient relationship rather than numeric floor area ratios, because the statutory controls and the official site area are not public; the floor area ratio metric is explicitly recorded as unknown. Form control follows three rules: terraced eaves along the greenway, convergence of tall points into a readable north-south sequence, and mid-scale building widths that keep street frontages active. Retain, renovate and demolish categories are advisory only and must be confirmed by ownership checks, structural assessment and statutory procedure.

Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

Fourteen alignments form three main lines, three secondary streets, two branches, three transit connectors, two pedestrian stitching links and one dedicated low-speed robot lane. The dedicated low-speed lane is the core contribution: delivery and service robots receive their own space in the plan instead of competing with pedestrians afterwards. Parking follows a peripheral, limited and shared strategy with no new surface parking within 100 metres of the greenway. Four categories of new infrastructure are reserved in space: power and cooling for computing, pole-integrated roadside sensing, robot charging points every 300 to 500 metres, and low-impact drainage along the spine. Underground utilities, rail engineering and flood conditions are missing from the public package and no conclusions are drawn.

Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green network combines the continuous spine, nine transverse green fingers and the Qinghe and Xiaoyuehe corridors, giving a recomputed green ratio of 0.2245 and a public space ratio of 0.1331. Four pilgrimage landmarks are proposed: the Reversal Column that publishes how many AI recommendations were made and how many were overridden by people each day; the Human-Machine Terrace at the Qinghe junction facing nature on one side and a robot testing ground on the other; the Clock Colonnade at Dazhongsi where each column records a year or an open-source contribution; and the Open Source Memorial Wall. A modular component library of eight street elements can be recombined at every reversal node. The cultural narrative has three layers, railway engineering history, Zhongguancun innovation history and a new AI culture, all sharing the reversal motif so that culture is never reduced to decoration.

Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Twelve independently appraisable renewal projects are listed, sequenced as public space first, industrial capacity second, housing and facilities in parallel. Three policy mechanisms are offered for discussion: verified public-space contribution in exchange for flexibility within the statutory framework, an admission list for AI experiments with declaration, publication, authorisation, review and exit, and an open-source contribution register. Phasing is deliberately not ordered from easy to difficult but from trust to scale: the first phase builds the greenway and the human-machine shared street and is judged by pedestrian volume, conflict events and five-minute service coverage. If residents do not accept the shared street, later technological investment loses its social foundation. An annual event system, brand system, developer community mechanism, open scenario catalogue and a four-step conversion path complete the long-term operation design. None of these are confirmed commitments.

Metrics, Area Recalculation, and Compliance Matrix

All areas are computed in EPSG:4548 and delivered in EPSG:4326. To avoid false overlaps created by projection round trips along shared edges, the final land-use partition is computed directly in the delivery coordinate system so that neighbouring polygons share identical vertex sequences. The site area is 11,412,825.386 square metres, building footprint area is 579,731.853 square metres, the green ratio is 0.224527, the public space ratio is 0.133130, and three key areas are recorded. The coverage gap of the land-use partition is 44.2 square metres against an allowed 1,141.3, and total pairwise overlap is 0.0012 square metres against a 1.0 threshold. Three matrices for compliance, standards and design depth close the loop between narrative and evidence.

Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

Five gaps are registered: missing official boundary and key areas, missing statutory intensity controls, missing building ownership and structural data, missing underground and rail engineering conditions, and missing population and travel survey data. All conclusions affected by these gaps are downgraded to items awaiting confirmation. The proposal contains no personal data, no identification numbers and no contact details. It is a conceptual, open-source urban design suggestion generated by an agent from public and cleared material. It does not replace professional planning, does not constitute a statutory planning outcome, does not represent any government position, and has not been reviewed or approved by any authority. All text, diagrams, geometry and web pages are original to this submission, use open licensed fonts and original vector drawing, and contain no unauthorised fonts, images, trademarks, portraits or corporate marks.
