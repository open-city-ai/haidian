# Jing-Zhang Co-Metabolism: A Verifiable, Roamable, and Co-Governable AI Urban Coexistence Belt

"CoPulse Jingzhang" does not cover the city with technology, but rather allows history, industry, everyday life, and public judgment to share a readable urban pulse. The proposal is structured around "one spine and two rings, three cores and six gates": a north-south cultural and public spine that carries the narrative of the site and supports slow-paced living, two service rings that connect innovative production with community life, with Zhongzhiyuan, Beijing AI Origin Community, and Dazhongsi forming three complementary innovation cores. Six low-impact public rooms integrate cross-line stitching, service, and display into everyday life. All spaces are conceptual proposals; provisional boundaries, recalculable metrics, and unknown control items run through the text, GeoJSON, PNG, PDF, and offline HTML. (Provisional Boundary) (Jing-Zhang)

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The three levels of scope are organized according to the "strategic coordination—overall structure—district validation" approach. Approximately 43.6 square kilometers of the Coordinated Research Area are used to identify the relationships between universities, research institutions, enterprises, open-source communities, talent living, and public cultural activities; this boundary is not precisely fabricated in this package. Approximately 11.4 square kilometers of the Overall Design Area carry the land use, updates, blue-green spaces, transportation, and facility structures. The key area of about 368.4 hectares is recalculated as approximately 369.29 hectares in the three temporary polygons of this announcement, with the discrepancy attributed to rough boundaries, which will be re-run once the official polygons are in place. [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [metric:key_area_count]

The spatial structure is defined as "one ridge, two rings, three cores, and six gates." "One ridge" is the Jing-Zhang Cultural Public Space Ridge, which integrates green space, public space, and pedestrian experience. "Two rings" are the Innovation Service Ring and Community Life Ring, not expressing road boundary lines. "Three cores" respectively carry full-stack validation, open-source transformation, and urban-type intelligent economy. "Six gates" are public rooms set along the horizontal seam positions, including Qinghe Co-governance Platform, Public Wisdom Standard Garden, Co-Flow Urban Living Room, Origin Point Open Source Courtyard, Centennial Scale Field, and Dazhongsi Wisdom Gate. This structure is expressed by three readable layers: road centerlines, green space, and public space. ## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The overall design adopts a framework of "first public backbone, then parcel calibration; first adaptive update, then incremental construction; first regular sandbox, then scenario diffusion." The six conceptual land uses are divided from the same Provisional Boundary topology to achieve full coverage, shared edges, and no overlap. The central cultural public spine provides a continuous base, with research and development transformation, innovative services, talent living, community services, and cultural display organized along both sides. This layout is used to test functional relationships without changing land use or replacing the control plan. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001] [depth:land_use_layout] [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE]

Updates are handled in four categories: A category "Value Retention" preserves buildings with historical, public, or ongoing use value; B category "Adaptive Reuse" enhances the reuse of existing stock through ground-level openness, energy-efficient facades, shared rooftops, and accessibility improvements; C category "Light Insertions" complements public functions with modular service pods, canopies, exhibition kiosks, and digital interfaces; D category "Pending Addition" only enters the architectural scheme after a building-by-building survey, ownership negotiations, control plan, and municipal review. The current 14 conceptual Building Footprints are merely spatial test-fits and do not represent existing buildings or serve as demolition judgments. [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001] [depth:retain_renovate_demolish]

The control depth is defined through "known—awaiting calibration—prohibited derivation" layers: boundary, concept base, green spaces, Public Space, and concept network can be recalculated from the scheme geometry; formal land use nature, Floor Area Ratio, Building Coverage Ratio, total building scale, height, setback, and road area are all kept unknown; when official conditions are lacking, it is not permissible to reverse-engineer control indicators from renderings. [standard:MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING] [depth:development_intensity_controls] The form principle only stipulates public character: ground-level access at the ridge edge, segmented massing at key nodes, prioritized green and shared spaces on rooftops, and preserved historical line of sight; specific height must be verified through the control plan, cultural heritage, landscape, and aviation conditions. [depth:height_massing_character]

### Zhongzhiyuan: Full Stack Validation Garden

Located as the "rule-led full-stack independent innovation acceleration zone." Spatially, it forms a T-shaped open framework with the Qinghe Co-governance Platform—Standard Garden—Cultural Public Spine, with industrial courtyards sutured in between; buildings prioritize retaining usable baselines, with the first floor inserting shared evaluation, standard collaboration, and visitor displays, and any additional volume only serving as pending evidence. Traffic is managed with dual-ring public entrances, testing logistics and daily slow travel in separate times and lines; Public Spaces will host a low-carbon garden, an open-source standard wall, and booking-based small-scale testing. AI scenarios include model safety sandbox, low-carbon computing collaboration, and robot low-speed validation, all requiring data minimization, professional review, and immediate stop capability. The initial focus is on modular standard kiosks and the Qinghe pedestrian interface; the primary risks are the Official Boundary, river conditions, existing buildings, energy consumption, and safety responsibilities are not clearly defined, and no additional scenarios will be expanded until these are closed. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001]

### Beijing AI Origin Community: Open Source Transformation Neighborhood

Located as the "Public Space within the Five-Minute Collaboration Circle for Universities, Focusing on Open Source Technology Transfer and Talent Living Community." The spatial structure consists of the Original Point Open Source Courtyard, the Near-School Technology Transfer Street, and the Talent Service Ring, which are connected at the first level with fine-grained incubation, legal services, exhibitions, residences, and daily consumption. Building updates follow small units, combinable partitions, reversible dividers, and zoned night-time disturbance areas. Prioritizing pedestrian and bicycle access, the design stitches the campus, park, and cultural spine together, with motor vehicles and loading/unloading activities organized in the outer ring. Public spaces include release steps, quiet collaboration courtyards, and all-weather accessible paths. AI scenarios include an open-source release assistant, enterprise service Copilot, health service navigation, and multilingual guides, with all significant recommendations confirmed by authorized personnel. The key is to first open a prototype first-level space and a public issue platform; risks include campus boundaries, intellectual property rights, rental costs, noise, and personal data boundaries, which must be calibrated by the university, property owner, community, and professional teams. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002]

### Dazhongsi: Intelligent Native City Living Room

Positioned as an "intelligent track portal, smart terminal, and content economy living room." Spatially, the Dazhongsi Smart Gateway connects the four quadrants pedestrian network, linking the station, commercial first floor, planned green spaces, and cultural spine. The architectural strategy prioritizes opening up the street-facing facades, reusing available spaces for demonstrations and experiences, and avoiding turning the portal into a closed exhibition space. Traffic organization emphasizes pedestrian crossings, barrier-free transfers, bicycle parking, and contingency plans for event day crowds; all bridges, tunnels, station entrances, and sections are currently issues awaiting specialized deepening. AI scenarios include multilingual cultural tours, interoperability demonstrations for smart terminals, and auxiliary safety reviews for events; crowd evacuation, medical, and traffic judgments are always handled by professionals. The key is lightweight signage and shared first-floor living rooms; the main risks are unverified station interfaces, road safety, municipal utility lines, cultural heritage connections, and commercial operation rights. ## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The traffic structure is not an additional road scheme but a set of "relationship lines" that require field verification: a north-south primary spine for slow travel, two longitudinal service loops, six transverse connectors and feeder routes within the key area, with a conceptual central line totaling 34,254.62 meters in length. [metric:mobility_network_length_m] [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001] The primary spine prioritizes pedestrian, cycling, accessibility, and park experiences; the dual loops handle arrival, service, and emergency needs; and the transverse connectors focus on cross-line, cross-road, station, and community entrance connections. Road area and road ratio are kept at unknown, as there are no road right-of-way or cross-sections; bridge and tunnel structures, station entrances, signaling, parking, and fire safety cannot be determined from the central line. [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]

Track integration follows a four-layer inspection: "station information—exit dispersal—five-minute walk—district destination." Dazhongsi focuses on the accessibility and activity flow in the quadrants, while the original community studies the daily connections between universities and tracks/bus services. Zhongzhiyuan examines external arrivals and the separation of test logistics. Parking strategies first manage demand and promote sharing, then consider incremental increases. Non-motorized vehicle facilities should be near the entrance but not obstruct blind paths, fire access, or tree pits. Any specific entrance connections, road renovations, and cross-line facilities require traffic modeling, surveying, property rights, and safety arguments. [standard:MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING]

Municipal infrastructure and New Infrastructure adopt "plug-and-play service pods": reserve end-side computational power, communication, energy monitoring, rain and flood management, and maintenance interfaces in public rooms. Equipment can be replaced, operate offline, and faults can be manually taken over. Distributed energy, computational power, and sensing only propose interface principles without specifying capacity; formal deepening requires power supply, communication, water supply and drainage, flood prevention, gas, heating, sanitation, fire safety, and underground pipeline data. Public services prioritize walkability and multi-channel access, combining talent, health, education, legal, and intellectual property entry points at the original community center, combining evaluation, standards, and enterprise services at Zhongzhiyuan, and combining cultural, commercial, international reception, and emergency services at Dazhongsi. ## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green structure consolidates three segments of conceptual green spaces into a traversable public cultural spine, covering an area of 2,124,585.55 square meters, which constitutes 18.6158% of the temporary scope; the deduplicated area of six public rooms and connecting spaces is 273,522.84 square meters, accounting for 2.3966%. [metric:green_space_area_sqm] [metric:green_ratio] [metric:public_space_area_sqm] [metric:public_space_ratio] These proportions are derived from the design layer rather than a current green space inventory; the classification of green spaces, flood protection, trees, and park boundaries still require specialized data. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-001] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001] [depth:blue_green_public_space]

Public Space is organized around three rhythms: the daily layer provides continuous walking, cycling, shading, resting, exercising, and accessibility; the collaborative layer offers small discussions, presentations, testing, and resident deliberations; the event layer hosts performances, celebrations, and international exchanges within controlled time periods. Each of the six public rooms is equipped with non-AI signage, human service, and visible scenario governance doors, avoiding the transformation of the park into a technology showcase. Sponge systems, vegetation, lighting, noise, and nighttime activities are designed with low disturbance, maintainability, and phased implementation principles, with specific plant species, retention, and illumination levels to be refined by the landscape, municipal, and ecological teams. [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES]

Four conceptual landmarks form an "holy pilgrimage not closed off" urban memory system: Dazhongsi Zhuihui Gate is the track portal and multilingual city living room; the Centennial Scale Field uses an updatable timeline to connect the history of the Jing-Zhang project and contemporary contributions; the Origin Open Courtyard allows the traceability of contributions to code, papers, standards, and public welfare applications; the Collective Wisdom Standard Garden translates evaluations, safety, and standards into public-friendly exhibitions. Honor displays adopt a five-field format of "contribution type—Evidence Chain—maintainer—update time—appeal entry," not ranking by capital scale. Component libraries include pulse signposts, open bracket seats, modular standard pavilions, low-level barrier-free screens, contribution plaques, and data status lights, all of which are recommended to be used after small-scale prototypes are validated.

Cultural narrative unfolds in three acts: the first act, "Century-Long Engineering Courage," addresses self-built infrastructure, surveying, and public facilities; the second act, "ZPark Open Innovation," covers education, research, markets, and open-source collaboration; the third act, "AI Co-Governance Civilization," discusses how humans retain the right to choose, question, amend, and halt technology. The three acts are connected through materials, oral histories, interactions, and public issues, without reducing history to a brand background or future to a single path. The architectural form emphasizes segmented volumes, open ground floors, restrained signage, and maintainable details; the approved Building Height remains unknown, with all line-of-sight, skyline, and heritage relationships pending formal condition verification. ## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

The primary risk is boundary trust: both SITE and KEY_AREA are provisional. The second risk is a control gap: the Floor Area Ratio (FAR), density, scale, height, and road setbacks are unknown. The third risk is a current condition gap: buildings, ownership, tenants, cultural heritage, trees, pipelines, and facility capacity have not been surveyed. The fourth risk is an implementation gap: the principal, funding, approvals, maintenance, and responsibility allocation are undetermined. The fifth risk is externalities from AI: biases, erroneous recommendations, data misuse, accessibility exclusion, automation dependency, and vendor lock-in. Each category of risk is addressed with the process: "data in place—professional review—public/user engagement—small-scale pilot—halt gate."
