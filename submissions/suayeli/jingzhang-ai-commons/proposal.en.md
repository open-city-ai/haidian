# Jing-Zhang Zhi Mai · Co-Existence Test Site: An Open Intelligent City on Centennial Infrastructure

## Overview of the Proposal: Make AI an Optionable, Exitable, and Auditable Component of Urban Public Capability

"Jing-Zhang Smart Pulse · Co-Existence Experimental Field" does not aim to build a closed science park, but to translate a century-old railway infrastructure into an open urban interface: the Heritage Axis is responsible for memory and publicness, while the Zhongzhiyuan, Beijing AI Origin Community, and Dazhongsi three cores respectively undertake validation, transformation, and dissemination. The Slow Travel Blue-Green Ring returns innovative resources to everyday life. The spatial strategy is summarized as "one axis with three cores, two wings in coordination, and ten scenic spots forming a network," and is subject to three constraints: any AI service must retain a non-digital alternative path; any spatial judgment must be able to revert to geometry and origin; and any operational commitment must specify the responsible party and exit mechanism. [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK] [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

Original design actions include: three "Intelligent Corridors" connecting the public core to three key areas, forming a conceptual network that integrates pedestrian, cycling, low-speed transit, and public services; an "Algorithmic Showcase" that makes transparent the uses, data, responsibilities, and appeal mechanisms of the Urban Agent; and a "Centuries—Questions" heritage narrative that juxtaposes the history of the railway project with AI ethical issues. The newly added corridors are recorded in [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-JZ-02-1], and all area-sensitive conclusions remain subject to the Provisional Boundary. [metric:site_area_sqm]

Design priorities are as follows: first, stitch gaps and open the ground floor, then deploy reversible lightweight test facilities, and finally discuss development volumes dependent on statutory conditions. This approach allows for short-term operations to provide feedback to the design team on master planning, traffic, utilities, and architectural detailing, without pre-setting demolition and construction conclusions.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The proposal is organized according to the three levels defined in the announcement: the Coordinated Research Area focuses on the 43.6 square kilometers of AI industry ecosystem, strategic positioning, innovation chain, and future urban form; the Overall Design Area focuses on the 11.4 square kilometers of urban and industrial areas surrounding the Jing-Zhang heritage park within 1-2 kilometers, requiring the formation of an overall framework for Urban Renewal, industrial space layout, traffic and municipal support, and Urban Character control; the Key-Area Detailed Design Area focuses on 368.4 hectares of three detailed design regions, requiring the clarification of functional types, building scale, classification of demolish–renovate–retain strategy, Public Space connectivity, and traffic organization. The three layers of scope are mapped to `compliance_matrix.json` to ensure that the required tasks in sections 1.3, 1.4, and 1.5 of the announcement, as well as agent.1-agent.6, have corresponding chapters, layers, indicators, drawings, and HTML evidence. ## Naturalization, Age-Inclusivity, and Boundaryless Academe: Integrate AI into Services and Return People to the City

Advanced AI innovation districts should not serve only developers, businesses, and devices, nor should they default to tech-savvy consumers as their primary users. Instead, they should be a city that welcomes everyone: children, youth, seniors, people with disabilities, those with low digital literacy, night shift and service workers, nearby residents, students, researchers, entrepreneurs, and visitors. All can enter, rest, interact, and receive non-digital equivalent services. "Advanced" is not measured by the density of visible technological infrastructure but by the comfort of the natural environment, the inclusiveness of Public Spaces, and the willingness of people to come together. [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK] [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:public_service_facilities]

### 1. Open to All: There are no default users or remaining groups.

The proposal redefines "The Belt" as a welcoming zone for all. Entry points, pathways, seating, drinking fountains, restrooms, lighting, resting areas, emergency assistance, information kiosks, and activities should not rely solely on smartphones, memberships, access control, or consumption. Space design must undergo seven checks for "equal seating rights": physical accessibility, economic accessibility, language accessibility, digital accessibility, temporal accessibility, psychological safety, and freedom to exit. Vulnerable users should not be post-design test subjects but should be integrated into co-creation, pilot testing, review, and termination mechanisms. [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001] [depth:risk_missing_data]

### 2. From the Building to the Tree Below: Nature is the First Interface of the AI Era

Innovative architecture should not permanently confine people within screens, air conditioning, and meeting rooms. The ground floor and Public Spaces should be inviting to workers to step outdoors for lunch, discussions, breaks, and presentations through the use of openable interfaces, continuous shading, canopies, arrays of trees, benches, pocket gardens, and climate-regulating spaces that are safe and water-friendly. AI should be relegated to the background, used for microclimate information for voluntary queries, irrigation maintenance, flood warnings, and assistive routes; ground-level visual design should avoid exaggerated mechanical skins, robotic forms, or "futuristic" decorations. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-001] [metric:green_ratio] [depth:blue_green_public_space]

Shade, water resources, and climate regulation as a collaborative component of public health infrastructure: Prioritize the retention and enhancement of suitable tree canopies, utilizing permeable ground surfaces, sunken green spaces, rain gardens, and maintainable detention units to reduce heat exposure and manage stormwater; specific tree species, canopy coverage, detention volumes, water quality, topography, and flood mitigation conclusions must await on-site ecological, water resource, and municipal data, not to be replaced by effect diagrams.

### 3. Surface Expressions of Slow Living and Invisible Infrastructure

Green spaces and public areas should reduce exposure to visible mechanical facilities, equipment boxes, railings, artificial decorations, and meaningless signboards. Necessary facilities should be integrated, concealed, and placed close to maintenance paths, and should be made accessible to users through minimal but clear signage and tactile information services; visual noise should not be added for the sake of a "tech aesthetic." Ground transportation should prioritize walking, cycling, accessible mobility, and public transit connections, reserving inefficient time for observation, sitting, strolling, playing, and chance encounters. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001] (Public Space)

To reduce exposure of ground delivery vehicles and equipment, consider utilizing existing underground spaces or new underground interfaces for the organization of a **low-speed, zoned, and manually takeover-able autonomous transport system**. This concept does not represent confirmed construction of underground roads; it is essential to first complete geological, ownership, pipeline, fire safety, flood prevention, evacuation, energy consumption, cost, noise, maintenance, and failure rollback arguments. Hazardous materials, mixed pedestrian and vehicle operations, and operations that cannot be manually taken over are not included in the pilot. [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]

### 4. Intergenerational Partnership Program: Youth are not lecturers, and seniors are not objects of transformation.

Collaboration between young people and the elderly should be based on mutual benefit rather than one-way "digital literacy." Young people can assist seniors in understanding AI services, protecting personal information, identifying scams, and choosing non-digital paths; in return, seniors can share their professional experiences, local memories, care knowledge, and life judgments. Both parties complete real tasks together in settings such as open-ended long tables, repair coffee tables, community kitchens, heritage storytelling, and public deliberations. Young people also demonstrate trustworthy, patient, caring behavior, and accountability for the consequences of technology through open workshops, volunteer services, and public debriefings, rather than performing acts of "being good."

The assessment should not focus on the number of courses but rather on whether seniors can autonomously choose services, whether there is a reduction in help-seeking anxiety, whether young people understand the boundaries of their responsibilities, whether there is a sustained connection between the two parties, and whether those with low digital literacy can still access equivalent public services. Any accompanying activities must not collect unnecessary personal, health, or family information.

### 5. Footstep Wisdom Track: A Touchable, Walkable AI Pilgrimage Landmark

AI holy sites do not adopt towering monuments that can only be looked up to, but rather "foot-level smart tracks" embedded in the ground. They set up three types of ground nodes that are treadable, touchable, and wheelchair accessible along the Jing-Zhang railway development narrative, for professional teams to deepen:

| Ground Node | Railways—AI Narration | Human Interaction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power Track | From Steam, Mechanical Transmission to Electrification | Step on or push the safety components to understand how energy is transformed; set up a static equivalent experience |
| Signal Track | From Railway Signals, Scheduling Coordination to Network Calculations | Multiple People Need to Stand at Different Points to Complete the Coordination Light Strip or Sound Feedback, No Identity Collection |
| Co-Creation Track | From Autonomous Control to Explainable and Exitable City AI | Participants jointly select the topics, review the rules, and access the appeals entry, with results finalized through on-site discussions |

The value of landmarks lies in "shared experience," not in creating new heights of power. All components must be slip-resistant, free of trip hazards, and free of glare and noise disturbances, preserving static reading methods that do not require sensors; professional confirmation is required for content involving heritage conservation, structural integrity, fire safety, and nighttime lighting. [depth:height_massing_character]

### 6. Boundaryless Campus City Ring: Integrating Campus Learning into Society and Bringing Social Experience into the Campus

The schools within the study area and its surroundings should not become isolated islands of knowledge. The proposal introduces the concept of a "Boundaryless Ring of Academe and City," connecting campus life with societal life through public open hours, shared courses, social issue workshops, cross-institutional walking and cycling connections, exhibition of achievements, community services, and joint maintenance. The notion of "breaking down campus barriers" does not mean eliminating safety boundaries or unconditionally opening up the campus. Instead, it transforms walls, access controls, and management rules from permanent separations into time-division, zoned, appointment-based, and monitored cooperative interfaces that retain non-digital entry points.

Before the formal campus boundary, school list, safety rules, minor protection, and open resource materials are in place, this plan does not draw specific routes through the campus nor does it commit to a particular scope of openness. ## Seat of Justice: Rebuilding Face-to-Face Cities Through Equal Seating Rights

Future AI should not become a curse that further divides people but should serve as a platform and foundation for communication. As repetitive labor is increasingly handled by machines, the most precious resource in human cities is not just efficiency but the time to stay together, the opportunity to be heard, and the space for comfort, encouragement, praise, and applause. The more we enter the AI era, the more face-to-face relationships should not be replaced by interfaces, recommendation streams, and automated services. To this end, this proposal translates the user's concept of "butt justice" into an official design concept—**Equal Seating Rights**: anyone's entry, stay, sitting, speaking, and being heard should not be determined by consumption capacity, occupational identity, physical ability, age, household registration, device ownership, or algorithmic ratings. [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK] [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:blue_green_public_space]

### Five seating rights

1. **Access Rights**: The seating network connects to transit stations, communities, parks, heritage sites, and three key areas, without requiring access through gates, consumption, or registration as a prerequisite for public seating.
2. **Right to Rest:** Ensure the provision of sufficient non-commercial, unshaded, fee-based seating that does not require scanning codes; cleanliness and order rules must not be designed to be hostile to homeless individuals, night-shift workers, or low-income populations.
3. **Shared Seating Rights**: Each exchange module simultaneously considers wheelchair seating with armrests and backs, positions for children and caregivers, different seat heights, movable chairs, and quiet side seats, ensuring that people with varying physical conditions are at the same conversation level.
4. **Right to Speak and Exit**: Sitting in a circle does not equate to forced socialization. The space provides open round tables, two-person benches, individual side seats, and pathways that allow for easy exit; AI does not determine who is worthy of an invitation based on emotion or facial recognition.
5. **Right to Be Heard:** AI can provide real-time captions, cross-language translation, hearing assistance, topic cards, and activity matching on a voluntary basis, but it will not record private conversations, establish social credit, or replace hosts, social workers, friends, or neighbors.

### Audit-able Conceptual Goals

Subsequent professional refinement recommendations suggest incorporating the "right to equal seating" into on-site observations and joint evaluations: record the all-day availability of free seats, co-seating units for wheelchairs, combinations of seat heights and backrests, the proportion of solitary versus group seating, participation in cross-group activities, services that can be accessed without digital devices, and exit events caused by hostile design or security rules. The specific numbers and proportions must be determined after the Official Boundary, foot traffic, accessibility, fire safety, operations, and co-created resident materials are in place; no pseudo-precise metrics are currently being manufactured. [metric:public_space_ratio]

Evaluation is not based on the criteria of "the longer one stays, the better" or "the more interactions, the better," but rather on the standard of whether something is truly present: people can join in, or they can remain silent; they can gather in a group, or they can be alone; they can use translation tools, or they can be completely offline. The success of AI is not about speaking for people, but about enabling more people to sit down and speak with their own voices.
