CENTENNIAL JING-ZHANG AI INNOVATION BELT · OPEN URBAN DESIGN PROPOSAL

ONE SEATJING-ZHANG / 京张一席

AI should not occupy the city first. It should first give residents a place to sit, ask, opt out, and reach a human.

Before an AI pilot receives one square metre of city space, it must return one square metre of visible public benefit.
01

Rewrite “smart” as a public action, not a device label

A seat is both a physical place and a position in public decisions. Each node combines accessible rest, a plain-language data notice, a non-digital route, a responsible human, and a review date.

04

Five questions decide whether technology stays or leaves

Calls and screens do not prove progress. Every 100 days, each pilot passes space, data, and public-value gates.

Can a person really sit or pause here?
Can the service work without a phone?
Is data use minimal and understandable?
Can a responsible human be reached?
Can the pilot fully remove, delete, or switch off?

100-day review

Gate one: space + accessibility
Gate two: data + safety
Gate three: public value

05

Test with movable seats before deciding what to build

Implementation does not pour the concept into permanent concrete at once. Small trials retain the right to leave, allowing the city to learn through use.

P0

Co-test preparation · 0-100 days

Accessibility audit, voluntary interviews, issue map, and device register; place twelve movable seats.

P1

Small trials · within year one

One controlled pilot per room; continue only after safe operation, usable exit, and public review.

P2

Urban stitching · years 1-3

Repair three east-west walking links, shaded rest, courier resupply, and public ground floors.

P3

Network governance · years 3-5

Create a data trustee, open evaluation, and Seat Exchange Day; replicate only approved scenarios.
