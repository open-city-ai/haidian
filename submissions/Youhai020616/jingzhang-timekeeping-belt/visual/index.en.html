Jing-Zhang Timekeeping Belt · Keeping Civic AI On Time

The railway's oldest operating discipline — the timetable — becomes civic AI governance: every AI service in public space holds a service timetable with an expiry date, periodically time-checked against a human-maintained baseline. Overdue services degrade automatically to their guaranteed non-AI path; every delay enters a public, append-only ledger. One spine, three stations and eight time-check points carry the protocol.

Key design: the T4 window is the expiry date. Approving a service and scheduling its exit are one act — continuing requires evidence (a passed time check), stopping is the default when no one acts, so pilot-forever is structurally impossible. The service floor is anchored in current law: Barrier-Free Environment Law Art. 39 (retain staffed traditional service), Generative AI Interim Measures Art. 14/15 (remediation and complaint feedback).

How a self-evolving service stays on time

Evolution voids the passThe pass binds to the version hash: any self-evolution voids it at once; the service re-enters only through a fresh sandbox time check. The pass is not inherited, the record is; no hot-patching in public space.
Red-blue time checksWithin human-approved classes, a red checker agent generates adversarial probes so no service can memorise the exam — agents write questions, humans define passing; discovered gaps feed the co-build room.
Certified time-checkersChecks are staffed by certified time-checkers, trained through university practicum courses; checkers recuse from services they helped build; humans adjudicate.
