## Core Mechanism: Living Ledger & Three-Entry Redline

Every working family keeps its own ledger: one column for housing, one for the children, one for daily necessities - settled at month-end, trimmed when over budget. This proposal translates that **household bookkeeping instinct** into an urban governance and spatial organisation mechanism that keeps working families "able to stay and live well" in the AI era - the sharp core of the "Jing-Cai Living Belt" is not an abstract zoning scheme but a belt that is **booked, settled, and constrained the way a family runs its household**:

- **Three-Entry Redline** = the three ledgers of living cost: **monthly childcare fees, commuting time, and housing cost**. Any AI service or industry introduction must first "make an entry" - declaring the impact it will have on these three ledgers; any service that pushes up the three ledgers automatically trips the redline.
- **Ledger Entry** = the booking unit for an AI service entering a living-service section: one service one entry, no entry no service. Each entry registers service scope, data boundary, and the promised impact on the three ledgers.
- **Living Ledger Section** = the deployment unit of space + service + price commitment: childcare, commuting and housing costs within a section are constrained by the three-entry redline, and AI services are booked and metered per section.
- **Monthly Settlement** = the "Dad's Review Panel" reviews all entries monthly, exactly as a family settles its accounts: over budget means downgrade, on target means renewal - turning the instinct of "running a household" into an auditable urban governance rhythm.
- **Human Review & Degrade** = every AI living service keeps a human service desk and a failure-degradation path, consistent with the common sense that "when things really go wrong, handle it by hand."

One-liner: **ONE BELT, THREE ENTRIES - NO ENTRY, NO SERVICE**. The mechanism maps directly to the submitted geometry: entries and redline sections map to the conceptual `AI_SERVICE_ZONE` layer, service nodes to the conceptual `SCENARIO_NODE` layer, and section boundaries are expressed by `PHASE` and `LAND_USE` combinations; all are conceptual proposals for professional teams to deepen[depth:overall_spatial_structure][source:SRC-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK].

**Living-Ledger lifecycle (conceptual operational definition)**: entry (submit service plan and three-ledger impact declaration) - record (redline-section registration, write into ledger) - use (call data and services within the section by ledger permission) - settle (the "Dad's Review Panel" monthly review checks whether the three ledgers are over budget) - close/degrade (exit on violation or unmet acceptance). Data rights and duties anchor on the ledger: the entrant is responsible for data in the section, collection follows the minimisation principle, and the whole process is auditable; the three-entry redline anchors on three observable indicators - monthly childcare fees, commuting time, and housing cost - and any AI industry introduction that raises these three indicators within a section triggers downgrade at monthly settlement[depth:risk_missing_data][depth:existing_conditions_diagnosis]. **The Three-Entry Redline is not enforced by the panel's word alone but anchored to implementable spatial and public-investment tools** (conceptual): ①childcare fees - tied to childcare facility provision standards, inclusive childcare subsidies and rental-housing childcare support; AI service entries verify inclusive childcare supply in the section; ②commute time - tied to slow-mobility priority investment, rail integration and greenway gap-closing, with an annual share of public investment increment committed to commute-improvement projects; ③housing cost - tied to talent-apartment/rental-housing provision ratios and rent monitoring disclosure; new industry intake must concurrently deliver its rental-housing provision commitment. The Dad's Review Panel runs the monthly settlement and publishes results; execution relies on planning conditions, public investment and rental-market monitoring - not "review without implementation"[depth:scenario_space_operation_matrix][depth:phasing_implementation].

The difference from generic "zoning management" is that this is a governance mechanism **modelled on household bookkeeping, with an entry-settle-close loop that is auditable and has an exit path**, rather than a static zoning - a ledger is something people can understand and come to settle each month, which is far closer to the real life of a working family than any token system[depth:scenario_space_operation_matrix].

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

In the coordinated research area, we shift the proposition from "how many AI buildings" to "how AI serves working families"[source:SRC-BEIJING-UA-PLAN]. The Jing-Zhang Railway was the first trunk railway independently designed and built by Chinese people; its spirit of self-reliance echoes today's call for AI self-reliance across a century[source:SRC-JINGZHANG-HERITAGE]. We propose positioning the innovation belt's industry around "affordable AI living infrastructure": community-level AI services, affordable public computing, and AI-literacy spaces for ordinary families - rather than another cluster of aloof headquarters. Future-city research therefore centers on the "people-algorithm-daily life" relationship rather than GDP. We further distill the industry proposition into a "living-cost red line": no AI industry introduction may push up surrounding childcare, commuting, or housing costs, ensuring working families can stay and live well.
