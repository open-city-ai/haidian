# Co-Verify Jing-Zhang | Proofline Jing-Zhang

"Co-verify Jing-Zhang" is not about affixing another layer of AI labels to the Jing-Zhang line, but rather translating the most valuable spirit of the railway—measurement, calibration, collaboration, and leaving traceable records—into future urban systems. The proposal uses the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park as a **Proofline Co-Verification Mainline**, connecting three innovation hubs: Zhongzhiyuan, Beijing AI Origin Community, and Dazhongsi. It extends to the wings of university research and urban life, setting up twelve walkable, publicly accessible, and human-reviewable scene stations. AI products are first tested in small-scale applications in real urban problems, making their applicable boundaries and failure records public. Professional personnel and users then jointly decide whether to expand their application. (Human Review)

The spatial structure is summarized as "one line, three cores, two wings, and twelve stations":

- Line One: A conceptual Proofline corridor within the submission range of approximately 1092.647 meters, overlaying rail heritage, the Walking and Cycling Network, open testing, and public narrative onto a single everyday route [metric:proofline_length_m].
- Three cores: Zhongzhiyuan is responsible for autonomous technology and safety verification, the Original Point Community is responsible for near-school technology transfer and open-source collaboration, and Dazhongsi is responsible for urban applications, international exchanges, and consumer experiences; the three areas currently serve as temporary research boundaries [metric:key_area_count].
- Wings: The west side "Knowledge—Research and Development Wing" connects academic institutions and research entities with innovative enterprises, while the east side "Life—Market Wing" connects communities, public services, and commercial scenarios.
- Twelve Stations: Four Industrial Validation Stations, Five Urban Service Stations, and Three Cultural Public Stations, with participation entry points through spatial nodes rather than mobile applications [metric:scenario_node_count].

The symbol consists of two parallel tracks and an open verification loop: deep blue represents engineering credibility, Jing-Zhang orange represents history and action, and aqua green represents open collaboration and ecology; an open endpoint is retained at the end, symbolizing that any model conclusion is subject to questioning, revision, and versioning. This graphic, along with all the drawings in this proposal, is a procedural original creation and does not use unlicensed trademarks, photos, or font assets.

Five international cases are extracted for inspiration, not direct replication. The inspiration from Singapore's one-north is to integrate work, living, learning, and shared facilities into a single innovative community. Hence, this scheme does not construct a closed "AI Park," but instead connects enterprises with the community through an accessible public ground floor and pedestrian corridors [source:CASE-ONE-NORTH]. Paris-Saclay emphasizes the transfer of technology between laboratories, proof-of-concepts, and incubators and the society, transforming into a "research—prototype—real scenario—feedback" short chain for the original community [source:CASE-PARIS-SACLAY]. London's Knowledge Quarter illustrates that knowledge clusters can also sustain operations through member networks, public activities, and places, translating into a Proofline membership collaboration mechanism across universities, enterprises, museums, and community institutions [source:CASE-KNOWLEDGE-QUARTER]. Cambridge's Kendall Square's experience of transitioning from an industrial area to a mixed-innovation district supports treating transportation, living amenities, and neighborhood Public Spaces as innovation infrastructure [source:CASE-KENDALL-SQUARE]. The project-based recruitment and phased cultivation of STATION F inspire the "Co-Test Camp" to recruit teams based on specific problems, set exit thresholds, rather than allocate display positions based on the fame of the enterprises [source:CASE-STATION-F].

The industrial ecology is organized into four mutually validating chains: universities and research institutions propose verifiable knowledge propositions; enterprises and open-source communities form prototypes; urban operators provide compliant, limited, and revocable testing conditions; and residents, users, and professionals collectively evaluate public value. The spatial supply corresponds to four types of carriers: isolated test spaces, a co-attesting hall that displays failure records, a shared ground floor that can be quickly adjusted, and community services that ensure daily living. Any industrial project entering Proofline must publicly disclose the responsible party, testing period, minimal data set, Human Reviewers, methods for handling unexpected downtime, and exit conditions.

Brand operations are conducted on an annual cycle rather than through one-off festivals. "Proofline Week" is a conceptual annual activity proposal: on the first day, city issues are released; from the second to the fourth day, small-scale testing and peer reviews are open; on the fifth day, residents and professionals conduct reverse questioning; on the sixth day, improved versions are showcased; and on the seventh day, the "Proofline Ledger" is released, documenting projects that have been approved, fixed, paused, or exited. Quarterly "Failures Exposed" debriefing nights are held; monthly open-source walking classes are arranged for newbies; and the current testing status is displayed daily at stations along the route. The activity does not equate to a government commitment and must be legally obtained by the future operating entity for the site, data, and safety permits.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The Land-Use Plan should be expressed according to public standards such as land space investigation, planning, and use control classification, forming a complete, closed, and seamless land zone division. The architectural scheme should distinguish between retained, renovated, updated, newly constructed, or yet-to-be-confirmed objects, and clearly define the suggested levels for the Building Footprint, function, scale, appearance, roof, massing, and height control. If the current buildings, ownership, control plan, and engineering conditions are lacking, the scheme can only propose methods and a list for calibration, but cannot fabricate the Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy conclusions.

Land use classification is based on [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE]. Building Height, massing, façade, and aesthetic controls are managed by [depth:height_massing_character], while the demolish–renovate–retain method is managed by [depth:retain_renovate_demolish]. The primary evidence for land use and buildings is [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001], [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001], and [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm]. (Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy)

The building scale and intensity metrics must be consistent with those in `metrics.json` and the layers. If the total building scale, Floor Area Ratio, Building Height, Building Coverage Ratio, green space ratio, setback, and building control lines lack official conditions, they should be listed as unknown or pending_control in the metric system, and must not be assigned fixed values to create a sense of precision. The A3 document should provide an updated project list and metric review table, while the A0 board should clearly express the key spatial structure and focal areas. The HTML page should offer an interactive view of the metrics and layers.

The Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy uses evidence thresholds rather than pre-drawing colors. Level One "Retain" applies to legally protected objects, buildings with good structural and usage conditions that support the continuity of the block; Level Two "Micro-renovation" targets buildings with closed first floors, high energy consumption, and insufficient accessibility but are otherwise primarily usable; Level Three "Functional Reconfiguration" targets inefficient or vacant spaces, converting them into shared halls, shared laboratories, and talent services through reversible interior finishes; Level Four "Update Candidates" must enter the demolition comparison only after all property rights, structural safety, cultural heritage, carbon emissions, and community impact assessments are complete. Current publicly available data are insufficient to make specific judgments on buildings for demolition, so the submitted layers do not express definitive demolition targets. Building Height follows the principles of heritage corridor interface continuity, moderate concentration around rail transit stations, and transitions along rivers and communities, with specific values to be determined by statutory control conditions.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The Blue-Green Space plan should be based on the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park Vitality Belt, integrating the travel needs of Qinghe, Xiaoyuehe, surrounding universities, enterprises, and communities, proposing a network of pedestrian paths, cycling paths, and green spaces that are north-south connected and east-west linked. The plan should identify pedestrian and cycling discontinuities, overpass nodes on the ring road, and landscape nodes at the southern and northern ends of the park, proposing a composite utilization strategy for parking, sports, innovative interactions, technology testing, application demonstrations, and public services.

Blue-green Public Spaces are reviewed by [depth:blue_green_public_space], with core evidence including [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-001] and [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001], as well as [metric:green_ratio] and [metric:public_space_ratio]. The Urban Design Management Measures require a holistic approach to landscape appearance, public spaces, and building control, thus this section also references [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES].

The urban design scheme should integrate the historical and cultural elements of the Jing-Zhang Railway, the innovation culture of Zhongguancun, and the AI innovation culture, utilizing cultural resources such as the Tsinghua Garden Railway Station and the Beijing Film Academy. It should propose the Urban Character, architectural style, roof forms, massing, facades, and guidelines for public art. The agent should also propose sign systems, cultural symbols, international communication narratives, AI pilgrimage landmarks, contribution walls, or honor display systems, but all brands, fonts, images, likenesses, and corporate logos must have clear rights sources. The control of the urban character should distinguish between official controls, design recommendations, and conditions to be confirmed, and no pseudo-precise control lines should be provided without cultural heritage or control plan justification.

Cultural narrative is not a patchwork of "old railway + new screens," but a continuous retranslation of three knowledge production modes: the Jing-Zhang Railway demonstrates autonomous construction capabilities through surveying, experiments, and engineering archives; Zhongguancun accelerates technology transfer through research, markets, and open collaboration; and the AI era requires that the evidence, limitations, and responsibilities of models be reintroduced into the public sphere. Along the route, signboards draw on the language of mileage, gauge, signals, and version numbers, providing all digital content with both physical plaques and accessible text summaries, thus avoiding the dominance of short-lived large screens in heritage spaces.

Three "AI Pilgrimage Landmarks" are spaces of public engagement rather than design competitions [metric:pilgrimage_landmark_count]. **Proofline Zero Co-Verify Zero Kilometers** is located in the Origin Community and serves as the registration and public objection entry point; **Open Milestone Walk Open Milestone Path** along the main corridor showcases replicable contributions, failure records, and version evolution; **Human-in-the-Loop Forum Human-in-the-Loop Forum** is situated in Dazhongsi and hosts professional review, resident inquiries, and international exchanges. Together, they form a complete experience of "beginning verification—along the way, see evidence—co-make decisions," allowing ordinary visitors to understand how AI enters the city even if they do not use their phones.
