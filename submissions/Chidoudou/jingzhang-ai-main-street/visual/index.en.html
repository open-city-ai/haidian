Mobility, blue-green space and seven rights

  1. Right to knowShow purpose, capability limits, accountable roles and current operating status on site.
  2. Right to chooseParticipation is voluntary; AI use is never a condition for accessing a public service.
  3. Right to an equivalent serviceKeep an equivalent no-app, non-AI, paper or human service route available.
  4. Right to human reviewResults affecting rights, safety, health or important resources must be reviewable by a named human role.
  5. Right to correct and deletePeople can correct errors and request withdrawal, deletion or an end to further data use under published rules.
  6. Right to appealProvide at least two accessible appeal routes among on-site, telephone and offline channels, with a recorded outcome.
  7. Right to exit and recoverAfter a pilot stops, restore ordinary public use, maintain service continuity and publish the reason for exit.

Buildings and renewal projects

Interfaces first; buildings later

Verify heritage, structure, rights, safety, public ground-floor access and accessibility before low-carbon adaptation or reversible additions. Height, FAR and demolish–renovate–retain decisions stay unknown.

Ten conceptual projects; three phase gates

Start with protocols, walking links and staffed service interfaces; deliver first-use yards next; expand, pause or exit only on public evidence.

Self-check status and assumptions

Passing four self-check gates means only that the package is reviewable. The site, three key areas, land use, buildings and project timing are provisional constraints. They cannot support official redlines, precise areas, statutory controls, ownership or engineering conclusions, and do not mean selection, approval or implementation.
