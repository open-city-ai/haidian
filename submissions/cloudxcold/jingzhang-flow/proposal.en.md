# Time Linked, Innovation in Motion

**JINGZHANG FLOW**

**An urban development belt that links city spaces, carries Jing-Zhang memory, and moves innovation results.**

**Time Linked. Space Woven. Future in Motion.**

## Design Basis and Source List

The proposal responds to the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt Open Call for Urban Design, the Agent Taskbook, the public site package, published policy material, OpenStreetMap context, and project-cleared design inputs. Evidence is separated into organizer requirements, publicly verifiable facts, design judgments, and information still requiring verification. The proposal never treats a public-map sample, a screenshot, or a conceptual diagram as a statutory planning control. [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK] [source:OSM-OVERPASS-20260829]

Complete official parcel boundaries, ownership, regulatory controls, surveyed buildings, utilities, and engineering conditions were not included in the cleared package. The submitted boundary and three key-area polygons therefore remain provisional constraints. They support conceptual generation and internal recomputation, but they do not replace an official planning boundary, cadastral survey, or professional site investigation. [source:SITE-PACKAGE] [data:geometry/site_boundary.geojson]

The human-readable proposal is coordinated with a machine-audit layer: GeoJSON carries spatial evidence; `metrics.json` records values and formulas; source, assumption, compliance, professional-standard, and design-depth files preserve traceability. Every spatial intervention is framed as a conceptual recommendation for later professional deepening.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized through three nested levels. The 43.6 km² Coordinated Research Area explains the regional knowledge, industry, community, transport, and ecological relationships. The approximately 11.41 km² Overall Design Area focuses on the Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park and the surrounding one-to-two-kilometre urban fabric. Three Key-Area Detailed Design Areas explore how the overall strategy can operate in contrasting urban settings. [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [metric:site_area_sqm]

The levels answer different questions: the research layer explains why the strategy is needed; the overall-design layer defines the public-space, land-use, and innovation-flow system; the key-area layer shows how spatial operations, programs, users, and implementation conditions come together. Their boundaries are conceptual and must be recalculated when organizer-supplied official geometry becomes available.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The research identifies an unusually dense concentration of universities, institutes, technology enterprises, young talent, and cultural resources. The main weakness is not a lack of innovation assets but weak everyday links across campuses, industrial compounds, neighbourhoods, roads, and rail infrastructure. The Jing-Zhang corridor can therefore become a shared civic medium rather than an isolated linear park. [depth:existing_conditions_diagnosis] [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]

Three key areas form a result-flow sequence. Beijing AI Origin Community concentrates knowledge creation, open-source collaboration, talent exchange, and incubation. Zhongzhiyuan supports full-stack integration, engineering acceleration, trustworthy validation, and professional collaboration. Dazhongsi connects innovation with market experience, public adoption, content consumption, and feedback. The Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing supplies knowledge, capital, data, and professional services; the Xiaoyue River Scenario Enablement Wing contributes community needs, public services, ecological interfaces, and operational feedback.

International cases—including Punggol Digital District, Barcelona 22@, Kendall Square, London Knowledge Quarter, Paris-Saclay, Toyota Woven City, and Toronto Quayside—support seven transferable lessons: organise a network rather than a single park, renew incrementally, open ground floors and forecourts first, mix daily life with innovation, separate ordinary exchange from controlled testing, use real operational feedback, and keep unverified conditions reversible.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The central concept is expressed as **one spine linking three zones, two wings weaving a circulation loop, and multiple paths integrating the city**. The Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park is the longitudinal public spine. Cross-city public links stitch east and west through streets, street corners, pocket parks, building forecourts, open ground floors, semi-outdoor passages, shared courtyards, blue-green spaces, and interchange nodes. [depth:overall_spatial_structure] [data:geometry/land_use.geojson]

The idea has three simultaneous meanings. Spatial linkage reconnects fragmented urban resources. Temporal linkage carries railway heritage from the past through the present into future urban life. Innovation transport moves knowledge, teams, prototypes, standards, products, public demand, and feedback among the three zones. The park is therefore both public-space infrastructure and the visible carrier of an innovation cycle.

Seven permeation strategies respond to different existing conditions: continuous streets, activated forecourts, porous buildings, shared boundaries, converted infrastructure edges, blue-green linkage, and multi-level hub connection. They are not fixed templates. Each intervention starts with the lowest-cost feasible action—shared existing facilities, activated ground floors, adaptive reuse, lightweight additions—and moves toward new construction only where evidence and conditions justify it.

Land use follows continuity rather than wholesale replacement. Existing university, research, community, commercial, and residential functions remain the base. Publicness is added through compatible mixed use, shared interfaces, public-service infill, and a limited number of incremental opportunity sites. Formal land-use codes, FAR, building height, and parcel-level demolition decisions remain subject to official controls and site verification.

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

The three areas are not isolated projects; each demonstrates a different part of the result-flow system. Their polygons remain provisional and are unsuitable for official redline or precise-area claims. Detailed actions are therefore expressed as adaptable spatial prototypes, operational sequences, and candidate nodes rather than engineering commitments. [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design] [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson]

**Beijing AI Origin Community — Knowledge Source and Incubation.** The proposal turns concentrated university knowledge into a near-campus innovation network. Its structure is one park spine, one Wudaokou innovation core, five inter-university stations, and a connected walking-and-cycling network. Shared classrooms, public ground floors, forecourts, courtyards, and third places support open-source collaboration, student exchange, project matchmaking, and early-stage incubation without building a new closed AI campus.

**Zhongzhiyuan — Innovation Accelerator.** Three flows overlap: research results and teams, professional integration and validation, and public/ecological feedback. Five acceleration mechanisms—connect and share, test and validate, grow and expand, innovation-street interface, and Xiaoyue River eco-innovation interface—translate the area's institutional resources into a distributed, garden-like innovation environment.

**Dazhongsi — Urban Application Lab.** A four-quadrant, three-layer public network links transit, commercial bases, office compounds, neighbourhoods, and public space. Ground-level walking, selected underground connections, and limited second-level bridges improve accessibility without assuming large-scale reconstruction. Four scenario families cover AI content and urban life, urban-agent services, smart-terminal experience and launch, and a controlled reserve for future applications.

## AI Innovation Ecosystem, Personas, and AI+ Scenarios

The AI ecosystem is organised as four linked layers: technical foundations, result transformation, sector applications, and urban support. The area already has strong universities, research talent, computing and model resources, but needs stronger interfaces for concept validation, engineering integration, intellectual-property and professional services, public demonstration, market adoption, cross-boundary communities, and everyday life for young talent. [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK]

Five principal personas guide spatial decisions: students and young researchers need accessible learning and peer exchange; research teams need cross-disciplinary rooms and result-display interfaces; enterprises and engineers need integration, validation, and professional services; residents and public-service staff need understandable, optional, useful services; visitors and partners need clear routes, demonstrations, and collaboration entry points.

Ten scenario cards organise the proposal: cross-campus knowledge discovery; urban-problem co-creation; public release and demand return; full-stack collaborative development; interoperability engineering validation; trustworthy-AI governance evaluation; embodied-intelligence safety validation; smart agents and new-terminal experience; AI content and digital consumption; and business conversion and city partnership. At least three are controlled testing scenarios. Ordinary exchange and public display are prioritised; testing enters public space only where boundaries, operators, safety, human review, and exit mechanisms are explicit.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

![Land Use Structure](assets/figures/land-use-structure.en.png)
*Formal land-use figure: adaptive reuse, mixed renewal, and limited infill for missing public and innovation functions.*

Land use is a conceptual structure based on compatibility, mixed use, and limited infill. The north maintains research and innovation-service functions; the central segment strengthens research, community, and public services; the south reinforces business, culture, and daily urban life. Public open space and the heritage park form a continuous base, while cross-city links improve access to campus, research, community, and commercial resources. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish] [data:geometry/land_use.geojson]

The strategy does not assign unsupported FAR, height, or total floor area. Buildings are classified by action rather than by premature demolition: retain and improve sound active buildings; renovate underused ground floors, forecourts, courtyards, passages, and edges; adaptively reuse suitable vacant space; and consider lightweight additions only after ownership, structure, fire safety, utilities, and demand are verified. Parcel-level decisions must be recalculated against formal survey and control data.

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The mobility strategy preserves urban operation while repairing walking, cycling, accessibility, and interchange discontinuities. The park spine supports continuous north–south movement; stronger east–west links appear in the three key areas; local streets, crossings, campus passages, and small public nodes form a finer network. Ground-level continuity is preferred. Underground or limited second-level links are considered only where rail, arterial roads, or closed edges cannot be resolved safely at grade. [data:geometry/roads.geojson] [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]

Municipal proposals remain a verification framework rather than unsupported capacity claims. Water, drainage, power, communications, sanitation, fire access, emergency movement, and charging requirements must be tested against existing systems and maintenance responsibilities. Public services combine everyday cultural, sports, community, sanitation, and convenience facilities with shared innovation services such as meeting rooms, exhibitions, roadshows, professional advice, and talent support.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

![Mobility and Blue-Green Public Space](assets/figures/mobility-bluegreen.en.png)
*Formal mobility and blue-green figure: the north-south heritage spine works with transverse public links, interchange nodes, and distributed ecological spaces.*

The blue-green system combines the Jing-Zhang park spine, cross-city green links, Xiaoyue River, pocket parks, street greenery, rain gardens, and ecological edge spaces. Public space is treated as a network rather than a sequence of isolated plazas: park paths connect to streets, forecourts, open ground floors, courtyards, bridge-under spaces, transit nodes, and neighbourhood services. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson]

Urban character combines railway memory, Zhongguancun innovation culture, and public-facing AI culture. AI is expressed through how space works—information access, shared services, adaptive operation, transparent governance, and reversible scenario equipment—rather than through a uniform futuristic façade. Building bases should become more porous and welcoming while material, colour, signage, lighting, and heritage interpretation retain a restrained shared identity.

Three AI pilgrimage and honour-display nodes form one monument, one loop, and one field: CODE ZERO at AI Origin Community, PROOF LOOP at Zhongzhiyuan, and URBAN ECHO at Dazhongsi. FLOW MILESTONES provides a distributed honour system along the belt, linking visible achievements to verifiable projects rather than symbolic spectacle alone.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Implementation begins with evidence and low-risk public value. Phase 1 verifies ownership, boundaries, access, building condition, utilities, and operational partners, then improves signage, walking continuity, lighting, planting, and small shared nodes. Phase 2 opens selected forecourts, ground floors, courtyards, and campus or compound passages through timed, booked, or event-based access. Phase 3 adds adaptive reuse, professional services, AI scenarios, and controlled testing. Phase 4 considers limited new construction only where unresolved demand and verified conditions justify it. [data:geometry/phasing.geojson] [depth:renewal_project_list]

The project list covers the park spine, cross-city public links, key-area shared nodes, innovation-service interfaces, hub connections, blue-green stitching, building-base renewal, AI landmarks, scenario operations, and annual events. Each project requires a responsible body, access agreement, safety and accessibility review, maintenance arrangement, and an adjustment or exit mechanism. Performance is assessed through use, accessibility, collaboration, feedback, and public value rather than construction quantity alone.

## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

This is a conceptual urban-design recommendation for an open call, not a statutory approval, investment commitment, construction document, or confirmed implementation programme. Provisional boundaries must not be presented as official planning boundaries. Land use, ownership, building capacity, structures, utilities, fire safety, mobility, heritage, ecology, and operating responsibilities require professional verification before implementation. [depth:risk_missing_data] [source:SITE-PACKAGE]

Project text, identity work, diagrams, scenario cards, and AI-assisted spatial illustrations were created by the project team or supplied and cleared by the user for this submission. External policy, standards, case facts, and public data retain their original provenance and limitations. AI images are conceptual illustrations rather than photographs or evidence of built work.

AI-enabled scenarios must follow data minimisation, privacy, algorithmic safety, content review, accessibility, child protection, human oversight, and clear operator responsibility. Sensing, robotics, automated mobility, or public testing requires visible notice, controllable boundaries, manual takeover, event records, and an exit mechanism. Scenarios without a complete safety and governance loop should not enter open public space.
