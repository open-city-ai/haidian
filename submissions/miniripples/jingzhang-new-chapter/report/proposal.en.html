Design Basis and Source List

The Jing-Zhang Railway, the first trunk railway independently planned and built by China, carries more than a century of engineering spirit and innovation. This proposal takes that historical legacy, the former Qinghuayuan Station, the surviving railway traces, and the surrounding green corridor as its principal spatial and cultural basis.

The proposal combines the documented project identity and design narrative in proposal.md with the structured indicators in metrics.json, the requirement responses in compliance_matrix.json, the standards alignment in standard_matrix.json, and the design-depth evidence in design_depth_matrix.json. The base spatial coordinates and geographic data used by the proposal are subject to their applicable open-data licenses.

Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

At a time of rapid progress in generative AI and embodied intelligence, “Jing-Zhang: A New Chapter” proposes to transform Qinghuayuan Station and its surrounding green corridor into the world's first open urban AI physical sandbox and innovation community.

The vision combines the century-old spatial legacy—the “rail trace”—with a future digital-intelligence network—the “intelligence vein.” The intended result is a future-city demonstration area with historical and cultural depth, spatial resilience, and the capacity for continuous algorithmic evolution.

The industrial strategy is driven by two mutually reinforcing systems:

  • AI + urban renewal: an open algorithm community supports iterative improvement of physical space and establishes a chain extending from chip and foundation-model validation to embodied-intelligence sandboxes and Urban Agent deployment.
  • Physical-digital integration:

    • - At ground level—history and public life: retain railway traces and historic buildings while creating low-density natural, cultural, public, and innovation-exchange spaces. - Below ground—data and energy: use underground railway-related and layered spaces for edge-computing nodes, modular micro data centers, and low-power sensing networks.

Regional Collaboration and the Spatial–Industrial Closed Loop of the “Three Zones and Two Wings”

In response to the taskbook framework for the “Three Zones and Two Wings” Source, the proposal treats the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt not as an isolated corridor-renewal project but as a proposed scenario hub and testing core within Beijing and the wider Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei AI ecosystem. It receives foundational technologies, computing resources, and material innovations from the north; conducts pilot testing, safety validation, and standardisation in Haidian’s dense urban environment; and then sends mature scenarios, product interfaces, and governance rules southward. Every interface beyond the project boundary is a research recommendation. It does not imply an existing dedicated network, a signed partnership, or a government implementation commitment.

Collaboration Logic and Flows Between the Two Wings

  • North Wing—foundational R&D and computing sources: Huairou Science City is proposed as a candidate interface for large-scale scientific facilities and chip and hardware research. Future Science City is proposed as a candidate interface for energy systems, low-carbon computing, and advanced materials. The design ambition is for both nodes to supply prototypes, development kits, models, energy solutions, and technical advice to Jing-Zhang, subject to verification of partners, data rights, and infrastructure conditions.
  • Core—the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt and Beiwei Community in Haidian: the core acts as the proposed algorithm origin and physical-testing hub of the Three Zones and Two Wings. It receives model and hardware prototypes from the North Wing and tests them through dense urban pilots, algorithm tuning, embodied-intelligence trials, and safety-governance exercises in Zhongzhiyuan, the Beijing AI Origin Community, Dazhongsi, and corridor public spaces.
  • South Wing—scaled engineering and application: the Beijing Economic-Technological Development Area is proposed as a candidate engineering and scale-up interface for autonomous driving, humanoid robotics, and embodied intelligence. Control models, edge-governance approaches, and testing protocols validated at Jing-Zhang would enter engineering, manufacturing, and large industrial scenarios only after admission, safety, and intellectual-property review.

Closed-Loop Evolution

The proposal establishes a three-stage industrial loop of original innovation, pilot validation, and scaled engineering:

$$\text{Huairou/Future Science City (original innovation)} \xrightarrow{\text{technology and computing inputs}} \text{Centennial Jing-Zhang (Haidian/AI Origin testing)} \xrightarrow{\text{mature scenarios and standards}} \text{Economic-Technological Development Area (engineering and scale-up)}$$

This forward path is paired with a reverse feedback loop. De-identified corner-case data, failure modes, and safety-governance logs produced in the Jing-Zhang test sandbox may return to North Wing research nodes only after review of data ownership, privacy, trade secrets, and cybersecurity. Quality feedback from engineering and use in the Economic-Technological Development Area returns to the Haidian validation stage under the same safeguards, creating a dynamic research–test–transfer–improve loop.

Non-Digital Service Fail-Safe

When a smart device fails, the network is unavailable, or a user has no smart device, three safeguards maintain basic community services:

1. Human Kiosks: physical service windows with published hours at major transit hubs and green-corridor junctions, complemented by 24-hour emergency telephones or call points. Cash, physical cards, or manually issued credentials remain available where permitted by operators and regulators. 2. Accessible printed guidance: high-contrast, large-text, plain-language, tactile, and Braille maps and instructions identify accessible routes, toilets, resting places, and human-service locations. 3. Human review and appeal: AI-assisted outcomes affecting individual rights—such as access restrictions, security alerts, or parking findings—cannot directly constitute a final penalty or denial of service. The proposal sets a service target to accept and begin human review within 48 hours, with urgent matters transferred immediately to a person; final deadlines follow applicable law, authority rules, and operating standards.

Industrial Test-and-Validation Scenario Protocols (Independent Agent 3 Deliverable)

To address safety and compliance when advanced AI enters real urban space, the proposal identifies three full-loop industrial test scenarios. Each scenario specifies data inputs, model capability, fail-safe degradation, human responsibility, and admission and exit rules. All numeric thresholds below are proposed test thresholds, not statutory standards; operators, regulators, insurers, and specialist safety teams must calibrate them before implementation.

Scenario 1: Outdoor Complex-Terrain Test Field for Embodied Intelligence

  • Data inputs: multimodal LiDAR point clouds, high-resolution depth-camera streams, terrain-resistance data, and joint-feedback sensors.
  • Model capability: dynamic gait control and real-time path planning on unstructured terrain such as stairs, gravel, mud, and slopes.
  • Fail-safe degradation: if a sensor disconnects or the posture angle exceeds the proposed 35-degree safety threshold, the system enters a locked safe state and activates fall-prevention damping. The threshold requires equipment-specific verification.
  • Human responsibility: outdoor tests use a one-to-one remote Safety Operator. Human collision assessment and emergency braking take priority over autonomous control.
  • Admission and exit: a proposed admission benchmark is 100 fault-free hours in simulation. Two boundary-crossing or loss-of-control incidents trigger suspension and corrective review; both thresholds remain subject to the approved safety case.

Scenario 2: Edge-Computing Low-Altitude and Micro-Circulation Delivery Test Field

  • Data inputs: lightweight on-device visual-inertial odometry, ultrasonic obstacle sensors, and local wind-speed and humidity data.
  • Model capability: self-organised coordination and dynamic obstacle avoidance by delivery robots or low-altitude drones in weak-network or offline conditions.
  • Fail-safe degradation: signal loss or computing overload switches the system to hardware-level fallback logic, using a controlled hover and descent, return procedure, or safe stop at a designated refuge point according to vehicle type.
  • Human responsibility: a central control room monitors the airspace or ground micro-circulation zone. Abnormal movement and cross-zone dispatch require secondary human authorisation.
  • Admission and exit: entry requires the approvals, certifications, insurance, and site permissions applicable to the vehicle and use case, including relevant civil-aviation or transport requirements. Entry into prohibited zones causes immediate suspension pending investigation.

Scenario 3: AI Urban Safety-Governance Sandbox

  • Data inputs: de-identified edge-camera crowd-density data, thermal sensors, and acoustic anomaly arrays.
  • Model capability: crowd-congestion risk prediction, multimodal recognition of safety events, and decision support for warnings.
  • Fail-safe degradation: when model confidence falls below the proposed 70% review threshold, the system automatically enters human-review mode and cannot directly trigger an alarm or equipment intervention. The threshold must be calibrated against task risk and false-positive/false-negative costs.
  • Human responsibility: security dispatch, traffic-signal intervention, and on-site response instructions always require human confirmation through a documented Human-in-the-loop process.
  • Admission and exit: connection to the pilot sandbox requires privacy-impact assessment, de-identification testing, bias and security review, access logging, and complaint and correction mechanisms. Any confirmed personal-data leak immediately suspends the interface and starts incident response.

Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The land-use structure combines commercial and research functions, innovation laboratories, an urban park and green corridor, and a mixed-use maker community. The four plots listed in the detailed-design table establish the current control framework for land use, development intensity, height, greening, density, and core functions.

The spatial strategy prioritizes retention of railway traces and historic buildings. Qinghuayuan Station is to be conserved and adaptively reused, while new research, community, and support spaces are inserted around the heritage structure. The current proposal does not identify specific buildings for demolition; any future retain-renovate-demolish decision must follow detailed survey, heritage assessment, and approval procedures.

Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The transport strategy is defined as reduced surface traffic, connected underground space, and priority for walking and cycling.

  • A 2.8-kilometre continuous and accessible walking and cycling greenway reconnects Dongsheng Town and the Qinghuayuan area across the former railway divide.
  • A low-speed autonomous shuttle loop runs around JZ-01 to JZ-04 and connects with nearby stations on Metro Lines 13 and 15.
  • The three-level underground system coordinates walking connections, rail-related space, edge data centers, and data-cable routes.
  • Public-facing AI services include heritage interpretation, environmental sensing, safe night-time movement, rapid prototyping, and district-scale simulation.

Implementation Feasibility and Engineering Risk Control (Specialty 3: Feasibility)

For uncertainty involving excavation, development intensity, automated mobility, and new communications infrastructure, the proposal applies four principles: prominent warnings, staged verification, reversible pilots, and comparison of heavy and lightweight options.

Statutory-Review Warning and Disclaimer

Drawings, tables, and text concerning B1F/B2F utilities, autonomous or low-altitude logistics routes, FAR, building height, road lines, and underground connectivity must display the following warning beside the relevant conclusion:

[Provisional Design Target / Pending Professional Statutory Verification] (Provisional design target; subject to statutory professional review and detailed-construction confirmation.)

The warning means that the content is for proposal comparison and recalculation exercises only. It is not regulatory planning, land-use, heritage, transport, municipal, fire-safety, low-altitude-operation, or construction approval. Future drawing updates must not hide the warning in an unreadable footer or rely only on a general note.

Ground Lightweight Alternative

A continuous underground utility corridor is a high-investment, long-duration option dependent on detailed investigation. Before a decision, it must be compared with the following reversible surface option through life-cycle cost, safety, operations, heritage, and landscape assessment:

  • High-investment option: use continuous or segmented underground utility corridors only in surveyed and approved sections where utility capacity, durability, and spatial conditions justify them.
  • Lightweight alternative—surface or shallow modular micro-corridor plus wireless edge stations:

    • - Modular micro-corridor: place openable and relocatable prefabricated shallow channels along the green-corridor edge or existing hard surfaces to reduce deep excavation. The cost reduction must be calculated from bills of quantities, ground conditions, utility diversion, maintenance, and reinstatement; the proposal does not present an unverified “approximately 60%” saving as fact. - Wireless edge stations: subject to spectrum, cybersecurity, radio-environment, power, fire-safety, and street-furniture review, integrate 5G-A, Wi-Fi 7, or other appropriate access equipment with compact lighting columns and edge nodes for phased deployment. Critical control, emergency communications, and high-reliability services retain wired backhaul or another redundant link; wireless access does not automatically replace corridor-wide fibre.

Both options follow a gate of temporary-node trial, operating evaluation, and then expansion or removal. If official boundaries, utilities, rail-protection requirements, or heritage conditions do not support the heavy option, the project automatically falls back to the lightweight option or cancels the facility.

Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The railway green corridor is both a heritage landscape and the primary slow-mobility axis. Its public spaces combine railway traces, ecological planting, accessible movement, embodied-intelligence testing, and everyday community use.

  • The green-space ratio is at least 38%.
  • Public open space accounts for 42% of the overall design area.
  • Permeable paving and sponge-city facilities reach a target permeability ratio of at least 85%.
  • New research and commercial buildings target 100% Three-Star Green Building certification and integrate distributed rooftop photovoltaics.
  • Demand-responsive misting, shading, water storage, flood-control facilities, and low-level night lighting support climate resilience and biodiversity.

The intended urban character is low-density and human-scaled at ground level, with fine silver-line-like spatial continuity between heritage structures, green space, contemporary research buildings, and the underground digital network.

Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Implementation is organized in three phases:

Long-Term Sustainable Operations and Attraction-to-Transfer Pathway (Independent Agent 6 Deliverable)

Long-term operation uses a three-party framework of government guidance, open-source community participation, and market-based operations, bounded by public interest, transparent governance, and the ability to exit. The proposal recommends studying a Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Community Special Purpose Agency (SPA). The SPA is a candidate governance mechanism, not an existing legal entity or a commitment of government funding or approval.

Candidate SPA Governance Structure

  • Participation: legally qualified government-guided platforms, open-source or public-interest organisations, professional operators, and other reviewed entities may participate through compliant procedures.
  • Responsibilities: spatial operations, matching of computing and test scenarios, enterprise and community services, event management, incubation, public-data audit, and annual performance disclosure. Statutory approval, enforcement, and safety-regulation powers remain with the responsible authorities.
  • Governance safeguards: a baseline for public-space access; disclosure of rents and support rules; procurement and investment conflict-of-interest firewalls; a data-protection officer; independent appeals; annual third-party audits; and a mechanism for replacing or terminating the operator.

End-to-End Attraction and Transfer Pathway

1. Community intake: identify projects through open-source events, case calls, and Hackathons, while providing equal entry routes for resident innovation, accessible technology, and public-service teams. 2. Inclusive incubation: offer time-limited rent-free or low-rent workspaces, basic computing allowances, and shared testing services to seed teams selected through transparent review. Allocations depend on budget, procurement, and fair-distribution rules and do not constitute an unconditional “zero-cost” promise. 3. Capital and scenario enablement: compliant investors independently decide whether to offer equity or other finance. The SPA discloses conflicts and provides booking, risk review, and validation services for the three industrial test scenarios; it does not guarantee investment returns or test success. 4. Scaled application: projects that complete safety, intellectual-property, market, and manufacturing review may choose connections to the Beijing Economic-Technological Development Area or other verified industrial nodes. Transfer or manufacturing remains a company decision and is not a mandatory South Wing outcome.

The annual operating loop is project entry, test validation, resource support, scale-up, performance review, and exit or renewal. Public reporting covers public-space opening hours, beneficiary composition, complaint handling, energy use, test incidents, and incubation outcomes so that operational goals do not erode community rights.
