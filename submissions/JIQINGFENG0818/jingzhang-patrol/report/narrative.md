# Derived Narrative Summary

This file is a short derived summary. It does not replace `proposal.md`, which is the authoritative
main text of this submission.

The Jing-Zhang Patrol Line treats robots and low-speed autonomous vehicles as the city's new track
patrollers: their first duty is not spectacle but walking every segment of the city every day, checking,
recording and reporting.

The proposal is built on a measurement rather than a metaphor. Within the overall design scope, the
slow-mobility network measures 208.9 km but breaks into 113 mutually disconnected components, the
largest holding only 42.9% of the length. Allowing travel along motor arterials raises that to 96.8%,
which isolates the diagnosis: pedestrians and low-speed wheeled devices must currently borrow motor
carriageways to move between districts. Six stitch links totalling 187 m of new footway raise the largest
component to 76.6%; one 19.4 m link alone reconnects 46.22 km of network.

Spatial organisation follows from this. Three patrol depots sit in the three key areas, beats are assigned
by network distance to the nearest depot, eight charge docks are spaced along the belt, and the razed and
disused Jing-Bao line traces (2.63 km) provide the heritage spine.

All spatial content is a conceptual suggestion based on the organizer's provisional boundary. Connectivity
findings derive from existing OpenStreetMap features and require field verification at each gap.
