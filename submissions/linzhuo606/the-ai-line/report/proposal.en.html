Barrier-Free Line: Six First Principles from Blind Authors

This chapter is the second main thread of the entire proposal, and its framework does not come from a manual but from the daily travel experiences of the proposal's author—a blind urban user. During the design process, AI once proposed the idea of "dividing quiet pedestrian zones on the green spine to protect the visually impaired," which was rejected by the author based on first principles: "If blind people can walk continuously on the tactile paving, zoning is not necessary, and I do not want to isolate the blind." This statement serves as the overarching principle for this chapter. International experience also supports this judgment: the lessons from Europe's "shared streets" practice show that open spaces without detectable boundaries, relying on eye contact for yielding, are "chaotic, dangerous, and exclusionary" for the visually impaired—issues do not lie in the mixing of crowds but in the loss of detectable order.

**Six Principles** (defined in the author's first-person perspective, AI engineering translation):

1. **Priority to Continuity**: The value of tactile paving lies not in its length but in its continuity—any break renders it ineffective, as trust is lost. The Green Spine guiding strip is laid out continuously for approximately 9,716 meters 指标, along 空间数据. This Green Spine pathway runs north-south through the entire Green Spine corridor and connects to twelve station plazas, with lateral branches connecting to each crosswalk node. Continuity is a matter of three parts construction and seven parts management: leveraging the existing urban sensing framework from this plan, deploy **AI Monitoring for Tactile Paving Occupancy**—identify occupancy (by vehicles, shared bicycles, or obstructions) → automatically dispatch a cleanup order → include occupancy hotspots in governance metrics, with the "Continuity Rate of Tactile Paving" becoming a real-time public indicator. This is also the 13th AI Scenario Card (Industrial Testing and Validation Category): Occupancy identification is one of the least privacy-invasive tasks in visual AI, with Human Review retained for dispatching. 2. **Fusion Rather Than Segregation**: The guide paths directly traverse open plazas and walkways, allowing both the visually impaired and everyone else to share the same space without any designated "exclusive areas." The only separation is **speed separation**—bikeways are separated from pedestrian surfaces by a grade and material, as quiet, high-speed electric bicycles and scooters pose a real physical hazard to those relying on auditory navigation; this protects all pedestrians (children, seniors, and those looking at their devices), regardless of the group, only by speed. 3. **Sound as Interface**: All 36 concept intersections 指标 are planned to include acoustic pedestrian signals (APS), and include **anti-silence maintenance clauses**—triggered on demand by buttons, mobile devices, or remote controls, with directional speakers and time-of-day volume adjustments to address nighttime noise complaints, prohibiting "fake activation." Each of the twelve stations will feature unique audio signatures for their sound markers (echoing the tradition of railway clocks and the "Intelligent Clock" system), with sound landmarks designed equally with visual landmarks. 4. **Structure Must Be Understandable**: Before crossing an intersection, visually impaired individuals need to mentally model the spatial structure. Three levels of provision: (a) **16 Tactile Model Nodes** 指标 —— twelve tactile map stations, a full-line tactile sand model at the original station, and physical models of complex nodes at the Fifth Ring Node, Dazhongsi Station, and the Huaquyuan Station (continuing the tradition of the bronze tactile models in the "Blind City" in Marburg, Germany); (b) each intersection is equipped with an audio structural description triggered by beacons or high-visibility codes (e.g., "There is a four-way intersection ahead, with six lanes in both directions, a safety island in the middle, and a metro entrance in the northeast corner"), which can be updated at any time; (c) **Open Source Tactile Model Library** —— the three-dimensional model files for the entire line and each node are openly released, allowing anyone to 3D print them and take them home, or community centers to have their own, to familiarize themselves with the intersection before leaving home. According to the author's and AI's joint search, this is the first of its kind in the world's cities, and it fully aligns with the open-source collaborative spirit of this plan. 5. **Be Patient**: Plan for 9 pedestrian safety islands 指标 at crosswalks along the main spine corridors (such as 空间数据 and other main conceptual corridors intersecting with north-south lines), allowing slower pedestrians to wait calmly for the next cycle when the green light ends. Accompany this with **optional extended green light duration** through both mobile and physical card channels. "Not having to rush even if you walk slowly" should be a right, not a privilege. 6. **Numbers Should Reflect Reality**: The road network layer of map navigation in Chinese megacities is already quite good, but the "last ten meters" are broken — GPS errors in building spaces range from 5 to 15 meters, making it impossible to find entrances, the starting points of tactile paving, or crosswalk locations. Solution: Place physical anchor points at intersections and entrances that can be scanned or have beacons to correct positioning; more crucially, establish an **open data layer for accessible facilities** — the coordinates and attributes of each segment of tactile paving, each APS (Accessible Pedestrian Signal), each safety island, and each accessible entrance should be made into machine-readable open data, available for all map vendors to use, and serve as the first demonstration dataset for the "Data Element Circulation" task in the Dazhongsi area. This package's GeoJSON evidence system is the prototype for this data layer.

**Investment Priorities** (Author's Judgment): The in-station human guidance services for the metro are already mature and reliable, making accessibility improvements merely an added bonus; resources should be concentrated on the open street network—those last one kilometer from the front door to the metro entrance.
