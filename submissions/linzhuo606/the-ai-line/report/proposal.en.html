The Accessibility Line: Six First Principles from the Blind Author

This chapter is the second main thread of the entire proposal, and its framework does not come from a manual but from the daily travel experiences of the proposal's author—a blind urban user. During the design process, AI once proposed the idea of "dividing quiet pedestrian zones on the green spine to protect the visually impaired," which was rejected by the author based on first principles: "If blind people can walk continuously on the tactile paving, zoning is not necessary, and I do not want to isolate the blind." This statement serves as the overarching principle for this chapter. International experience also supports this judgment: the lessons from Europe's "shared streets" practice show that open spaces without detectable boundaries, relying on eye contact for yielding, are "chaotic, dangerous, and exclusionary" for the visually impaired—issues do not lie in the mixing of crowds but in the loss of detectable order.

Six Principles (defined in the author's first-person perspective, AI engineering translation):

1. Priority to Continuity: The value of tactile paving lies not in its length but in its continuity—any break renders it ineffective, as trust is lost. The Green Spine guiding strip is laid out continuously for approximately 9,716 meters 指标, along 空间数据. This Green Spine pathway runs north-south through the entire Green Spine corridor and connects to twelve station plazas, with lateral branches connecting to each crosswalk node. Materials and winter regime (a precondition for any continuity commitment, de-watering addition): the guiding strip uses monolithically formed, prefabricated high-strength raised units — glue-on strips and nail-on studs are prohibited, since snow-plow scraping that peels them is the top cause of tactile-paving failure in northern cities; chloride de-icing salts are banned within the corridor (mechanical clearing plus eco-friendly de-icers instead); manual snow-clearing plans list the guiding strip and crossing plates as priority surfaces, backed by a spare-parts pool and an annual replacement budget. If the winter regime is not in place, every continuity commitment in this chapter automatically downgrades. Continuity is a matter of three parts construction and seven parts management: leveraging the existing urban sensing framework from this plan, deploy AI Monitoring for Tactile Paving Occupancy — identify occupancy (vehicles, shared bicycles, piled goods) and feed it into the governance loop. The loop builds no dispatch system of its own: it rests on a pre-signed responsibility matrix (occupancy type × responsible unit × handling deadline × verification method — motor vehicles to traffic police, shared bikes to operator SLAs, shop spill-over to urban management, construction hoarding to the housing authority), and findings drop into the existing 12345 complaint-response and grid-governance platforms as formal, time-bound work orders. Continuity metrics use a repair-oriented caliber: per-segment average work-order handling time and re-inspection pass rates are published, audited monthly by blind users in the field — measuring repair speed rather than an instantaneous state. This is also the 13th AI Scenario Card (Industrial Testing and Validation Category): occupancy identification is one of the least privacy-invasive tasks in visual AI, with Human Review retained. 2. Fusion Rather Than Segregation: The guide paths directly traverse open plazas and walkways, allowing both the visually impaired and everyone else to share the same space without any designated "exclusive areas." The only separation is speed separation—bikeways are separated from pedestrian surfaces by a grade and material, as quiet, high-speed electric bicycles and scooters pose a real physical hazard to those relying on auditory navigation; this protects all pedestrians (children, seniors, and those looking at their devices), regardless of the group, only by speed. 3. Sound as Interface: All 36 concept intersections 指标 are planned to include acoustic pedestrian signals (APS; signal facilities are under the traffic-police authority, with per-intersection co-build agreements). Anti-silence is enforced by preset degradation plans instead of ad-hoc judgement: automatic night switch to on-demand mode (button / phone / remote wake), directional speakers, adaptive volume; "shutdown" is removed from the complaint-closure options, any silencing requires a written justification to the disability federation or the co-creation council, and the sounding rate is patrolled at the same level as tactile-paving continuity. Each of the twelve stations will feature unique audio signatures for their sound markers (echoing the tradition of railway clocks and the "Intelligent Clock" system), with sound landmarks designed equally with visual landmarks. 4. Structure Must Be Understandable: Before crossing an intersection, visually impaired individuals need to mentally model the spatial structure. Three levels of provision: (a) 16 Tactile Model Nodes 指标 —— twelve tactile map stations, a full-line tactile sand model at Origin Station, and physical models of complex nodes at the Fifth Ring Node, Dazhongsi Station, and Tsinghua Garden Station (continuing the tradition of the bronze tactile models in the "Blind City" in Marburg, Germany; outdoor tactile components avoid bare metal — dark metal surfaces can exceed 60 °C in a Beijing summer and freeze-stick to bare hands in winter — using low-thermal-conductivity mineral-resin or stone composites, light-colored low-heat-absorption surfaces, relief drainage details and mandatory shading, preferably under pavilion eaves; bronze remains only as symbolic pieces in shaded spots); (b) each intersection is equipped with an audio structural description served from smart-pole-powered audio anchors or high-visibility codes (battery beacons prohibited; e.g., "There is a four-way intersection ahead, with six lanes in both directions, a safety island in the middle, and a metro entrance in the northeast corner"), content versioned with a last-verified date; (c) Open Source Tactile Model Library — the library publishes symbolized derived models (not raw survey geometry) together with print parameters and acceptance test cards, so that anyone can 3D print them at home or at community reprint points to learn an intersection before leaving home; a version-and-recall system requires updates within 90 days of any junction rebuild, with community notification and consumables written into the annual budget. A non-exhaustive public search found no city-scale precedent; it is positioned as an original proposal awaiting validation, in the open-source collaborative spirit of this plan. 5. Be Patient: Plan for 9 pedestrian safety islands 指标 at crosswalks along the main spine corridors (such as 空间数据 and other main conceptual corridors intersecting with north-south lines), allowing slower pedestrians to wait calmly for the next cycle when the green light ends — a concept count: in deepening, each intersection undergoes a lane-count / saturation / bus-lane screening, kept where approvable and replaced elsewhere by pedestrian-protection phases or extended greens with curb extensions that shorten the crossing, with reductions accepted. Accompany this with optional extended green light duration through both mobile and physical card channels. Rest is likewise time tolerance: benches follow the UK DfT Inclusive Mobility method benchmark (station and key-area segments at most 50 meters apart, elsewhere at most 100 meters; engineering compliance remains with GB 55019-2021 and Beijing local standards), each with armrests, a backrest and an adjacent wheelchair space. "Not having to rush even if you walk slowly" should be a right, not a privilege. 6. Numbers Should Reflect Reality: The road network layer of map navigation in Chinese megacities is already quite good, but the "last ten meters" are broken — GPS errors in building spaces range from 5 to 15 meters, making it impossible to find entrances, the starting points of tactile paving, or crosswalk locations. Solution: place pole-powered audio anchors and high-recognition codes at intersections and entrances to correct positioning (no battery beacons); more crucially, establish an open data layer for accessible facilities — the coordinates and attributes of each segment of tactile paving, each APS (Accessible Pedestrian Signal), each safety island, and each accessible entrance made machine-readable, opened to map vendors in tiers by navigation-map qualification (jointly reviewed with the disability federation and surveying authorities; monitoring data published T+1 or weekly, anonymized — no over-promise of "real-time opening to all vendors"), serving as the first demonstration dataset for the "Data Element Circulation" task in the Dazhongsi area. This package's GeoJSON evidence system is the prototype for this data layer.

Investment Priorities (Author's Judgment): The in-station human guidance services for the metro are already mature and reliable, making accessibility improvements merely an added bonus; resources should be concentrated on the open street network—those last one kilometer from the front door to the metro entrance.

From accessibility to universal inclusion: the six principles naturally spill over to a much wider public — refuge islands and extended greens serve the elderly, children and stroller users; tactile models double as all-age science exhibits; the guiding strip is everyone's directional reference in rain, fog and darkness (the "curb-cut effect"). Four inclusion commitments are added: (1) non-digital alternative paths — every AI scenario and city service keeps a no-phone, no-app way of use (physical buttons, staffed windows, telephone channels), with the tactile models themselves the most important non-digital interface; (2) affordability — public accessibility services (inquiry, accompaniment, model-library files) are free of charge, and open-source model files may be printed by communities to lower the access threshold; (3) a safety net for low-income and digitally disadvantaged groups — station service units keep staffed hours, and every AI refusal or failure closes with a human fallback; (4) international visitors covered by the same structure — a foreigner in a Chinese-language environment faces the same structural problem of "the default channel being unreadable", so the solutions are shared: international pictograms as the base layer of signage and plaques, inquiry columns and audio anchors with live translation (the system answers in whatever language the user speaks), high-recognition codes read aloud in 30+ languages by the user's own phone, and a visitor mode for every service requiring no local phone number or local apps. Accessibility, age-friendliness and internationalization are three names for the same design problem in this proposal. Inclusion is not an appendix to accessibility but its natural extension.
