# Jing-Zhang Zhi Mai: Open and Co-Existing AI City Living Room

The English name is JINGZHANG AI COMMONS. The brand slogan is "One Spine · Three Loops · Five Stations · Twelve Scenarios / ONE SPINE · THREE LOOPS · FIVE STATIONS · TWELVE SCENARIOS." This proposal transforms the century-old Jing-Zhang from industrial transportation legacy into a public learning infrastructure facing the future: AI is not hidden in a closed campus but is understood by residents, researchers, businesses, and visitors in a walkable, verifiable, auditable, and shareable urban living room.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The Coordinated Research Area is understood to be approximately 43.6 square kilometers, addressing how the "Jing-Zhang Innovation Belt can form a unique ecosystem within the global AI network"; the Overall Design Area is understood to be approximately 11.4 square kilometers, addressing how "industry, community, transportation, blue-green spaces, and culture can coexist and synergize within the same urban structure"; the key areas total approximately 368.4 hectares, with more detailed spatial-operational prototypes proposed for Zhongzhiyuan (192.1 hectares), Beijing AI Origin Community (104.3 hectares), and Dazhongsi (72 hectares). The announcement area is used for task-scale description, while the submitted geometric area is derived from a temporary rough polygon, and the two should not be mixed. [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [metric:site_area_sqm] [metric:key_area_count]

Between layers, a two-way mechanism of "sinking research topics and elevating scene performance" is adopted: the upper layer determines five categories of topics—trusted AI, open-source transformation, smart economy, talent community, and railway culture; the middle layer uses a continuous public spine and three types of closed-loop organizational spaces; and the key areas break down these topics into verifiable projects, events, and governance protocols. After anonymization, human review, and public feedback, the pilot data from each key area are fed back to the overall layer to adjust network priorities, and then returned to the research layer to evaluate the industrial ecosystem and global collaboration. This approach ensures that macro visions are grounded and that single-point demonstrations can form innovation corridors.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

Six public case studies provide transferable mechanisms rather than formal transcriptions: Singapore's one-north suggests integrating research and development, industry, residence, and public life into testable mixed-use environments; Cambridge's Kendall Square suggests that innovation employment growth must be synchronized with transportation, housing, and Public Space; Toronto's MaRS suggests that research, business, capital, and the public sector require co-located platforms for daily encounters. [source:CASE-ONE-NORTH] [source:CASE-KENDALL-SQUARE] [source:CASE-MARS-TORONTO]

Paris' STATION F's Share/Create/Chill space gradient illustrates that entrepreneurial communities require high-density workspaces, shared services, and informal interactions; Berlin's Adlershof long-term campus management demonstrates that scientific, business, media, and talent services must be connected by a stable operational entity; Helsinki's Maria 01 shows that brownfield updates can form global networks through member communities, continuous events, and honor displays. [source:CASE-STATION-F] [source:CASE-ADLERSHOF] [source:CASE-MARIA-01] The six cases collectively support the idea that "space is the container for agreements": what truly is migratory is not the landmark form, but rather testing access, shared equipment, open-source release, talent services, public auditing, and long-term event calendars.

Accordingly, the research scope proposes three industrial loops. The northern "Trusted Full-Stack Verification Ring" connects model safety, embodied intelligence, edge computing power, and scenario compliance; the central "Open Source Conversion Ring" connects universities, communities, developers, intellectual property, and enterprise services; the southern "Smart Economic Public Life Ring" connects intelligent body commerce, community care, cultural consumption, and international exchange. The three rings share a set of city-level interfaces: scenario application, risk grading, data minimization, Human Review, public disclosure, performance evaluation, and exit mechanisms. The goal is not to chase after a single enterprise but to reduce inter-institutional collaboration costs and form reusable trusted innovation infrastructure.

Future cities are judged by six categories of people: Researchers need testable and publishable outcomes, entrepreneurs need low-cost equipment and conversion services, enterprise engineers need compliance validation, residents need daily conveniences and appeal channels, public operators need auditable logs, and international visitors need clear multilingual signage. The spatial quality is thus defined as "a 15-minute accessible public learning network, an innovatory district that is governed but always open, and a city path that reads railway memories," rather than isolated science parks.

Spatially, it forms a three-layer interface where equipment can enter and exit, the public can observe, and risks can be isolated; operationally, it implements test application, ethics and safety pre-review, limited time periods, human takeover, data desensitization, and result public summary. ## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The land use structure is based on a continuous park and green space as the public base, with research and education, culture, commercial services, residential, and community services forming a walkable mixed-use area. The shared topological algorithm first subtracts the green public spine from the Provisional Boundary polygons, then zones the remaining area, ensuring that there is no overlap in land use categories, and that the merged zones fully cover the provisional boundary; this is a verifiable geometric relationship rather than a replacement for statutory land use categories. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001] [metric:green_space_area_sqm]

The Building Footprint adopts a discrete, small-scale, and penetrable capacity prototype along both sides, avoiding the creation of a continuous closed perimeter wall. The northern area leans towards laboratories, incubators, and R&D, the central area towards education, culture, and mixed uses, while the southern area leans towards community services, talent housing, and commerce; functions can be adjusted according to scene performance. Due to the absence of existing buildings, the retain_renovate_demolish layer field is uniformly set to undetermined_pending_survey, clearly indicating no individual demolition judgments have been made. [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm]

The workflow for the demolish–renovate–retain strategy consists of five steps: establishing a baseline map of existing buildings and ownership; completing assessments of structural safety, usage efficiency, cultural value, and carbon emissions; categorizing objects as retain, repair, renovate, add, or exit candidates; conducting stakeholder engagement and professional reviews; and then incorporating the conclusions into updated project plans and phased implementation. Buildings related to rail infrastructure, with strong community memory, and adaptable structures should be prioritized for retain and renovate candidates; clear public interest, alternative relocation, carbon cost, and comparative analysis must be provided for any objects that need to exit. (Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy)

Height volumes are controlled using the concept of "corridor low, nodes gathered, and districts with layers": open spaces for sunlight, wind, and views are left on either side of the spine, while public nodes are formed by low-level shared bases that create accessible interfaces, gradually accommodating flexible research and development and living spaces outward. Specific meters, Floor Area Ratio, Building Coverage Ratio, and setback distances remain unknown and will be refined through three-dimensional modeling and multiple scheme comparisons once the cultural heritage, sunlight, traffic, and control plan conditions are met.

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

Traffic strategies prioritize pedestrian and cycling continuity, proposing a central greenway, two parallel slow-moving lines, and seven east-west connecting stitches. The central greenway connects five stations and twelve scenes. The west-side cycling line serves cross-zone commuting, while the east-side walking line links community life. The horizontal lines weave together the railway sides, track connections, and surrounding neighborhoods. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-G-01] [metric:road_length_m] [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]

Do not draw fictitious current major roads, transit entrances and exits, or road right-of-way at this stage. After obtaining official traffic data, overlay the conceptual lines with the existing road network to complete pedestrian isochrone analysis, crosswalk demand, bus transfers, bicycle conflict analysis, freight and emergency access, parking supply and demand, and traffic impact assessment. Connections that do not meet safety or capacity conditions should be moved or removed. Test streets should use reversible facilities, time-limited access, and physical separation, ensuring that general traffic and accessibility are always maintained.

Municipal infrastructure and New Infrastructure form an "first capacity, then scenario" access model: models and robot testing require calculations for electricity, communication, fire safety, water supply and drainage, and waste heat; edge computing nodes prioritize integration with renewable energy, storage, and waste heat utilization; sensors collect only the minimum data necessary to deliver services, and specify storage duration, access controls, and deletion mechanisms. [data:geometry/constraints.geojson#CONSTRAINTS-001] [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]

Public services are delivered through shared front desks and human fallback support: Each station will feature visible human service desks, accessible restrooms, quiet spaces, resting points for children and seniors, multilingual guided tours, and emergency assembly information; AI assistants will provide consultation and support but will not replace formal approval, medical judgment, or public safety responsibilities. The scale of facilities and service radius will be verified after obtaining population, facility status, and traffic data.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green structure converts the Jing-Zhang linear memory into a "city learning river" that is usable in daily life: continuous greenways provide shade, rain and flood regulation, and habitat connectivity, five green cores offer places for pause, activities, and display, and twelve small nodes provide plug-and-play scenarios. The green area and Public Space area are recalculated by the same Provisional Boundary after being cut, and the ratio is only suitable for internal scheme comparison. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-001] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001] [metric:green_ratio] [metric:public_space_ratio] [depth:blue_green_public_space]

Public Space follows four principles of the interface: maintaining continuous open views along the spine; setting multiple entrances and traversable gray spaces at the first floor of buildings; testing areas with readable boundaries to explain risks and participation methods; and providing low-stimulation, low-threshold, and all-weather pause points on the community side. Accessibility is not an add-on, but a system of path gradients, tactile paving, contrast in information, auditory cues, rest intervals, and human assistance. Nighttime lighting avoids glare and over-medialization, with important information readable even when the network is down.

Urban Character does not adopt the generic "tech blue glass" aesthetic, but instead combines railway material memories, Beijing courtyard scale, and visible open-source structures: the rhythm of sleepers is translated into paving and framing, rivets and steel rail details are translated into disassemblable joints, bricks and warm gray establish a daily base, and only cyan and teal are used for public navigation. The new and old relationship emphasizes recognizability without anachronism; digital interfaces serve the physical space, and screens do not obscure historical cues and public directions.

After the rainfall, floodwater, tree resources, water systems, and soil data are in place, the green framework requires further watershed delineation, assessment of sponge facility scale, habitat evaluation, and full-age canopy verification. If the existing water systems or protected trees conflict with the conceptual nodes, the nodes should be moved rather than altering the ecological objects. All temporary activities should use recoverable ground surfaces and modular facilities to minimize repeated construction.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Nine conceptual project packages are sequentially as follows: P1 Smart Pulse Continuous Greenway and Barrier-Free Foundation; P2 Qinghe Trustworthy AI Test Garden; P3 Mass Intelligence Full-Stack Co-Testing Station; P4 Origin Open Source Exchange Station; P5 Xiaoyuehe Urban Life Experiment Station; P6 Dazhongsi Intelligent Economic Living Room; P7 Four Pilgrimage and Honor Nodes; P8 City-Level Data Minimization and Public Audit Protocol; P9 Global AI Week and Community Operations Network. The project packages include space, operations, compliance, and performance, integrating construction and activity operations without separation. [metric:renewal_project_count] [depth:renewal_project_list]

Phase 1: For the first 0-2 years, it is recommended to focus on reversible, lightweight, and low-data-risk public activities and guided tours: after completing data supplementation, public communication, and safety checks, pilot open-source nights, digital narratives, and temporary greenways can be implemented; Phase 2: From years 3-5, with clear property rights, building inventory, transportation and municipal conditions, and legal requirements, promote adaptive reuse, shared services, and connections to Public Spaces; Phase 3: Only discuss full-stack validated networks, long-term infrastructure, and regional coordination after year 6. [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-001] [depth:phasing_implementation]

Each issue will set five decision gates: the Data Gate to confirm boundaries and the current status, the Public Gate to confirm impacts and appeals, the Professional Gate to complete planning, architecture, transportation, utilities, cultural heritage, and safety reviews, the Compliance Gate to confirm data and algorithm responsibilities, and the Operations Gate to confirm the subject, budget, maintenance, and exit strategy. If any gate is not passed, the project can only continue with further research or reduce the pilot scope. The phased boundaries are conceptual north, middle, and south zones for easy expression, not construction segments, project initiation scope, or commitment to timelines.

Policy recommendations include shared equipment booking and safety tiering, open-source contribution feedback, public data minimization, community-prioritized time slots, transparent summaries of failed tests, accessible performance procurement, and annual third-party evaluations. Site authorization should employ time-limited and revocable mechanisms; no single entity should be granted permanent exclusive display rights. Publicly funded components should specify their reusability scope. When operational performance falls below threshold levels, service adjustments should be prioritized before considering a change in space use.

## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

The greatest risk is not whether a particular form is accurate, but that the Provisional Boundary, data gaps, and AI scenarios are misread as legally binding or directly implementable conclusions. To address this, the boundaries and focus areas should be indicated with low-contrast dashed lines; statutory intensities, demolitions, parking, and municipal capacities should not be filled with placeholder values; all spaces and policy actions should be presented with the tone of "conceptual suggestions, awaiting professional refinement, requiring approval confirmation"; and the official polygons should trigger a full recalculation upon arrival. [depth:risk_missing_data]

AI scenarios are risk-rated based on data privacy, technological maturity, implementation complexity, public acceptance, operational costs, spatial disputes, and fairness and inclusivity. Scenarios involving robots, public safety, resident services, and accessibility must retain human takeover, conspicuous notifications, appeals, log auditing, and exit options; no data unrelated to the service should be collected, and no decisions on penalties, admissions, or basic public services should be made using opaque models. Test failures are learning materials, but only de-identified summaries should be publicly disclosed.
