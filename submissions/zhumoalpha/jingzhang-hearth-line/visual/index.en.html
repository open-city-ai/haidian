One street, three markets, west lane and east bank

  • One street: the Hearth Line street is not a scenic greenway but a linear public living room where markets, evening talk and morning exercise happen at once.
  • Three markets: Morning (a research district's morning), Learning (campus life and the open-source community), Night (AI-native night economy and the new temple fair).
  • West lane: government, capital and professional services take the form of small old shops you can walk into and talk in.
  • East bank: a waterside living laboratory of morning exercise, water monitoring and waterside markets.
  • Eight market crossings: the east-west stitching nodes are small markets and service points rather than pure traffic nodes.
Naming logic: the white steam of the Jing-Zhang locomotives and the cooking smoke of the market outside the station are the same rising plume - the reverse side of engineering history is the history of daily life. Zhongguancun began as a market too, and AI-native business is the new market.

Morning Market · Zhongzhiyuan AI Innovation Acceleration Area

North segment: the morning pole of the research district. A scientist's day starts with breakfast, a running loop and a stand-up lawn.

  • A running route and breakfast workshops are paired along the northern street; the breakfast map and the elderly meal service share one window mechanism.
  • The stand-up lawn is the outdoor meeting room of the research community - the spatial handle for a morning that makes researchers want to stay.
  • The full-stack innovation agenda is answered through daily life: a liveable morning comes before a laboratory that retains people.
  • Test T1, a driverless breakfast-cart micro-loop, sits on the internal branch loop and is separated from pedestrian flows by time and section.

AI ScenariosAI 场景

Twelve living-scenario cards and three testing scenarios are anchored to 15 SCENARIO_NODE points. The Hearth Line's stance is that AI steps back so that life steps forward: AI does scheduling, reconciliation, prompting and dispatch backstage, while vendors, teachers, doctors, riders and neighbours stay in front. Every card carries three governance columns - a non-AI equivalent service, a human-review and complaint channel, and an exit-and-stop condition.

Twelve living-scenario cards

SC-01 · SN-001

Breakfast map and elderly meal service

Location
Morning Market: northern breakfast workshops and meal windows along the whole street
Users
Early commuters, dual-income families, older people living alone, breakfast vendors
AI capability
Aggregates stall opening status, queue length and remaining meal portions by time slot and suggests walkable breakfast and meal options.
Privacy and human-review boundary
Uses only voluntary vendor reports and on-site counts; no face recognition and no personal diet profiles. Meal eligibility lists are confirmed by community staff.
Operator (simplified RACI)
R: sub-district meal-service provider; A: community service centre; C: vendors' self-governing group; I: district civil-affairs and market-regulation departments.
Non-AI equivalent service
A printed breakfast map and a notice board at each meal window are updated in parallel; phone and counter registration remain fully valid.
Exit and stop condition
If suggestion error rates exceed the agreed threshold for two consecutive weeks, or an identity mix-up is reported, algorithmic suggestions are suspended and scheduling returns to staff.
SC-02 · SN-002

AI market: price transparency and provenance

Location
Night Market: New Temple Fair plaza and surrounding food markets
Users
Residents buying groceries, stallholders, small catering businesses
AI capability
Turns self-declared prices, batches and provenance into a comparable daily price list and traceability card, flagging abnormal swings for review.
Privacy and human-review boundary
Prices and provenance are information vendors publish voluntarily; no customer identity or consumption profile is collected, and disputes are settled by market staff.
Operator (simplified RACI)
R: market operator; A: merchants' co-governance council; C: market regulator; I: sub-district and consumer representatives.
Non-AI equivalent service
Printed price boards and traceability cards stay in place; a vendor's spoken explanation and physical inspection always take precedence.
Exit and stop condition
Mispricing that causes a dispute, or a vendor report that data is driving unfair ranking, takes ranking and recommendation offline immediately.
SC-03 · SN-003

Neighbourhood feast kitchen: shared-kitchen booking and safety watch

Location
Learning Market: shared kitchens and courtyards in the AI Origin Community
Users
Young renters, international students, neighbourhood families, canteen volunteers
AI capability
Handles time-slot booking, stove and gas status prompts, and cleaning confirmation; abnormal temperatures trigger an on-site alert.
Privacy and human-review boundary
No video recognition inside kitchens - only equipment status and environmental sensing. Booking data is kept only as long as needed and can be reviewed or deleted by the manager.
Operator (simplified RACI)
R: shared-kitchen manager; A: residents' committee; C: fire and gas-safety advisers; I: user representatives.
Non-AI equivalent service
A paper booking sheet and a duty-manager rota run in parallel; anyone can register in person.
Exit and stop condition
If a safety sensor alert is not reviewed within the agreed window, or false alarms disrupt use, operation returns to staffed-only mode.
SC-04 · SN-004

Companion strolling: reassuring routes for elders and pets

Location
Middle segment: the Lantern Wall plaza and the corridor edges
Users
Older residents, dog walkers, accompanying family members
AI capability
Assembles gentle walking routes from gradient, shade, seating, toilets and lighting, and prompts rest points and return times.
Privacy and human-review boundary
No positioning and no trajectory storage by default; family location sharing requires the elder's in-person consent and can be switched off at any time.
Operator (simplified RACI)
R: park and corridor operator; A: sub-district; C: elderly-care providers; I: families and resident representatives.
Non-AI equivalent service
Printed route boards and rest-point signs line the street, and a volunteer walking team runs on a fixed rota.
Exit and stop condition
Any positioning error that risks a person getting lost, or any unauthorised location sharing, suspends the companion function immediately and notifies the family.
SC-05 · SN-005

School-route guardianship: community care for children's walking paths

Location
Learning Market to East Bank: market crossings and hearth-front interfaces near schools
Users
Primary pupils and parents, duty teachers, street-front shops
AI capability
Compiles crossing waits, construction blockages and lighting gaps during school-run hours into a shared care list, and shows which shop havens are open.

Testing and validation scenarios

T1 · SN-013

Driverless breakfast-cart micro-loop

Internal branch loop of the Morning Market segment

A short low-speed loop tests fixed-route operation, yielding and restocking of a driverless breakfast cart, within limited hours and length and always with on-site staff.

Non-AI equivalent service
Fixed carts and staffed breakfast windows operate at the same time, so pausing the test never costs anyone a breakfast.
Exit and stop condition
Any conflict with a pedestrian, or two consecutive days of failed yielding, halts operation and suspends the test.
Incident response and rollback
On-site staff can stop the vehicle with one action; an incident note and rollback plan are issued within 24 hours and a public review precedes any restart.
T2 · SN-014

Low-speed right-of-way test for community delivery robots

Learning Market to East Bank rider delivery lane

Tests right-of-way negotiation between low-speed delivery robots, riders and pedestrians, checking yielding priority, night visibility and takeover points; the test section is separated from school routes in time and space.

Non-AI equivalent service
Human delivery and station pickup remain fully available, and residents may refuse robot delivery in favour of a rider.
Exit and stop condition
Failed yielding, intrusion into a school route or insufficient night visibility ends the test immediately.
Incident response and rollback
Takeover points provide physical power cut-off and manual control; every incident is logged and the rules roll back one version before reassessment.
T3 · SN-015

Age-friendly smart-home pilot in an existing neighbourhood

An older Dazhongsi neighbourhood (Night Market segment)

A small pilot in an existing neighbourhood tests fall alerts, medication reminders and emergency calls for usability and false-alarm rates, and how well elders and families understand the data boundary.

Non-AI equivalent service
Grid-worker home visits, neighbourly checks and landline calls are fully retained; leaving the pilot affects no existing service.
Exit and stop condition
False-alarm rates beyond the agreed threshold, or a participant's request to leave, restore the original service and remove the devices at once.
Incident response and rollback
The data processor must explain remediation within 48 hours and provide deletion evidence; no expansion is allowed before the review is complete.

How the three columns work: the first states how residents get the same service without AI - paper, counters, phone, volunteers. The second states who sees the data, who can review it and where complaints go (responsible, accountable, consulted, informed). The third states which threshold suspends the algorithm and hands the case to people. The three testing scenarios add incident response and rollback. All of this is a mechanism recommendation; thresholds and responsibilities must be agreed by operators before implementation.
Scenarios and landmarks are conceptual recommendations. Scenarios touching personal information require separate assessment and data minimisation, and those involving minors, health or location collect no identifiable data by default. Landmarks sit outside heritage protection scopes and need dedicated review. Urban AI prompts rather than adjudicates, and the final judgement always rests with people.
