# Jingzhang Human City OS

> From an AI showcase to a city for people in the AI era. AI is not the protagonist of the city; it is an auditable, rejectable, and reversible capability serving residents, workers, founders, and public institutions.

## Core Concept and Four Operating Mechanisms

The core concept is the Human City OS: a release system that organizes urban space, public services, and AI agents so they remain observable, rejectable, and reversible. AI is not turned into a landmark, and automation does not replace human judgement. Each urban capability is split into four auditable mechanisms: a human-priority mechanism that protects staffed service, accessibility, and the right to refuse; an evidence-release mechanism that binds place, time, data, accountability, and version gates; a space-service interface mechanism that connects the city API, walking network, public space, and scenario cards into a reviewable service chain; and an exit-and-repair mechanism that requires shutdown, removal, data deletion, objection handling, and independent review together. [depth:phasing_implementation] [metric:version_release_count]

Together these mechanisms turn “from an AI showcase to a city for people” from a value statement into a spatial-governance-operations protocol. Every pilot must answer who may start it, who retains final responsibility, how the public can refuse, what evidence permits expansion, and how the system recovers after failure. Unknown metrics remain `unknown` rather than being filled by model inference or case analogy; every spatial band remains a design suggestion, not a statutory land-use zone, official boundary, or implementation commitment.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The ecosystem is not reduced to attracting large firms. It is decomposed into six capabilities: frontier research, affordable testing, trustworthy data, workforce transition, social acceptance, and international services. Large teams can provide foundation models and platforms. Small teams and one-person companies can enter public-interest scenarios through shared laboratories, model and compute services, legal support, and data-authorization assistance. Workers whose occupations are changing can move through a reskilling corridor toward robotics operations, data quality, scenario operations, accessibility services, and incident review. [source:HD-OPC-2026] [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

Six international cases contribute mechanisms, not imported urban forms. Singapore's Punggol Digital District demonstrates how an open digital platform and digital twin can become callable district infrastructure. Helsinki's Smart Kalasatama demonstrates the value of an inhabited community, resident participation, and agile pilots over a one-time “smart city delivery.” Barcelona's 22@ experience warns that innovation-district renewal must address housing and existing communities. Seoul AI Hub combines education, incubation, research, and public support. Busan Eco Delta City places living labs, a regulatory sandbox, open data, and robotics within the same public program. Toronto's Quayside debate shows why independent governance, responsible data use, and public purpose must precede sensor deployment. [source:CASE-PUNGGOL] [source:CASE-KALASATAMA] [source:CASE-QUAYSIDE]

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The overall design does not replace an existing city with one giant AI campus. It establishes six functional operating layers within the provisional design boundary: community retention and support; international and OPC services; reskilling and public-data learning; reversible white space; city API and embodied-AI R&D; and screen-free green/climate resilience. The six polygons cover the submitted site without overlap, and their areas and shares are recalculated from one GeoJSON source in EPSG:4548. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-H01] [metric:design_partition_area_sqm] [depth:land_use_layout]

The community-retention support band occupies approximately 18.3% of the provisional site, while reversible white space occupies approximately 15.1%. These are spatial proxies describing the design allocation; they are not a resident-retention rate or a statutory reserve ratio. A real resident-retention rate requires a rights-cleared household baseline, eligibility rules, informed consent, and longitudinal registration, so the outcome metric remains `unknown`. [metric:community_retention_support_area_ratio] [metric:reversible_space_ratio] [metric:resident_retention_rate]

The city API uses a six-step protocol: catalogue, authorize, invoke, log, audit, and exit. A municipal-facility agent may call only minimized and tiered interfaces. Water, electricity, heat, transport, and public-data calls retain human takeover and incident traceability. Beijing's current public-data authorization rules emphasize legal compliance, public-interest priority, security, evaluation, and exit. The proposal therefore treats a “data-factor district” as an auditable institutional prototype, not a technology showroom. [source:BJ-PUBLIC-DATA-2026] [data:geometry/constraints.geojson#ZONE-B-API]

Reversible design uses modular components, removable service pods, short-cycle leases, and meanwhile use to mediate the mismatch between three-month AI cycles and ten-year urban construction cycles. Every pilot must file removal, restoration, and data-deletion plans at approval. A v0.2 pilot cannot expand to v1.0 without independent evaluation and public review. Building height, development intensity, construction volume, and retain-renovate-demolish decisions remain for professional development because official controls and surveys are absent. Eight building footprints locate program prototypes only: Community Retention Service Station, Reskilling Workshop, Age-Friendly Human Channel Station, City API Exchange, Embodied-AI Test Bay, Compute-Energy and Recovered-Heat Audit Station, OPC Shared Laboratory, and International Service Commons. Their combined 44,160-square-metre footprint is recalculated from concept geometry. Without an existing-building survey, property data, or regulatory controls, it cannot become a development quantum or demolition list. [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-A-01] [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm]

Retain-renovate-demolish decisions follow “survey first, classify second, decide last.” Safe structures with historical or community value are retained first. Buildings that can be made accessible, efficient, and useful through insertion are renovated first. Only demonstrated structural or public-safety failures proceed to professional demolition review. New construction must show that existing space cannot meet the need and that a reversible pilot has passed evaluation. Every concept building is marked `pending_existing_building_survey`; no conclusion is made about a real building. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish] [depth:height_massing_character]

The anti-gentrification toolkit has four institutional components: a resident-retention registration protocol, priority return for small businesses, temporary trading space during construction, and reinvestment of public benefit into community services. Real retention and return rates require a lawful, informed-consent baseline and longitudinal records. This package reports only the spatial support share and does not market a spatial proxy as a social outcome. [metric:resident_retention_rate] [depth:development_intensity_controls]

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The transport system recognizes people and machines as distinct right-holders, while life safety, accessibility, and the right to refuse automation take precedence. The human network includes the reskilling corridor, night safety connection, continuous age-friendly walking, and screen-free green space. The machine network includes the controlled silicon-right-of-way corridor, a conceptual low-altitude layer, city-API nodes, and embodied-AI test grounds. Intersections use visible status, low-speed zones, human takeover, and incident shutdown; “optimal efficiency” cannot overrule pedestrians. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-A-NIGHT] [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]

The low-altitude concept-corridor length is recalculated from geometry, but airspace, clearance, noise, privacy, fire, route, and permitting data are absent. It is therefore a vertical functional layer for further study, not an engineering alignment. Silicon right-of-way likewise describes a controlled testing protocol, not a grant of road rights. No line on a figure is presented as an approved route or operating service. [metric:low_altitude_concept_corridor_length_m] [metric:silicon_right_of_way_length_m]

Compute-energy coordination is a front-end spatial gate, not a late-stage mechanical optimization. New or expanded intelligent-computing facilities must first comply with current Beijing PUE requirements. The proposal recommends 30% green electricity as an initial design baseline verified by power-purchase credentials and metering, while expressly distinguishing it from a statutory project value. Recovered heat may connect to community energy only when temperature grade, seasonal demand, heat network, metering, and economics are demonstrated. [source:BJ-COMPUTE-2024] [metric:operational_pue] [metric:green_electricity_share]

Public services use dual channels. AI may assist translation, queuing, accessibility navigation, and work-order recommendations, while staffed counters, telephone service, and on-site assistance remain available. A municipal agent can recommend but cannot autonomously issue a planning permit, interrupt water or power, exercise enforcement, or decide personal rights. Interface logs, permission revocation, continuity, and disaster-recovery drills are acceptance requirements. [source:BJ-PUBLIC-DATA-2026] [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]

## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

The largest spatial risk is that an official boundary change would alter every area, route, and node. The largest social risk is rent pressure that displaces residents or small businesses. The largest technical risk is machine movement, data, or a digital twin bypassing human control. The largest energy risk is presenting policy targets as measured results. The largest climate risk is promising flood performance without hydrological evidence. Each risk has an assumption, stop condition, and recalculation trigger.
