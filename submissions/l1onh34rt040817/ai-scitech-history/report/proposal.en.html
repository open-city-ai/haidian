Design Basis and Source Materials

This formal proposal is grounded in the pre-qualification announcement published by the Haidian Branch of the Beijing Municipal Commission of Planning and Natural Resources for the "Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt International Urban Design Solicitation." Machine-readable sources include design_brief.json, allowed_design_space.json, sources.json, enums/, ranges/, schemas/, data/source_registry.json, and data/processed/agent_fact_pack.md. All design decisions must be decomposable into traceable sources, reproducible metrics, verifiable layers, and human-reviewable assumptions. The announcement requires the proposal to reach both regulatory detailed planning urban design depth and comprehensive implementation plan urban design depth, meaning textual narrative cannot substitute for GeoJSON, metrics tables, A3 booklets, A0 boards, and HTML electronic deliverables.

Development Vision: Benchmarking Global Tech Districts

The proposal benchmarks five internationally renowned technology districts: Silicon Valley Sand Hill Road (venture capital-university-enterprise clustering), London King's Cross (historic railway hub to knowledge economy), New York Cornell Tech Roosevelt Island (campus-park-community integration), Singapore one-north (garden innovation园区), and Tokyo Marunouchi (corporate headquarters-international roadshow integration). The underlying logic -- heritage asset activation, innovation clustering, blue-green space embedding, and transit hub integration -- is translated into the Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park's unique temporal-spatial narrative strategy.

Core Concept: Centennial Jing-Zhang SciTech History Timeline

The core concept uses the 9 km Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park spine as a temporal axis, transforming over 100 years of science and technology history (Railway 1909, Zhongguancun 1980s, Internet 2000s, AI 2026+) into a walkable, interactive urban narrative space. The concept is realized through three design operations: (1) temporal anchor markers at spatial segments corresponding to each epoch, (2) space-time mapping that allocates physical distance along the 9 km axis proportionally across the four epochs, and (3) an interactive narrative layer using AR guides, AI voice assistants, and digital twin interfaces.

Spatial Structure: One Axis, Three Cores, Four Epochs, Multiple Nodes

The spatial framework is organized as "One Axis, Three Cores, Four Epochs, Multiple Nodes." The 9 km Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park spine runs from Xizhimen in the south to Qinghe in the north, connecting three core AI industry districts. The three core areas are: Dazhongsi AI Industry Cluster (72 ha, urban intelligent economy district), Zhongzhiyuan AI Independent Innovation Acceleration Zone (192.1 ha, garden-style full-stack innovation district), and Beijing AI Origin Community (104.3 ha, campus-neighborhood technology transfer district). The four epochs along the axis are: Railway Era (1909-1970s, Xizhimen-Dazhongsi), Zhongguancun Era (1980s-1990s, Dazhongsi-Zhongguancun), Internet Era (2000s-2010s, Zhongguancun-Wudaokou), and AI Era (2026+, Wudaokou-Qinghe). Approximately 12 flexible open-source spatial nodes are distributed along the axis.

AI Industry Ecosystem: Four-Epoch Industry Map

The proposal constructs a four-epoch industry map along the temporal axis. The Railway Era focuses on infrastructure and engineering confidence (railway museums, engineering education). The Zhongguancun Era covers electronics and IT启蒙 (university labs, incubators, early-stage tech companies). The Internet Era addresses platform economy and digital services (headquarters, unicorns, tech media). The AI Era centers on large models and agent ecosystems (AI computing facilities, testing scenarios, international roadshows). Twelve core AI industry scenarios are identified, covering the full chain from basic R&D to public experience.

AI-Native Urban Form

The proposal articulates four principles for AI-native urban morphology: (1) architecture-algorithm integration, where building facades incorporate sensors, display interfaces, and environmental response devices; (2) public space as testing ground, where autonomous driving, robot delivery, and smart lighting operate in real urban environments; (3) data-driven spatial iteration, using AI to analyze foot traffic, activities, and energy consumption; and (4) low-carbon intelligent infrastructure combining distributed energy, edge computing, and smart grids.

Transportation and New Infrastructure: Three-Layer Slow-Mobility Network + 4+1 New Infrastructure

The three-layer slow-mobility network consists of: fast layer (12-15 km/h, commuter cycling), slow layer (4-8 km/h, park strolling and cultural experience), and quiet layer (0-3 km/h, AI interactive immersion). The 4+1 new infrastructure includes sensing layer (urban sensor network every 500 m), computing layer (edge computing stations), network layer (low-latency 5G and LoRa), energy layer (photovoltaic pavement, smart microgrids), and the +1 operations layer (AI CityOS digital twin platform).

Public Space Vitality: One Corridor, Three Parks, Multiple Nodes

The public space system comprises the 9 km Jing-Zhang science and culture corridor, three themed parks (Dazhongsi station-front composite ecological park, Zhongzhiyuan Qinghe low-carbon innovation park, Beijing AI Origin Community knowledge-sharing park), and approximately 12 small-scale street-level public space nodes embedding community services, AI experiences, and micro-green spaces. AI scenarios are integrated into each public space type, including AR timeline guides, autonomous shuttle stops, environmental monitoring AI, and outdoor collaborative AI assistants.

Cultural Narrative: Three-Layer Cultural Narrative

The three-layer cultural narrative system covers macro-level city brand narrative ("Centennial Jing-Zhang SciTech History Timeline"), meso-level spatial narrative (paving materials, landscape installations, architectural styles, and soundscape design varying by epoch along the 9 km axis), and micro-level interactive narrative (AR historical restoration, timeline interactive screens, AI dialogue assistants, and contributor digital walls at 12 flexible nodes).

AI Scenario Cards: 12 Scenario Cards

Twelve AI scenario cards detail the proposal's core AI application scenarios, each specifying target users, spatial location, data sources, privacy boundaries, human review mechanisms, and operating entities. Scenarios include open-source launch halls, security governance sandboxes, edge computing stations, AI slow-mobility navigation, international roadshow halls, low-carbon innovation corridors, campus-industry transfer streets, data element lobbies, AI living service streets, autonomous shuttle services, AR timeline guides, and AI art interactive installations.

Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The land use plan follows national land use classification standards, forming complete, closed, and seamless用地 zones. Blue-Green Space, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green space plan uses the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park vitality corridor as its backbone, coordinating Qinghe River, Xiaoyue River, surrounding universities, enterprises, and community travel needs. The plan identifies slow-mobility断点, elevated crossing nodes, and landscape nodes at the park's southern and northern ends, proposing strategies for复合利用 of parking, sports, innovation exchange, technology testing, application display, and public services.

Operational Mechanism: City as Repo + Three-Phase Implementation

The "City as Repo" operational concept treats urban space renewal and operation as an open-source repository. Each scenario node, public space, and infrastructure unit can be "forked," "committed," and subject to "pull requests." The three-phase implementation plan covers: Phase 1 (0-1 year, flash activation) with temporary installations, AR guides, autonomous shuttle pilots, and lightweight scenario nodes; Phase 2 (1-3 years, system building) with the three-layer slow-mobility network, edge computing stations, and core area public space upgrades; and Phase 3 (3-5 years, full operation) with complete four-epoch spatial narrative, 12 scenario cards in full operation, and the Global AI Activity Week becoming routine.

Three-Level Scope Framework

The proposal adopts a three-level scope framework: the coordination study scope (covering broader regional context for industry research, regional transport analysis, and urban development pattern assessment), the overall design scope (the 9 km heritage park axis and its 200-500 m flanking hinterland for regulatory-plan-depth urban design), and the key area detailed design scope (three core areas for comprehensive implementation plan depth).
