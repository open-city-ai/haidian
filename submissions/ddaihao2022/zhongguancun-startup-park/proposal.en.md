# Zhongguancun Startup-Park Cluster

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Zhongguancun Startup Parks", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Startup Lounge, Hard-Tech Pilot Park, Roadshow Lawn) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Startup Lounge is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Startup Lounge is the pitch-and-mingle node hosting roadshows, community events and informal exchange. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Hard-Tech Pilot Park focuses on Hard-Tech Pilot Park provides pilot lines, makerspaces and validation facilities for physical-product startups., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Roadshow Lawn integrates public activity and industry services around Roadshow Lawn is an open-air stage and market for demos, recruiting and public showcases.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Zhongguancun Startup Parks public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Zhongguancun Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Zhongguancun Startup Parks, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (SP-01..SP-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Metrics, Recalculation and Compliance

Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, slow-coverage, transit coverage, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Risk, Copyright and Compliance

This package does not claim official approval, finalized regulatory control, land ownership or guaranteed implementation. All assets declare sources and licenses in `sources.json` and `report/copyright_statement.md`. The HTML pages load no remote scripts, tiles, fonts, iframes or APIs.


## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: 22@Barcelona
22@Barcelona has since 2000 transformed former industrial land into a knowledge-intensive innovation district with retained and new public space. For the startup-park cluster it shows old factories can become low-cost incubation and collaboration space.

### Case 2: Hsinchu Science Park (Taiwan)
Hsinchu Science Park, established in 1980, uses a government-led clustered park with shared infrastructure to support hard-tech like semiconductors, a successful model. For the Hard-Tech Pilot Park it argues shared pilot and validation facilities lower entry cost.

### Case 3: Cambridge Science Park (UK)
Founded by Trinity College in 1970 next to the university, Cambridge Science Park pioneered the park model with research-industry proximity, an origin of science parks. For the Startup Lounge it shows campus proximity yields natural talent and transfer flow.

### Case 4: Cambridge Innovation Center (CIC)
CIC provides flexible shared startup offices and meeting space in multiple cities, lowering launch cost via on-demand leasing and building an entrepreneur community. For the Startup Lounge it argues shared offices suit early teams better than isolated buildings.

### Case 5: MaRS Discovery District (Toronto)
MaRS builds on universities and hospitals as a mission-driven urban innovation hub offering incubation, mentorship and capital. For the Zhongguancun startup parks it shows clustering around public institutions anchors a startup ecosystem.

### Case 6: JTC LaunchPad @ one-north (Singapore)
Singapore's LaunchPad at one-north concentrates accelerators, makerspaces and hard-tech labs into a high-density startup community. For the Roadshow Lawn and Hard-Tech Pilot Park it shows high-density collaborative space sparks chance exchange.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older-adult accessibility: gentle paths, seating and large-print signage.
- Disability accessibility: continuous tactile paths, wheelchair-accessible stage and low signage.
- Children and family: market and lawn care areas with safety.
- Low-income groups: public roadshows and markets are free, lowering participation barriers.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
