# Qinghe Waterfront Eco-Restoration Belt

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Qinghe Waterfront Eco-Belt", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Waterfront Wetland Core, Habitat Restoration Belt, Waterfront Activity Park) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Waterfront Wetland Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Waterfront Wetland Core restores a living wetland that anchors biodiversity and public education along the river. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Habitat Restoration Belt focuses on Habitat Restoration Belt reconnects native planting, bird corridors and cleaner water across the riverbank., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Waterfront Activity Park integrates public activity and industry services around Waterfront Activity Park offers boardwalks, nature play and low-impact recreation on the restored shoreline.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Qinghe Waterfront Eco-Belt public-space system.

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Qinghe Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Qinghe Waterfront Eco-Belt, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Cheonggyecheon Restoration (Seoul)
Cheonggyecheon is a Seoul river restoration completed in 2005 that daylighted a buried stream into a continuous waterfront green corridor, improving central microclimate and public life. Its lesson for the waterfront eco-belt is that daylighting and blue-green mixing raise accessibility and vitality.

### Case 2: Bishan-Ang Mo Kio Park (Singapore)
Bishan-Ang Mo Kio Park naturalized a concrete canal into a meandering river under Singapore's ABC Waters programme, combining stormwater management with public recreation. For the Qinghe belt it shows naturalized banks serve both water quality and waterfront use.

### Case 3: Qiaoyuan Wetland Park (Tianjin)
Tianjin Qiaoyuan is a wetland park built on derelict land using low-cost native planting and stormwater wetlands to clean water and restore habitat, a Chinese nature-based-solution example. For the Waterfront Wetland Core it argues for low-cost native self-sustaining wetlands.

### Case 4: HafenCity Waterfront Renewal (Hamburg)
Hamburg HafenCity is among Europe's largest inner-city waterfront renewals, using flood-resilient levels and continuous public waterfront promenades to remake the old port. For the Waterfront Activity Park it shows flood infrastructure layered with everyday public space.

### Case 5: Benthemplein Water Square (Rotterdam)
Rotterdam's Benthemplein Water Square is a climate-adaptive blue facility that serves as a sports and gathering plaza in dry weather and stores storm water in storms, showing dual-use blue-green infrastructure. For the Waterfront Activity Park it argues activity grounds double as storm storage.

### Case 6: Munich Isar Plan
Munich's Isar Plan, from 2011, renatured a concreted river into near-natural gravel banks and meanders, restoring ecology and waterfront recreation. For the Habitat Restoration Belt it shows restoring natural river form lifts biodiversity and flood resilience.

### Sustainability risks
- **Hydrological sustainability**:  restoration depends on continuous level and quality monitoring; without it, degradation follows.
- **Ecological sustainability**:  native planting and bird corridors need long-term care, avoiding post-construction neglect.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older-adult accessibility: gentle boardwalks, seating and non-slip paving.
- Disability accessibility: continuous tactile paths, wheelchair-accessible viewpoints and low signage.
- Children and family: nature-play supervision and shallow-water safety.
- Low-income groups: waterfront public space is free and activities are low-barrier.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
