# Campus Open-Shared District

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Campus Open-Share Street", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Campus Gate Share Core, Open Courtyard Belt, Community Living Room) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Campus Gate Share Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Campus Gate Share Core turns campus edges into shared front doors where university facilities meet the street and rail transfer. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Open Courtyard Belt focuses on Open Courtyard Belt stitches open courtyards into bookable, community-managed spaces along the campus edge., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Community Living Room integrates public activity and industry services around Community Living Room provides a neighborhood hall for co-working, exhibitions and daily social life.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Campus Open-Share Street public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Zhongguancun Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Campus Open-Share Street, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (CS-01..CS-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: MIT Open Space, Kendall Square (Cambridge, USA)
In 2019 MIT opened roughly 0.4 ha of publicly programmed open space at Kendall Square with performances, markets and community events, turning a research edge into a civic living room. Lesson: campus open-sharing should start with edge public space and activity programming rather than one-off wall removal.

### Case 2: University of Copenhagen, Nørre Campus (Denmark)
The University of Copenhagen's Nørre Campus is woven into the city, with ground floors broadly open to citizens (cafes, libraries, walkways), blurring campus and community edges. Lesson: the Open Courtyard Belt can adopt ground-floor public programming so teaching and city life share one interface.

### Case 3: The University of Tokyo, Komaba Campus (Japan)
Komaba Campus opens its museums, botanical garden and public lecture halls to the city, forming stable shared gate nodes. Lesson: the Campus Gate Share Core can organize around bookable exhibition-teaching and open facilities, not merely open passage.

### Case 4: Seoul Metropolitan Government Campus Town Program (South Korea)
Since 2014 Seoul's Campus Town program turns university-adjacent space into mixed nodes for youth startups, co-working and community use. Lesson: the Community Living Room can adopt its campus-municipality co-management model, hosting students, residents and entrepreneurs in one shared venue.

### Case 5: National University of Singapore, University Town (Singapore)
NUS University Town (opened 2011) uses porous open blocks for study, living and public life, with walkways linking campus and neighbouring communities. Lesson: the open-share loop's slow-mobility stitching must keep campus inside and outside continuously reachable, avoiding enclosed courtyards.

### Case 6: University of Melbourne, Parkville Campus (Australia)
Parkville Campus is treated as continuous urban public realm, with trams and public streets crossing the campus and research ground floors open to the city. Lesson: the Campus Gate Share Core should co-locate rail transfer with open ground-floor facilities as an operable urban interface.

- Goal: Verify campus-edge facilities can be booked open to the public.

**CS-02 Courtyard Slow Stitch**
- Goal: Verify continuous, reachable slow mobility inside and outside campus.
- Method: Survey gaps along the Open Courtyard Belt and complete walkways.
- Pass: Reference the slow-mobility layer in roads.geojson with zero gaps.

**CS-03 Living Room Program**
- Goal: Verify the Community Living Room hosts daily co-working and showcase.
- Method: Run monthly co-working and exhibition-teaching events with feedback.
- Pass: Reference the Community Living Room node in public_space.geojson and event logs.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: step-free ramps and rest seating at gates and living rooms.
- Disability: accessible information in booking and wayfinding.
- Children and families: family-friendly facilities in courtyards and living rooms.
- Low-income groups: shared facilities open free or low-cost.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
