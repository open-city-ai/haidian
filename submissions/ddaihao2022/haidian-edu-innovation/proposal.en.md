# Haidian Education-Tech Innovation Corridor

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Edu-Innovation Corridor", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Zhongguancun Edu-Core, Campus双创 Street, Qinghe Talent Lounge) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Zhongguancun Edu-Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Zhongguancun Edu-Core anchors open labs and knowledge exchange between universities and industry. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Campus双创 Street focuses on Campus双创 Street concentrates results transfer, incubators and proof-of-concept space near campuses., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Qinghe Talent Lounge integrates public activity and industry services around Qinghe Talent Lounge hosts talent hostels, matching and community interaction along the green-blue belt.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Edu-Innovation Corridor public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Zhongguancun Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Edu-Innovation Corridor, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (EI-01..EI-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Metrics, Recalculation and Compliance

Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, slow-coverage, transit coverage, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Risk, Copyright and Compliance

This package does not claim official approval, finalized regulatory control, land ownership or guaranteed implementation. All assets declare sources and licenses in `sources.json` and `report/copyright_statement.md`. The HTML pages load no remote scripts, tiles, fonts, iframes or APIs.


## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Stanford Research Park (USA)
Founded by Stanford University in 1951, Stanford Research Park is the origin of university-derived innovation districts, linking campus research with neighbouring firms. Lesson: the Edu-Core should anchor open labs and knowledge sharing while connecting to industry transfer.

### Case 2: Cambridge Science Park (UK)
Founded by Trinity College, Cambridge in 1970, Cambridge Science Park is among Europe's earliest university-linked science parks, forming an R&D cluster around the university. Lesson: Campus双创 Street can adopt its college-owned incubation and proof-of-concept vehicles.

### Case 3: Sophia Antipolis (France)
Planned from 1969 near Nice, Sophia Antipolis is a science city mixing research, living and enterprises. Lesson: the Edu-Innovation Corridor should link diverse innovation nodes with blue-green and slow mobility.

### Case 4: Tsukuba Science City (Japan)
Designated in 1963, Tsukuba Science City concentrates national research institutes and universities, creating dense edu-research talent. Lesson: Qinghe Talent Lounge can adopt its talent concentration and public-service support.

### Case 5: Singapore One-North (Biopolis / Fusionopolis)
Built from 2001, Singapore One-North organizes research, enterprise and talent living through clusters like Biopolis and Fusionopolis. Lesson: the Edu-Innovation Corridor should co-locate R&D, transfer and talent living.

### Case 6: Hsinchu Science Park (Taiwan)
Established in 1980 and driven by ITRI, Hsinchu Science Park formed a densely clustered semiconductor and talent innovation ecosystem. Lesson: Campus双创 Street should provide continuous vehicles from proof-of-concept to industry landing.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: accessible rest and guidance at the talent lounge.
- Disability: accessible information in labs and transfer.
- Children and families: age-appropriate science activities on open days.
- Low-income groups: public activities and facilities open free or low-cost.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
