# AI Senior-Friendly Community

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Senior-Friendly Commons", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Senior Service Center, Barrier-Free Slow Street, Neighborhood Mutual-Aid Yard) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Senior Service Center is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Senior Service Center consolidates day-care, health check and barrier-free services as the care anchor of the community. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Barrier-Free Slow Street focuses on Barrier-Free Slow Street removes level changes and adds rest nodes to make daily walking safe and continuous for older adults., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Neighborhood Mutual-Aid Yard integrates public activity and industry services around Neighborhood Mutual-Aid Yard provides a community kitchen, co-care space and socializing yard co-run by residents.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Senior-Friendly Commons public-space system.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: WHO Global Network for Age-Friendly Cities and Communities
The WHO launched its Global Network for Age-Friendly Cities and Communities in 2010, with an eight-domain framework covering transport, housing and social participation. Lesson: senior-friendliness should be coordinated at city scale across services, space and participation rather than as scattered projects.

### Case 2: Kampong Admiralty, Singapore
Kampong Admiralty, designed by WOHA and completed around 2018, stacks senior public rental housing, a polyclinic, a community plaza and a rooftop farm into one community hub. Lesson: co-locating eldercare, healthcare, social space and greenery in one building shortens older adults' daily travel and improves walkability.

### Case 3: Hogeweyk dementia-friendly village, Netherlands
Hogeweyk in Weesp, Netherlands (2009) offers near-normal living for people with dementia through a freely walkable, familiar neighbourhood scale. Lesson: cognitive accessibility requires continuous, legible, low-wayfinding-confusion slow-mobility and public space.

### Case 4: Mehrgenerationenhaeuser, Germany
Germany's federal Mehrgenerationenhaeuser program, running since 2006, sets up intergenerational activity and mutual-aid spaces in communities. Lesson: intergenerational co-located space fosters neighbourhood mutual aid and reduces social isolation of older adults.

### Case 5: AARP Network of Age-Friendly States and Communities, USA
AARP's Network of Age-Friendly States and Communities, active since 2012, encourages localities to improve transport, housing and services through livability self-assessment. Lesson: measurable self-assessment and community participation drive phased senior-friendly renewal.

### Case 6: Ambition for Ageing, Greater Manchester, UK
Greater Manchester's Ambition for Ageing (2015-2021) used an asset-based approach to grow older-adult-led mutual-aid networks at community level. Lesson: senior services should build on local assets and older adults' agency, not top-down provision alone.

### Test & verification scenarios (>=3)

**TS-01 Barrier-free access test**
- Goal: Verify that a step-free slow street improves continuous access for older adults.
- Method: Remove level changes and add rest nodes, then observe mobility.
- Pass: Reference roads/public_space layers; continuous barrier-free path coverage rises.

**TS-02 Mutual-aid service test**
- Goal: Verify that the Mutual-Aid Yard strengthens social connection for isolated older adults.
- Method: Run community-kitchen and companionship activities at the yard and track participation.
- Pass: Reference key_areas layer; stable mutual-aid groups and participation records formed.

**TS-03 Privacy-friendly sensing test**
- Goal: Verify risk-point sensing works without collecting personal profiles.
- Method: Identify fall or crowding risk points via aggregated statistics only.
- Pass: Reference constraints layer; risk points locatable with no personal data.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: large-type signage, seating, low glare and emergency call.
- Disability: step-free, wheelchair access and accessible toilets.
- Children & family: intergenerational space and escorted-access paths.
- Low-income groups: free or low-cost services and mutual-aid quotas.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
