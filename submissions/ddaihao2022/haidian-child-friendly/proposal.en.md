# Haidian Child-Friendly District

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Child-Friendly Growth Loop", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Children Explore Core, Nature Play Belt, Safe School Route Street) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Children Explore Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Children Explore Core concentrates nature play, exploration and learning in a safe, car-free heart. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Nature Play Belt focuses on Nature Play Belt links adventure trails, sensory gardens and cycle paths along the green-blue line., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Safe School Route Street integrates public activity and industry services around Safe School Route Street calms traffic and protects walking routes to schools.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Child-Friendly Growth Loop public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Suzhoujie Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Child-Friendly Growth Loop, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (CF-01..CF-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Barcelona Superblocks (Spain)
Since 2016 Barcelona's Superblocks narrow through-traffic and return streets to pedestrians, children and play, sharply cutting passing traffic. Lesson: the child-friendly loop can use traffic calming to turn living streets into playable, learnable public space.

### Case 2: Rotterdam Groene Speelplekken (Netherlands)
Rotterdam built a network of green play places using natural materials and rain gardens instead of hard play equipment, encouraging exploratory play. Lesson: the Nature Play Belt can adopt naturalized, low-maintenance play landscapes.

### Case 3: Oslo Car-Free School Streets (Norway)
Oslo closes streets around schools at drop-off and pick-up times, forming car-free school streets that improve walking safety. Lesson: the Safe School Route Street can calm and close by time band to protect children's commutes.

### Case 4: Copenhagen Child-Friendly City (Denmark)
Copenhagen puts children first in planning, expanding pedestrian zones and adding exploratory play spaces. Lesson: the Children Explore Core should organize scale and explorability from a child's perspective.

### Case 5: Vancouver School Streets & Play Streets (Canada)
Vancouver uses timed school streets and neighbourhood play streets to improve children's activity safety and reach. Lesson: child-friendly action can start light, using programming to drive spatial renewal.

### Case 6: Antwerp Speelstraten / Play Streets (Belgium)
Antwerp runs resident-requested temporary street closures for children's play, a normalized community practice. Lesson: school routes and community slow paths can be co-created by residents, asserting public space through activity.

- Goal: Verify car-free, walkable school routes at drop-off and pick-up.

- Goal: Verify the Nature Play Belt is continuously reachable.
- Method: Survey trail gaps along the green line and complete them.
- Pass: Reference the Nature Play Belt layer in green_space.geojson with zero gaps.

**CF-03 Explore Experience**
- Goal: Verify the Children Explore Core hosts exploratory play.
- Method: Run family explore events and collect child feedback.
- Pass: Reference the Children Explore Core node in public_space.geojson and event logs.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: rest seating and shade when accompanying children.
- Disability: accessible facilities in play and school routes.
- Children and families: all-range family-friendly free activities.
- Low-income families: friendly facilities open free or low-cost.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
