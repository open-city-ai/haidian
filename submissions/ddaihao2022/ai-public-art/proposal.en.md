# AI Public-Art & Showcase Network

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "AI Public-Art Showcase Loop", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Art Installation Core, Digital Showcase Belt, Community Co-Create Yard) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Art Installation Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Art Installation Core anchors the network with permanent and rotating sculpture, light and public-art commissions along the heritage corridor. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Digital Showcase Belt focuses on Digital Showcase Belt provides projection-mapped facades, AR stages and open-air screens for scheduled night-time showcases., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Community Co-Create Yard integrates public activity and industry services around Community Co-Create Yard hosts workshops, murals and curated exhibitions where residents and artists co-produce public art.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Vivid Sydney, Australia
Vivid Sydney, held since 2009, activates harbour-front public space with building projection, light art and music programming. Lesson: scheduled night-time showcases can strongly lift waterfront vitality and visitation, but should favour temporary, low-intrusion light installations to avoid lasting disturbance to residents and ecology.

### Case 2: Durham Lumiere, United Kingdom
Durham Lumiere, a biennial UK light-art festival, temporarily transforms the city centre with works shown for only a few days. Lesson: temporary public art animates civic space without permanent infrastructure and builds community identity through participatory curation.

### Case 3: teamLab digital art museums, Tokyo
teamLab Borderless and teamLab Planets in Tokyo are large immersive digital-art museums using projection and sensor interaction to draw major audiences. Lesson: immersive digital art can be an attraction engine, but ticketing, crowd flow and equipment upkeep require a mature operator and a capacity assessment.

### Case 4: The High Line, New York
New York's High Line converted an abandoned elevated railway into a linear park with a standing public-art commissioning program. Lesson: embed art within everyday slow-mobility corridors so that walking, green space and public art compound into a walkable, communicable urban interface.

### Case 5: WATERLICHT and GLOW, Netherlands
Daan Roosegaarde's WATERLICHT and Eindhoven's GLOW festival use artist-led light installations to tell environmental stories about water and the city. Lesson: artist-led participatory light works mobilise the public and convey civic narratives, but must coordinate with urban safety, copyright and night-lighting management.

### Case 6: 798 Art Zone, Beijing
Beijing's 798 Art Zone reused a former electronics factory district into an art district of galleries, public art and an artist community. Lesson: industrial-heritage-to-art-district renewal builds a stable creator community and public-art ecosystem, but needs clear tenure, an operating entity and defined public-space access.

### Test & verification scenarios (>=3)

**TS-01 Night-showcase vitality test**
- Goal: Verify that projection/AR showcases lift night-time vitality.
- Method: Deploy temporary projection and interactive installations along the belt and monitor footfall.
- Pass: Reference roads/public_space layers; night visitation and peak footfall rise with no complaints.

**TS-02 Community co-creation path test**
- Goal: Verify a feasible resident-artist co-creation path.
- Method: Run mural/workshop programs at the Co-Create Yard and collect feedback.
- Pass: Reference key_areas layer; at least displayable works and participation records produced.

**TS-03 Installation upkeep safety test**
- Goal: Verify sustainable installation upkeep and safety.
- Method: Set equipment inspection and content-refresh cadence.
- Pass: Reference constraints layer; inspection closed-loop with no safety hazards.

Three themed landmarks are set along the site, one per key area:

1. Art Installation Core: luminous sculpture and standing light landmark along the heritage line.
2. Digital Showcase Belt: projectable historic facades and open-air theatre.
3. Community Co-Create Yard: workshop and curatorial space renewed from older courtyards.

### Digital honor wall
On the HTML visual page and at annual events, an honor display shows:
- Public-art contribution wall: credits creators, community and volunteers.
- Annual light-season honor board: showcases outstanding showcases and co-creation.
- Residency honors: recognises resident artists and open-source contributions.
- Neighbourhood co-create award: rewards resident-led public-art actions.

> Note: the honor system's scoring algorithm, review and anti-abuse mechanism must be co-designed by multiple stakeholders in Phase 1.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: seating, low-glare lighting and clear wayfinding at showcase areas.
- Disability: step-free paths and accessible/hearing-assisted viewing positions.
- Children & family: family workshops and touch-safe installations.
- Low-income groups: free-access time slots and community co-create quotas.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
