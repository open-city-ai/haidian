# Jing-Zhang Sport & Health Vitality Belt

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Jing-Zhang Sport-Health Axis", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Sport Park Core, Health Slow Belt, Community Fitness Node) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Sport Park Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Sport Park Core is the stadium-and-park node hosting events, active recreation and wellness programs. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Health Slow Belt focuses on Health Slow Belt provides exercise trails, rehab gardens and a non-motorized-priority corridor for daily activity., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Community Fitness Node integrates public activity and industry services around Community Fitness Node offers neighborhood gyms, plazas and bookable sport spaces close to residents.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Queen Elizabeth Olympic Park, London
After the 2012 Games, London converted the Olympic Park into a public sport, green-space and healthy-living legacy park. Lesson: major event venues should continue vitality through public green space and everyday sport uses, avoiding post-event vacancy.

### Case 2: Bogota Ciclovia and Cicloruta, Colombia
Since the 1970s Bogota closes streets every Sunday for walking and cycling (Ciclovia) and built an extensive cycle-path network (Cicloruta). Lesson: periodic car-free streets and continuous cycle networks raise public health and slow-mobility vitality at low cost.

### Case 3: Copenhagen Cycle Superhighways, Denmark
Copenhagen has promoted regional cycle superhighways connecting city and hinterland since around 2012, using commute cycling to boost daily physical activity. Lesson: commute-oriented continuous cycling infrastructure is among the most effective ways to embed sport-health into daily travel.

### Case 4: Cheonggyecheon Stream Restoration, Seoul
Seoul completed the Cheonggyecheon restoration in 2005, replacing an elevated road with a linear blue-green corridor and waterfront activity space. Lesson: blue-green linear space delivers microclimate regulation and daily recreation while linking community fitness nodes.

### Case 5: China 15-Minute Fitness Circle policy
China's national fitness strategy promotes sport facilities usable within a 15-minute walk of communities. Lesson: distributing community fitness nodes by living circle aids daily participation more than concentrated large venues.

### Case 6: The 606 / Bloomingdale Trail, Chicago
Chicago converted an abandoned elevated railway into The 606 linear recreation trail in 2015, offering running, cycling and fitness. Lesson: rail-heritage linear space can become a continuous, accessible wellness corridor compounding with the slow-mobility system.

### Test & verification scenarios (>=3)

**TS-01 Slow-fitness vitality test**
- Goal: Verify that the Health Slow Belt boosts daily fitness activity.
- Method: Provide trails and fitness nodes along the belt and monitor usage.
- Pass: Reference roads/green_space layers; daily use and duration rise.

**TS-02 Community fitness access test**
- Goal: Verify 15-minute accessibility coverage of community fitness nodes.
- Method: Assess node service area via walking isochrones.
- Pass: Reference key_areas/public_space layers; coverage ratio meets target.

**TS-03 Event safety test**
- Goal: Verify evacuation and safety organization for events.
- Method: Run a drill at the Sport Park and check evacuation paths.
- Pass: Reference constraints/public_space layers; evacuation closed-loop with no hazards.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: gentle trails, rest nodes and senior-friendly equipment.
- Disability: accessible tracks and reachable fitness venues.
- Children & family: family sport zones and supervised spaces.
- Low-income groups: free or low-cost open time slots.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
