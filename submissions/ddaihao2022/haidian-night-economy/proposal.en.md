# Haidian Night Economy & 24-Hour Vibrancy District

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "24h Vitality District", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Wudaokou Night-Consumption Core, Zhongguancun Culture Belt, Qinghe Waterfront Night Market) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Wudaokou Night-Consumption Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing A 24-hour consumption node around Wudaokou rail station concentrating late bookstores, midnight dining and performance foyers to anchor the district's night-life identity. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Zhongguancun Culture Belt focuses on An open-air culture corridor linking open theaters, market atriums and a light-trail promenade to stage night-time cultural programming., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Qinghe Waterfront Night Market integrates public activity and industry services around A waterfront night market along the Qinghe riverfront combining stalls, sports fields and night lighting as a family-friendly evening destination.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: London Night-Time Economy & Night Czar
In 2016 the Greater London Authority appointed a dedicated Night Czar to coordinate pubs, venues, transport and safety and to publish a night-time economy strategy. Lesson for this theme: a coordinating body can align late-night formats, extended transit hours and lighting with public safety.

### Case 2: Amsterdam Night Mayor
Since 2012 Amsterdam has elected an industry-led Night Mayor to broker dialogue among government, businesses and residents over noise and neighborhood conflict. Lesson: soft mediation can manage night-time externalities without blanket closures.

### Case 3: Tokyo 24-Hour City Initiative
The Tokyo Metropolitan Government advanced a '24-hour Tokyo' policy to stimulate night tourism, late dining and night-consumption support after an event downturn. Lesson: policy and programming can activate evening hours around universities and rail stations.

### Case 4: Shanghai Night-Economy Demonstration Areas
Since 2019 Shanghai has announced batches of night-economy clusters and 'Night Shanghai' landmarks with extended-hours operation and street-seating rules. Lesson: clusters plus clear operating rules define the boundary and operator of night-open areas.

### Case 5: Seoul Night-Tourism Special Zones
Seoul designated night-tourism special zones, extended late-night metro on core lines and programmed night markets and light events. Lesson: extended transit plus night programming links waterfront and commercial districts into a walkable night loop.

### Case 6: Chengdu Night Economy
Chengdu built a nationally recognized night-consumption brand around Kuanzhai alleys and the Jinjiang night cruise, emphasizing everyday, low-threshold and riverside formats. Lesson: night vibrancy comes from daily, accessible and waterside formats rather than a single large show.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: extended lighting and seating, large-print wayfinding and accessible night paths.
- Persons with disabilities: accessible ramps, tactile paths and captioned/hearing-support events.
- Children & families: safe activity zones and family time-slots by water and green space.
- Low-income groups: retain low-threshold, non-consumption night public activities and open space.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
