# Jing-Zhang Heritage Slow-Culture Loop

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Jing-Zhang Heritage Loop", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Jing-Zhang Heritage Gateway, Slow-Culture Station, Waterfront Showcase Park) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Jing-Zhang Heritage Gateway is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing An entrance node for the century-old Jing-Zhang railway, hosting ruin displays, a visitor center and a cultural forecourt. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Slow-Culture Station focuses on A slow-mobility stop offering bike stations, creative workshops and tea breaks along the heritage loop., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Waterfront Showcase Park integrates public activity and industry services around A green riverside park staging open-air theaters, waterfront markets and heritage displays.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Jing-Zhang Heritage Loop public-space system.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: New York High Line
New York converted a disused elevated freight rail line (built circa 1930s) into a linear elevated park opened in 2009, a benchmark of industrial-heritage reuse. Lesson for this theme: threading continuous slow mobility and public space along linear rail heritage is more sustainable than demolition.

### Case 2: Paris Coulée Verte / Promenade Plantée
Paris transformed a disused rail viaduct into one of the world's first elevated parks in 1993, layering walking above and arched uses below. Lesson: rail viaduct structures can stack slow mobility, culture and commerce.

### Case 3: Seoul Seoullo 7017
Seoul converted a derelict overpass into a pedestrian sky garden in 2017, linking the station and surrounding blocks. Lesson: pedestrian bridges can stitch heritage and community severed by expressways.

### Case 4: Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park (Haidian)
The Jing-Zhang railway, opened in 1909 under Zhan Tianyou, was among China's earliest self-built trunk railways; Haidian is turning the old alignment into a linear heritage park along the same corridor. Lesson: local heritage is the design resource; the slow loop should directly extend the existing heritage-park fabric.

### Case 5: Berlin Park am Gleisdreieck
Berlin turned a disused rail yard into an urban park in 2013, keeping track fabric as a readable historic layer. Lesson: rail-yard open space should preserve legible industrial traces rather than be fully regraded.

### Case 6: Philadelphia Rail Park
Philadelphia is converting a disused rail corridor into a community park, with its first phase opened in 2018, emphasizing neighborhood access and low cost. Lesson: a heritage slow loop can start with low-intervention, phased segments and gradually close the ring.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: gentle slow paths, rest seating and large-print ruin panels.
- Persons with disabilities: accessible ramps, tactile paths and captioned/hearing-support events.
- Children & families: safe activity zones and family tours by water and green space.
- Low-income groups: retain free, open heritage public space and non-consumption activities.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
