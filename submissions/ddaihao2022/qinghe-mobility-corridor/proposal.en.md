# Qinghe Slow-Mobility & Transit Interchange Corridor

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Qinghe Blue-Green Slow Corridor", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Qinghe TOD Core, Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone, Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Qinghe TOD Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Qinghe TOD Core: a station-city hub emphasizing four-quadrant walkability and slow-mobility gap mending. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone focuses on Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone: a broken-link connectivity area with greenways and non-motorized priority., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link integrates public activity and industry services around Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link: a near-campus interchange for community micro-circulation and green travel.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Qinghe Blue-Green Slow Corridor public-space system.

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Qinghe Stn.

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Qinghe Blue-Green Slow Corridor, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Copenhagen Green Cycle Routes & Cycle Superhighways
Copenhagen has built green cycle routes (Gronne Cykelruter) and suburban cycle superhighways (Cykelsuperstier) since around 2010, using continuous, segregated and comfortable networks that support a high cycling commute share, widely seen as a cycling-city model. Lesson: the Qinghe slow-mobility and transit corridor should link rail stations with a continuous, segregated slow network that prioritizes cycling and walking continuity and safety.

### Case 2: Paris Bike Plan (Plan Velo / REV)
Paris launched its Plan Velo around 2019, planning roughly 1,000 km of protected bike lanes and a Réseau Express Vélo (REV) backbone of fast cycling routes, writing slow-mobility priority into street redesign. Lesson: the Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone can borrow the tiered logic of 'fast cycling backbone + block mending' to close gaps and form non-motorized-priority streets.

### Case 3: Bogota Ciclovia & TransMilenio
Since 1974 Bogota closes 120+ km of streets every Sunday for car-free cycling and activities (Ciclovia), and in 2000 opened TransMilenio BRT as a rail-grade interchange backbone. Lesson: the Qinghe corridor can combine transit interchange with periodic car-free slow events to build a slow-mobility culture and interchange habit at low cost.

### Case 4: Seoul Cheonggyecheon Restoration
Seoul dismantled an elevated highway and restored the Cheonggyecheon stream around 2005, creating a waterfront walking and ecological corridor through the city center that revived linear slow mobility and public life. Lesson: the Qinghe blue line should use a blue-green slow composite corridor to link public space, turning the waterfront from a drainage channel into a walkable, sociable urban living room.

### Case 5: Utrecht Centraal Station Redevelopment
Utrecht Centraal is among the busiest rail hubs in the Netherlands; its 2010s Stationsgebied redevelopment reorganized rail, bicycle parking, bus and slow networks within a walkable domain under station-city integration. Lesson: the Qinghe TOD Core should center on four-quadrant walking and bicycle interchange, stitching station entrances and slow gaps within one walkable domain.

### Case 6: New York City Streets Plan & Brooklyn Bridge bike lane
New York City's Streets Plan commits to expanding protected bike lanes and public space, and in 2021 opened a separated Brooklyn Bridge bike lane that converted motor-vehicle space to slow mobility. Lesson: the Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link can use near-campus street redesign with protected non-motorized lanes and a micro-circulation loop to improve slow-mobility safety around campuses.

**TS-01 Slow-gap detection accuracy**
- Goal: AI-detected slow gaps vs field survey achieve overlap >= 90%.
- Method: Item-by-item comparison of AI list vs field survey.
- Pass: Overlap >= 90% and every gap has a layer anchor [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001].

**TS-02 Transit interchange accessibility**
- Goal: Verify Qinghe Stn 800m walking-interchange coverage matches layer computation.
- Method: Compare walk-isochrone vs interchange-coverage layer.
- Pass: Coverage error within tolerance and no personal-trajectory output.

**TS-03 Bilingual figure consistency**

- Method: Item-by-item comparison of 5 bilingual figure labels and metrics.
- Pass: All en labels are valid English; numeric error = 0.

2. Xitucheng Slow-Mend Zone 'Greenway Stitch': continuous tree-lined path with non-motorized-priority paving as a gap-link exemplar.
3. Heqing Rd Neighborhood Link 'Near-Campus Loop': a campus micro-circulation path with interchange signage improving school-trip slow mobility.

On the HTML visual page and at annual events, an honor display shows:
- Qinghe Slow Week annual board: honors outstanding slow-interchange nodes and operations.
- Gap-Mend contributor board: showcases community and volunteer slow-mending work.
- Slow-Safety Award: honors slow-mobility safety and interchange operations.
- Accessibility-Friendly Certification: honors barrier-free and inclusive slow system.

> Note: the honor system's scoring algorithm, review and anti-abuse mechanism must be co-designed by multiple stakeholders in Phase 1.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: continuous level slow paths, rest seating and safe crossings.
- Persons with disabilities: ramps, tactile paving and interchange wayfinding.
- Children & families: safe school-trip slow paths and family activity space.
- Low-income groups: free, accessible slow and interchange facilities.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
