# Haidian Youth-Friendly Third-Space Network

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "YouthPost Third-Space Network", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Wudaokou Youth Hub, Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space, Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Wudaokou Youth Hub is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Wudaokou Youth Hub: a transit-anchored 24-hour learning-and-social node with book-cafe and shared study pods. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space focuses on Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space: a roadshow atrium and open-source workshop linking talent, mentors and enterprises., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt integrates public activity and industry services around Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt: a waterfront night-life strip of markets, sports fields and night lighting.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the YouthPost Third-Space Network public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Wudaokou Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the YouthPost Third-Space Network, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (YT-01..YT-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, slow-coverage, transit coverage, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Helsinki Central Library Oodi
Helsinki Central Library Oodi opened in 2018, funded by the city and designed by ALA Architects, stacking a library, learning workshops, citizen services and maker facilities into three floors of continuous public space, often called the 'living room of the city'. Lesson: the Wudaokou Youth Hub should center on a 24-hour book-cafe and shared study pods, co-locating reading, learning, socializing and public services within a transit-accessible space rather than as pure commerce.

### Case 2: Seoul Seongsu-dong Creative District
Seoul's Seongsu-dong, once a shoemaking and light-industry district, was repurposed from the 2010s by private capital and young entrepreneurs into cafes, design studios, indie brands and co-working spaces, spontaneously forming a youth-culture quarter likened to the 'Brooklyn of Seoul'. Lesson: Haidian's youth third-spaces should reuse university and legacy industrial stock with low-threshold spaces and pop-up mechanisms to invite youth co-creation, avoiding wholesale demolition.

### Case 3: Barcelona Superblocks (superilles)
Barcelona piloted 'superblocks' (superilles) from around 2016, slowing traffic inside grouped blocks to return streets to walking, play and community life; the concept was advanced by urban planner Salvador Rueda and trialed in Poblenou. Lesson: the third-space loop should prioritize slow mobility and community activity, turning streets from through-traffic corridors into places for youth and neighborhood interaction.

### Case 4: Tokyo Innovation Base (TIB)
The Tokyo Metropolitan Government opened the Tokyo Innovation Base (TIB) around 2020 as a public support facility for youth and startups, offering roadshow space, mentoring, co-creation desks and events that connect government, companies and young talent. Lesson: the Zhongguancun Startup Third-Space should be built around a roadshow atrium and open-source workshops, forming an open collaboration platform linking government, universities, enterprises and youth.

### Case 5: Medellin Library Parks (Parques Biblioteca)
Medellin built library parks (Parques Biblioteca) in hillside neighborhoods during the 2000s under 'social urbanism' - e.g. Biblioteca Espana in 2007 - inserting quality public space, learning and culture into communities where disadvantaged youth live. Lesson: the Qinghe Night-Vitality Belt and neighborhood links should bring cultural facilities within reach of youth and low-income groups, using public space to advance social inclusion.

### Case 6: Copenhagen Superkilen
Copenhagen's Superkilen (2012, by BIG, Topotek1 and Superflex) sits in the multi-ethnic Nørrebro district and uses globally-sourced furnishings and paving to organize a linear public space that encourages interaction among youth and residents of different backgrounds. Lesson: youth third-spaces should use recognizable, participatory public art and furniture to spark interaction across a diverse youth community.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: age-friendly night lighting and seating, barrier-free community walking.
- Persons with disabilities: ramps, tactile paving and wayfinding.
- Children & families: family activity space and safe play facilities.
- Low-income groups: keep affordable, free third spaces and activities.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
