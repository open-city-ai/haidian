# Haidian Food & Culture District

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Haidian Food-Culture Belt", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Food Market Core, Time-Honored Street, Waterfront Food Street) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Food Market Core is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing Food Market Core: a flagship public-market and maker space for tasting, retail and food-startup incubation and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Time-Honored Street focuses on Time-Honored Street: a heritage-commercial block rehabilitating Beijing-old-brand food culture with youth formats, organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Waterfront Food Street integrates public activity and industry services around Waterfront Food Street: a riverside dining-and-events belt linking blue-green space with night-time food economy. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Haidian Food-Culture Belt public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Suzhoujie Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Haidian Food-Culture Belt, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (FC-01..FC-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Milan Urban Food Policy & Mercato di Porta Nuova
Milan created a city-level food-policy unit in 2015 and launched the Milan Urban Food Policy Pact, signed by 200+ cities; it also rehabiliated legacy markets (e.g. Porta Nuova) into community food hubs pairing affordability, zero-waste and direct small-producer supply. Lesson: the Haidian Food-Culture Belt should be anchored on a public-market governance coalition of government, universities, time-honored brands and startups, not merely a commercial street.

### Case 2: Copenhagen Torvehallerne & Reffen
Copenhagen organizes local seafood, baking and migrant cuisines into walkable, open-air food clusters (Torvehallerne hall, Reffen container street-food) with strong sustainability and local-supply framing. Lesson: the Food Market Core should keep flexible stalls and pop-ups, inviting university and community co-creation in a 'permanent market + weekend food fair' rhythm.

### Case 3: Tokyo Depachika (department-store food floors)
Tokyo department-store basements (depachika) concentrate premium bento, wagashi and heritage brands on one floor, creating high-intensity daily food retail and gift economy. Lesson: Time-Honored Street can borrow the 'curated display + trust' retail model, turning Haidian's university and research crowds into high-frequency consumers and a gift economy.

### Case 4: Barcelona Municipal Markets Network
Barcelona runs roughly 40 neighborhood municipal markets as a food network blending fresh retail, sociability and public space under unified operating standards and price monitoring. Lesson: the Haidian belt can organize its nodes as a 'community food-node network' under one brand and one food-safety standard, avoiding fragmented management.

### Case 5: Rotterdam Markthal & Fenix Food Cluster
Rotterdam stacks market, housing and public space in the Markthal building and uses old warehouses for the Fenix Food Cluster. Lesson: the Waterfront Food Street can adaptively reuse industrial and riverside fabric, compositing dining, market and showcase into a photogenic, shareable landmark.

### Case 6: Singapore Hawker Centres
Singapore's hawker centres were inscribed as UNESCO intangible heritage in 2020; about 120 centres serve affordable multi-ethnic food under unified government food-safety and cleaning management. Lesson: the Haidian belt must keep an 'affordable, diverse, clean' public-interest floor, bringing time-honored brands and street food under one public food-safety framework.


## Logo & Visual Identity (VI)

### Naming system
The package's overall concept name is **"Haidian Food-Culture Loop"** (abbreviated **FCL**). The name maps directly onto the spatial structure and thematic positioning of this proposal and avoids generic slogans.

### Identity principles
Primary: Food Red `#C0392B` (appetite and Haidian heritage brands); Secondary: Wheat Gold `#E1A95F` (market warmth), Celadon Green `#2E8B7B` (waterfront ecology); Accent: Ink `#3B3A39` (text and base map).

- Typography: Chinese headings in Source Han Sans Bold; English headings in IBM Plex Sans; body in Source Han Sans Regular.
- Applications: A0 board title bar, A3 booklet cover, HTML page header, market wayfinding signage.

### Wayfinding system
1. Tier-1 nodes (Food Market Core entrance): concept name + market map + QR code (links to HTML visual).
2. Tier-2 nodes (Time-Honored Street intersections): heritage-brand index + tasting booking + queue indicator.
3. Tier-3 markers (Waterfront Food Street): food map + accessibility hint + waste-sorting guide.

> Note: the Logo and VI above are conceptual; final vector files require a professional design team grounded in this positioning, and all fonts and images must have cleared-rights sources.

### Test & verification scenarios (>=3)

**TS-01 Time-honored brand recognition accuracy**
- Goal: AI-recognized heritage shops vs manual register achieve overlap >= 90%.
- Method: Item-by-item comparison of AI list vs field register.
- Pass: Overlap >= 90% and every shop has a layer anchor [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001].

**TS-02 Queue wait-time prediction**
- Goal: Peak queue-wait prediction MAPE < 20% at the Food Market Core.
- Method: Compare AI prediction vs field samples (peak/off-peak).
- Pass: MAPE < 20%; no personal-trajectory output.

**TS-03 Bilingual figure consistency**
- Goal: Verify information equivalence of bilingual figures.
- Method: Item-by-item comparison of 5 bilingual figure labels and metrics.
- Pass: All en labels are valid English; numeric error = 0.

Three themed landmarks are set along the site, one per key area:

1. Food Market Core 'Ring of Five Flavors' (ground paving + scent interaction): a ring symbolizing five flavors, showing live queues and recommendations.
2. Time-Honored Street 'Time-Food Wall' (facade wall + archive projection): shows Haidian heritage-brand lineage and craft at the block entrance.
3. Waterfront Food Street 'Food-Boat Pier' (mobile boat-shaped pods): waterfront dining and night-event node.

### Digital honor wall
On the HTML visual page and at annual events, an honor display shows:
- Zero-waste restaurant leaderboard (anonymized)
- Heritage-brand innovation adoption board
- Community food-creator leaderboard
- Partner enterprise and farmer logo wall

> Note: the honor system's scoring algorithm, review and anti-abuse mechanism must be co-designed by multiple stakeholders in Phase 1.

## Cultural Narrative & Communications

### Narrative spine
The package's narrative revolves around its theme:
1. From street food to brand: Haidian's university and heritage food memory upgrades from snacks to a communicable city brand.
2. From consumption to symbiosis: the belt stitches dining, market and blue-green space into an everyday vitality belt.
3. Local-global mutual learning: Haidian learns from and with Milan, Singapore and Barcelona on food-city governance.

- **Funding**:  Phases 1-2 can rely on market rent and event sponsorship; Phase 3 needs diversified revenue (brand, data services).

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: large-type UI + voice guidance at market interfaces; volunteer assistance at offline touchpoints.
- Persons with disabilities: fully accessible food loop (ramps, continuous tactile paving, accessible crossings); navigation prioritizes accessible paths.
- Children & families: Waterfront Food Street provides independent, protected family slow paths and dining zones separated from traffic.
- Low-income groups: all digital services keep offline alternatives (paper guides, hotline) so no one is excluded.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
