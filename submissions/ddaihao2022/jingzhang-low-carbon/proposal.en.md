# Jing-Zhang Low-Carbon Transit Demo Loop

## Design Basis and Source Register

This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. Every design judgment is decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis, and uses the maintainer-registered provisional boundary with explicit precision caveats and metric recalculation.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Zero-Carbon Mobility Loop", uses the blue-green public space and rail stations as the public-space spine, the three key areas (Zero-Carbon Hub, Slow-Priority Loop, Green-Power Station) as innovation anchors, and universities, enterprises, communities and rail stations as the everyday network, forming a spatial organization of "one belt, three cores, multi-point scenes, blue-green slow composite loop".

## Key-Area Detailed Design

The Zero-Carbon Hub is positioned as a themed innovation district emphasizing A zero-carbon transit hub integrating green power, multimodal transfer and a mobility center. and the fusion of AI with urban life. The Slow-Priority Loop focuses on A car-light, tree-shaded loop prioritizing walking and cycling over through-traffic., organizing near-campus and community-linked public space and implementation projects. The Green-Power Station integrates public activity and industry services around A photovoltaic, EV-charging and edge-compute station that powers the loop's low-carbon services.. All three key areas must reach the depth of a planning-implementation scheme.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas

Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering AI-enabled public services, industry services and urban-life scenarios anchored at the three key areas and the Zero-Carbon Mobility Loop public-space system.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal

Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services

The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute, centered on the Qinghe Stn.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape

A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Zero-Carbon Mobility Loop, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing

Six renewal projects (LC-01..LC-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, slow-coverage, transit coverage, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This package benchmarks the most relevant international urban practices for this theme, extracting strategies directly transferable to the site. All cases are from public sources and imply no official partnership or endorsement.

### Case 1: Copenhagen Cycle Superhighways
The Copenhagen metropolitan area has built regional cycle superhighways since 2012, using continuous priority signals and paving to raise commuter cycling. Lesson for this theme: a slow-priority loop should rely on a regional continuous cycling network, not scattered paths.

### Case 2: Shenzhen All-Electric Bus Fleet
Shenzhen replaced its entire bus fleet with battery-electric vehicles around 2017, becoming the first city with a fully electric bus fleet. Lesson: zero-carbon hubs and transfers should prioritize electrification and green-power supply over fossil-fuel shuttles.

### Case 3: Freiburg Vauban Low-Car District
Germany's Freiburg Vauban district, built from the 2000s, is known for car-light living, passive houses and good slow-mobility environment. Lesson: renewal along the slow-priority loop should pair car-light space with low-carbon building standards.

### Case 4: Curitiba Bus Rapid Transit (BRT)
Brazil's Curitiba built a dedicated-lane bus rapid transit system from the 1970s, replacing private-car dependence with high-capacity transit. Lesson: a low-carbon demo loop should cut car-trip intensity via prioritized transit and transfers.

### Case 5: Oslo Car-Free & Zero-Emission Centre
Oslo has advanced car-free living streets and a zero-emission-zone goal in its city centre since 2019, expanding walking and cycling space. Lesson: the slow-priority loop can adopt car-free streets and zero-emission zoning as implementation levers.

### Case 6: China Near-Zero-Carbon Emission Pilot Zones
China launched near-zero-carbon emission pilot zones at the national level around 2014, encouraging renewables and low-carbon transport trials in parks and communities. Lesson: the Jing-Zhang low-carbon loop can align with existing near-zero-carbon methods, specifying green-power coverage and zero-carbon-station indicators.

## Accessibility & Privacy

### Accessibility design
This package follows WCAG 2.1 AA. Concrete measures:
- **Visual**: HTML color contrast >= 4.5:1; figures provide text descriptions (alt text); A3/A0 boards use large type (title >= 24pt, body >= 14pt).
- **Operation**: interactive elements keyboard-accessible; touch targets >= 44x44px; key service forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: key wayfinding is 'conclusion-first' (direction before explanation); Chinese-English terms stay consistent.

### Vulnerable-group care (scenario-specific)
- Older adults: gentle car-free streets, rest seating and large-print charging guidance.
- Persons with disabilities: accessible ramps, tactile paths and hearing-support events.
- Children & families: safe activity zones and family cycling in green and slow areas.
- Low-income groups: retain free, open slow-mobility public space and non-consumption activities.

### Data privacy & security
- **Minimal collection**: only trip/service-relevant aggregates are collected; no personal behavior trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from content; raw data encrypted.
- **Use limitation**: used only to improve the services described here; not sold, not for commercial recommendation, not shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: citizens may view and track their feedback status; may request deletion of personal data (except statutory retention); may demand human review instead of pure automated decisions.
- **Audit**: an independent third party audits data-privacy compliance annually and publishes the report.

> This section is guided by the Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL), the GDPR and the Data Security Law; detailed rules must be finalized with legal professionals in Phase 1.
