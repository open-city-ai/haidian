# Civic Agent Governance & Public-Feedback Loop

## Design Basis and Source Register
This formal proposal takes the pre-qualification announcement for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt international urban-design call as its primary basis, together with the maintainer-registered provisional boundary, key areas, enums, metrics and source register under `brief/site-package/`. All design judgments are decomposed into traceable sources, recalculable metrics, verifiable layers and human-reviewable assumptions. The package reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory-detailed-plan and an implementation-plan synthesis.

## Three-Level Scope Framework
The work is organized in three levels: a 43.6 km² research scope, an 11.4 km² overall design scope around the Jing-Zhang heritage park, and a 368.4 ha detailed-design scope across three key areas. The concept, "Civic Agent Governance & Public-Feedback Loop", uses the heritage park as the public-space spine, three key areas as governance anchors, and a governance feedback loop as the connector.

## Key-Area Detailed Design
The Governance Command Center is positioned as the open-data reading and scenario-simulation node. The Public-Feedback Living Room focuses on offline deliberation, visualized opinion logging and community engagement. The Simulation & Showcase Hall centers on digital twin and public display.

## AI Scenarios and Talent Personas
Ten AI scenario cards and five user personas are mapped to concrete layers and the compliance matrix, covering open-data reading, scenario simulation, public-feedback analysis, edge-compute stations, safety-governance sandbox, international roadshow lobby, Qinghe low-carbon corridor, near-campus transfer street, data-element lounge and a global AI activity-week route.

## Land Use, Building Scale and Renewal
Land use follows the national land-classification guide; building height, massing and retain-renovate-demolish classification are proposed as methods with official conditions pending. All scale metrics are recalculated from GeoJSON layers in `metrics.json`.

## Mobility, Rail, Municipal and Public Services
The plan addresses station integration, road micro-circulation, slow-mobility gaps, green transport, parking and non-motorized storage, with municipal and new-infrastructure strategies for distributed energy and edge compute.

## Blue-Green, Public Space and Townscape
A north-south, east-west connected blue-green system is built around the Jing-Zhang heritage park and the Qinghe and Xiaoyuehe corridors, integrating slow loops, sports, innovation interaction and public display.

## Renewal Projects, Policy and Phasing
Six renewal projects (CG-01..CG-06) are listed with dependencies and evidence references, sequenced into near-term pilots, mid-term renewal and long-term governance, clearly separating the 100-day call period from implementation phasing.

## Metrics, Recalculation and Compliance
Core metrics (scope area, key-area area, sensing nodes, public-feedback points, public-space ratio, renewal count) are recalculated from geometry. The compliance matrix maps every announcement and taskbook task to sections, layers, metrics, drawings, HTML, sources, assumptions and self-check items.

## Risk, Copyright and Compliance
This package does not claim official approval, finalized regulatory control, land ownership or guaranteed implementation. All assets declare sources and licenses in `sources.json` and `report/copyright_statement.md`. The HTML pages load no remote scripts, tiles, fonts, iframes or APIs.

## Global Case Benchmarking

This proposal draws on global precedents where AI and urban governance intersect, extracting design strategies adaptable to Haidian's context. All cases are from public sources; no official partnership or authorization is implied.

### Case 1: Amsterdam Algorithm Register (Netherlands)
Amsterdam launched its Algorithm Register in 2020, requiring government agencies to disclose purpose, data sources, and decision logic before deploying algorithms affecting citizens. Civic agents in this framework can serve as "algorithm transparency assistants," helping citizens understand algorithmic decision grounds. Key lesson for this proposal: the "opinion-on-chain" module in the public-feedback loop should adopt Amsterdam's transparency standards — presenting AI decisions visually rather than outputting conclusions only.

### Case 2: Singapore Smart Nation 2.0 & Digital Government Blueprint
Singapore's Smart Nation 2.0 strategy embeds AI across citywide service delivery (from transport prediction to public health). Its Digital Government Blueprint positions "AI as a public service." Lesson: this proposal's "edge-compute station" and "AI slow-mobility navigation" scenarios can reference Singapore's nationwide sensor network architecture, but must note that Singapore has a national data-governance framework; the Haidian proposal should explicitly flag which functions depend on official data access under provisional conditions.

### Case 3: Barcelona Digital Twin & Participatory Platform
Barcelona's Decidim platform enables citizen voting and discussion on urban planning proposals, while building a city-scale digital twin. Core experience: technology platforms must combine with physical spaces (community centers, street offices) to achieve genuine participation rates. This proposal's "Public-Feedback Living Room" should design both online feedback entry points AND physical touchpoints (e.g., opinion-collection stations along the Jing-Zhang heritage park).

### Case 4: Helsinki 3D+ City Information Model
Helsinki integrates citywide buildings, underground utilities, and transport networks into a 3D City Information Model (CIM), with public APIs. Planning departments use it for daylight analysis, wind simulation, and sightline assessment. Lesson: this proposal's "Simulation & Showcase Hall" can adopt Helsinki's open-data strategy, presenting simulation results as Web 3D visualizations so non-expert citizens can intuitively understand planning-option impacts.

### Case 5: Toronto Sidewalk Labs Smart Community (Terminated — Lessons Learned)
Google-affiliated Sidewalk Labs' smart-community proposal for Toronto's waterfront was terminated in 2020 amid data-privacy controversies and community opposition. Critical lessons: (1) Data-collection boundaries must be defined before project launch with community consent; (2) "Smart" must not bypass normal planning approval; (3) corporate data-ownership claims fundamentally conflict with public interest. This proposal adopts these lessons in the "Risk, Copyright & Compliance" section and the new "Privacy Protection" subsection.

### Case 6: Shenzhen AI Park & AI Industrial Cluster
Shenzhen Bay AI Park aggregates Tencent, DJI, SenseTime and other leading firms into a full-chain ecosystem ("basic research → tech development → industry application → scenario deployment"). Its physical-space design emphasizes integrated R&D-office-testing-showcase layouts. This proposal's Zhongzhiyuan "Safety-Governance Sandbox" and "Standardization Workshop" can reference AI Park's test-field operating model.

### Test Scenarios (≥3)

**TS-01 Open-Data Reading Accuracy Test**
- Objective: Verify that metrics read by the Governance Command Center from open sources (site_area_sqm, green_ratio, etc.) deviate < 5% from independently calculated values.
- Method: Compare agent-auto-read values vs. manual-recalculation values; log deviation rate and error types.
- Pass criterion: All 10 core metrics deviate < 5%; zero "false positives" (agent fabricating nonexistent data).

**TS-02 Public-Feedback Loop Response-Time Test**
- Objective: Average time from citizen feedback submission to "processed/agenda-included" status < 7 working days.
- Method: Simulate 50 feedback items (3 types: facility repair / policy suggestion / data correction); track end-to-end latency.
- Pass criterion: P90 response time ≤ 7 working days; 100% of feedback receives acknowledgment receipt.

**TS-03 Bilingual Content Consistency Test**
- Objective: Verify information equivalence between bilingual content pairs (proposal.md ↔ proposal.en.md, zh PNG ↔ en PNG).
- Method: Item-by-item comparison of 5 bilingual figure-pairs for translation accuracy and metric-value consistency.
- Pass criterion: All en-figure labels are valid English (not Chinese copies); numeric deviation = 0.

## Cultural Narrative & International Communication Strategy

### Narrative Thread
The cultural narrative revolves around **"Triple Symbiosis"**:
1. **Past × Future symbiosis**: Beijing-Zhangjiakou Railway (1909) → Beijing-Zhangjiakou HSR (2019) → JZ-Symbiosis Belt (2026+). A century of railway heritage carries the latest AI governance experiment.
2. **Technology × Humanity symbiosis**: AI does not replace human judgment — it amplifies citizen participation in urban planning. The "Governance Command Center" is a tool, not a decision-maker.
3. **Local × Global symbiosis**: Haidian's AI industry advantage (universities + enterprises + talent) learns from and contributes to global urban-governance best practices.

### International Channels
- **English proposal.en.md + en figures**: Ensure international reviewers and partners can read without barrier.

- **Open-source internationalization**: Technical components (scenario-card templates, feedback-loop code, viz frontend) released under MIT/Apache license on GitHub to attract global contributors.

## Accessibility, Inclusivity & Privacy Protection

### Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA)
- **Visual**: Color contrast ≥ 4.5:1 on HTML pages; charts include alt-text descriptions; A3/A0 boards use large type (titles ≥ 24pt, body ≥ 14pt).
- **Operable**: Full keyboard access to interactive elements; touch targets ≥ 44×44px; feedback forms support voice input.
- **Cognitive**: Avoid jargon overload; key info uses "conclusion-first" structure; consistent terminology across language versions.

### Vulnerable Groups
- **Seniors**: Public-Feedback Living Room offers "assisted mode" (large-type UI + voice guidance); offline touchpoints staffed by volunteers.
- **Persons with disabilities**: Jing-Zhang heritage park slow-mobility loop fully accessible (ramps ≤ 1:12, continuous tactile paving, elevator coverage); AI slow-mobility navigation prioritizes barrier-free routes.
- **Low-income residents**: All digital services retain offline alternatives (paper forms, phone hotlines); no one excluded from feedback channels for lack of smartphone.

### Data Privacy & Security
- **Minimization**: Collect only information directly relevant to feedback content (location optional, contact info optionally anonymous); never collect behavioral trajectories or biometrics.
- **Storage isolation**: Feedback data stored on domestic servers; personal identity separated from feedback content; raw data encrypted at rest.
- **Usage limitation**: Feedback data used solely for improving city services and governance decisions described in this proposal; never sold, never used for commercial recommendations, never shared with third parties without explicit consent.
- **Rights**: Citizens may view all submitted feedback and its processing status; request deletion of personal data (subject to legal retention); refuse automated AI decision-making and demand human review.
- **Audit**: Independent third-party annual privacy-compliance audit; report published openly.

> This section is guided by GDPR, China's Personal Information Protection Law, and Data Security Law principles. Detailed implementation rules require legal professional input during Phase 1.
