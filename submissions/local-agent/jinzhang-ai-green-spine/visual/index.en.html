Visitor experience from arrival to return

Understand Jingzhang before seeing AI; make the city usable before adding intelligence. A visitor can complete a meaningful trip without downloading an app, creating an account, or using facial recognition, with a story, rest, human help, and a clear way to leave early.

01 / What is worth visiting?

The highlight is not one masterplan image; it is four experiences that can actually be walked.

Railway memory

A physical timeline, tactile fragments, seating, and a human explanation make the Centennial Jingzhang story the beginning of the trip.

Everyday green spine

Shade, water, food, repair, cash-compatible basic service, and clear physical signs turn landscape into a useful daily street.

AI as public service

The AI Origin Open Courtyard uses a flipbook, paper decision tree, and human conversation to explain how AI serves people.

Observable testing

The Zhongzhiyuan Test Harbor shows robot yielding, service interoperability, and offline degradation with a safety line, stop procedure, and result board.

Routes, nodes, and distances are conceptual targets derived from the provisional boundary. They are not official redlines, surveyed walking measurements, or committed facilities.

04 / What if it rains, someone is tired, or the system fails?

Convenience is not “there is an app? it is that the experience still works when a service fails.

Rain

  • Welcome Desk issues a short-route card
  • Use the covered Welcome Desk–AI Origin sequence
  • Keep the same paper route color

Heat / fatigue

  • Prioritize shade, water, and a short route
  • Make turn-back points visible
  • Leaving early is not failure

Lost child / item

  • Move to Human Takeover
  • Use a numbered paper log
  • No profile or digital check-out

AI / device failure

  • Switch to fixed signs
  • Switch to paper boards
  • Staff explain and stop the test
The next co-test records wayfinding time, missed nodes, time to first toilet/water, continuation after rest, human takeover success, understanding of weather branches, and whether visitors can distinguish real facilities, tests, and pending items.
