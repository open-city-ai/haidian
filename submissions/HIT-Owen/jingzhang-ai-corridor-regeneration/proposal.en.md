# Jing-Zhang Signal Corridor (THE SIGNAL CORRIDOR): Requiring AI Services Traversing Public Space to Signal Their Status

> The Jing-Zhang Railway operated safely for a century, not by virtue of a single locomotive that never broke down, but through a signal system that everyone could understand, where boundary violations were recorded, and where a red light meant an immediate stop.
>
> Today, AI services are becoming the new "trains" traversing urban public space, and most of them have no signal lights: once launched, they remain permanently active; errors are quietly patched; there is no red light to display, and no one knows where the signal lights are. **This proposal translates the signal discipline of the railway into a spatial institution for AI governance**—every AI service entering the corridor must "signal": publicly disclosing what it does, what data it uses, who is responsible, and when it can be stopped; signals can be seen, questioned, and halted by any resident.
>
> AI serving the city does not need to be proven perpetually correct; it needs to be proven to be **signaling at all times**.

The "one belt" here is not a new redline, but a translation of the three-level scope from the announcement into an operable signal governance method; the "three stations" correspond to the three key areas; the "twelve points" embed the rights of visibility and appeal into the daily pedestrian scale.

## The One-Belt Overall Concept: Jing-Zhang Signal Corridor [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK] [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK]

### Unified Naming and Brand Identity

This proposal unifies the Chinese and English nomenclature: "Jing-Zhang Signal Corridor / 京张信号带." "Jing-Zhang" inherits the Jing-Zhang Railway (京张铁路) constructed in 1909 under the direction of Zhan Tianyou—this railway was the first to safely operate trains over complex terrain using a signal system developed by Chinese engineers; "Signal" draws on the dual meaning of railway signal lights—both a physical device for traffic control and a governance grammar that "makes behavior visible and accountability traceable." Each AI service entering public space holds a "signal card": service identifier, functional scope, data sources, responsible party, stop conditions, and expiration date—publicly readable, questionable, and haltable. The naming system includes three sub-brands: Benchmark Signal Station (Zhongzhi Park · Benchmark Signal Station), Origin Signal Station (AI Origin Community · Origin Signal Station), and Public Signal Station (Dazhong Temple · Public Signal Station), forming a two-tier identity structure of a master brand plus three station sub-brands.

### Logo Design Direction (Conceptual Suggestion)

The logo takes the railway "semaphore" (臂板信号机) as its motif: a horizontal arm means "stop," a lowered arm means "proceed." The proposal abstracts this into a concise geometry of "one vertical, one diagonal"—the vertical line as the signal mast, the diagonal line as the arm angle, and the solid circle at the arm's endpoint representing the human intervention point. This symbolic authority extends: each of the three signal stations adopts a different angular position of the arm, forming isomorphic-yet-divergent sub-identities. Colors draw from the Jing-Zhang signal palette: signal red (#B5443A, used only for stop, downgrade, and human intervention states, strictly prohibited for decorative use), punctuality teal (#1F7A72, marking in-period compliant services), historic brick red (drawn from the red brick hue of the old Qinghuayuan Station (清华园火车站) building, pending field sampling), technology deep blue, and warm gray for auxiliary layers. The logo can be reduced to a monochrome version for seals, signage plaques, and black-and-white printing; it has both horizontal and vertical formats. All fonts must use open-source licenses.

### Color and Typography System Direction (Reference Proposal)

The primary color palette recommends three colors: historic brick red (drawn from the red brick wall hue of the old Qinghuayuan Station building, final color values pending field sampling confirmation), innovation teal-green (drawn from the Qinghe River (清河)–Xiaoyue River (小月河) water system and Haidian (海淀) green space system hues), and technology deep blue (drawn from the Zhongguancun (中关村) enterprise cluster and nighttime computing facility lighting hues). Auxiliary colors are warm gray and neutral gray, used for signage backboards and information hierarchy differentiation. The typography system recommends Source Han Sans (思源黑体) (or equivalent open-source sans-serif Chinese typeface) for Chinese, Inter or IBM Plex Sans for English, and monospace fonts such as JetBrains Mono for data and code, ensuring visual consistency in mixed Chinese-English typesetting, numeric labeling, and code display. All fonts must be open-source or licensed; uncleared commercial fonts must not be used.

### Three-Zone, Two-Wing Signal Coordination Loop (Conceptual Suggestion)

The "three zones" are the three functionally non-interchangeable signal stations: Benchmark Signal Station (Zhongzhi Park · signal standard formulation and safety baseline maintenance), Origin Signal Station (AI Origin Community · service entry registration and open-source verification), and Public Signal Station (Dazhong Temple · status disclosure and delay publication). The "two wings" are functional extensions: **Zhongguancun Technology Service Wing** (standards, intellectual property, investment and financing, and legal resources along Zhongguancun Avenue (中关村大街), near the Wudaokou (五道口)–Zhongguancun Station (中关村站) axis), and **Xiaoyue River Scenario Wing** (community, education, healthcare, consumption scenario applications and resident feedback along the Xiaoyue River). The three zones and two wings form a closed loop of "signal → verify → broadcast → feedback correction → benchmark update": the benchmark station establishes signal standards → the origin station organizes service entry and open-source verification → the public station broadcasts operational status and delay records → the Xiaoyue River scenario wing collects scenario feedback and resident appeals → feedback flows back to the benchmark station for standard updates. This coordination loop is a conceptual suggestion; its visualization drawings are saved in the `report/` directory and do not constitute statutory spatial control.

### Regional Synergy Linkage (Conceptual Suggestion)

The Jing-Zhang Signal Corridor links with the broader Beijing science and technology innovation landscape at a larger scale:

- **Beiwei Community (北纬社区)**: Science and technology innovation communities along the North Fifth Ring Road (北五环) to North Qing Road (北清路), serving as an extension hinterland for talent living and industrial services; recommended to connect northward via the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park slow-mobility ring.
- **Future Science City (未来科学城, Changping (昌平))**: One of Beijing's "Three Cities and One Zone" for the international science and technology innovation center, focusing on energy and frontier technology; recommended to form a "basic research–applied transformation" dual-axis linkage with the Jing-Zhang Belt via Rail Line 13/Changping Line.
- **Huairou Science City (怀柔科学城)**: Focusing on large-scale scientific facilities and basic research, forming a complementary "basic science–AI application" relationship with Haidian university sourcing, linked through academic exchange, data sharing, and joint laboratories.
- **Economic Development Zone (经开区, Yizhuang (亦庄))**: Focusing on high-precision industry and intelligent manufacturing, forming an "algorithm + manufacturing" chain with the Jing-Zhang Belt, through mutual standard recognition, joint scenario development, and two-way enterprise establishment.
- **Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei (京津冀)**: Coordinating Tianjin computing power and Hebei data annotation and content review industries to form a regional division of "Haidian algorithm sourcing–Tianjin-Hebei computing and data support," based on the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei coordinated development framework.

The above regional synergies are all conceptual suggestions, requiring confirmation by relevant parties in formal planning, and do not constitute confirmed government implementation arrangements.

### Signal Card: The Public Signal for Every AI Service

The core governance tool of the Signal Corridor is the "signal card"—every AI service entering the corridor's public space must hold one. The signal card contains six mandatory fields: (1) service identifier and functional scope (what it does), (2) minimum-necessary data and sources (what data it uses), (3) responsible party (who is responsible), (4) stop conditions and expiration date (when it can be stopped), (5) non-AI equivalent pathway (human fallback), (6) signal broadcast location (at which signal point it is publicized). After registration at the Origin Signal Station, services receive a maximum 180-day operational window; upon expiration without signal verification, the service automatically downgrades to a non-AI equivalent pathway—stopping is the default outcome, and renewal requires evidence. Delay records enter the Public Signal Station's public archive, append-only. Any resident may initiate a review at the nearest signal point.

Urban AI agents can assist in identifying slow-mobility breakpoints, public space heat maps, facility maintenance, and activity safety risks, but cannot replace planning approval, cannot output unauthorized personal profiles, and cannot claim official implementation commitments. All AI scenario data must comply with the principles of minimum necessity, public sourcing, explainability, and human review, entering structured layers and the compliance matrix [standard:GENERATIVE-AI-INTERIM-MEASURES] [standard:BARRIER-FREE-ENVIRONMENT-LAW]. Each scenario simultaneously registers three pieces of information: "who is operating, what are the data boundaries, who can halt it," and publishes them at signal points. No facial-recognition-driven public management scenarios; no describing immature technologies as comprehensively deployable.

### Cultural Narrative: Three-Layer Overlay

The cultural narrative of the Jing-Zhang Signal Corridor consists of a three-layer overlay, forming a walkable cultural sequence along the public space slow-mobility system:

1. **Jing-Zhang Railway Historical Narrative Layer (1909—)**: Starting from the Jing-Zhang Railway constructed under the direction of Zhan Tianyou, focusing on the Qinglongqiao (青龙桥) "人"-shaped switchback engineering achievement, the historic Qinghuayuan Station (清华园火车站) building, and the milestone significance of the Jing-Zhang Railway as China's first self-built railway.
