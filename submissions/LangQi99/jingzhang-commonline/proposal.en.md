# Jing-Zhang Co-create COMMONLINE A learnable, verifiable, and collaboratively built AI city public businness (Note: "businness" appears to be a typo and should likely be "businness" or "businesse" if intended to be a specific term, or corrected to "busines" if meant to be "business")

## Design Basis and Source List

This plan adopts a six-step closed loop of "evidence—population—network—space—indicators—governance." The first step only accepts traceable evidence: official announcements define the scope of comprehensive research, overall design, and the tasks for three key areas; the warehouse task book supplements the scenarios, brands, operations, and open-source requirements gathered from intelligent body submissions; temporary geometry only generates and verifies constraints. The second step starts from the daily activities of researchers, entrepreneurs, engineers, residents, students, visitors, and urban operators, without deriving the city from a technical checklist. The third step integrates innovation chains, pedestrian chains, Public Space chains, and data governance chains; the fourth step generates alternative land uses, buildings, roads, green spaces, public spaces, and phased developments; the fifth step recalculates the indicators from geometry; the sixth step manages implementation with Human Review, evaluation thresholds, and exit mechanisms. ## Three-Level Scope Framework

Integrate and study the layer to address the innovative ecology and regional collaboration within approximately 43.6 square kilometers without drawing new control lines; at the overall design layer, form a spatial system within about 11.4 square kilometers of temporary constraints consisting of "one public backbone, three innovation loops, five types of urban laboratories, and twelve scenario stations"; at the key design layer, take Zhongzhiyuan, AI Origin community, and Dazhongsi as root technologies, technology transfer, and urban application validation samples. The three layers are not in a scaling relationship, but rather a feedback relationship of "strategic assumptions—spatial prototypes—local falsification": if the key areas cannot meet the conditions for walking, public services, operations, and safety, the overall structure must be retreated; if the overall structure cannot support the industrial chain loop, the regional strategy must be adjusted. [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

COMMONLINE does not interpret Jing-Zhang as a single landscape axis, but rather transforms Public Space into an innovative infrastructure. The main line carries continuous pedestrian access, greenery, cultural narratives, public services, and low-risk testing; three loops are designated for the northern segment "Root Technology—Toolchain—Safety Governance," the middle segment "Campus Achievements—Open Source Collaboration—Entrepreneurship Incubation—Talent Living," and the southern segment "Intelligent Bodies—Endpoints—Content—Consumption—Public Experience." Five types of urban laboratories are Open Stack Labs, City Sandboxes, Talent Commons, Heritage Interfaces, and Operation Observation Stations. The geometric indexing for three layers of scope is found at [data:geometry/site_boundary.geojson#SITE-001], [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002], and [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003], with tasks mapped into `compliance_matrix.json`. All layers will be recalculated after replacing the formal boundaries.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The judgment of the area strategy is not about "reinventing a science park," but about repairing three types of fractures: the lack of a low-cost interface between research outcomes and corporate experimentation, the absence of a shared living room between intense professional work and daily life, and the gap between the expansion of AI capabilities and the visible governance process. To address these, we propose the "Root-to-Stack, Idea-to-Impact, Model-to-Life" three-loop strategy: the northern segment focuses on gathering foundational models, toolchains, evaluation, and safety governance; the middle segment connects university outcomes, open-source communities, legal intellectual property, incubation, and talent services into a walkable network; the southern segment makes intelligent agents, terminals, content, and business services visible, subject to questioning, and subject to review in the real urban interface. The eastern wing envisions a low-risk urban sandbox facing Xiaoyuehe, while the western wing accommodates international talent, intellectual property, capital, and professional services; both wings are directions for concept collaboration, not new boundaries. [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK] [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

Brand name "Gongzhi Jing-Zhang" emphasizes collective wisdom rather than one-way intelligence; the English name COMMONLINE refers to public lines, common bottom lines, and open protocols. The logo prototype is formed by an unbroken line in the shape of a "person," railway switch, and neural node. The colors use deep Jing-Zhang blue, open cyan, and signal orange, without using any third-party trademarks. Three conceptual AI pilgrimage sites are located at the northern segment "Open Stack Tower," the middle segment "Origin Release Stairway," and the southern segment "Model City Archway": the landmarks derive their significance from public activities and knowledge contributions, without competing. The global case studies share the common insight of organizing institutions, spaces, and services rather than replicating architectural forms; the number of case studies is recorded by [metric:ecosystem_case_count], and the number of landmarks is recorded by [metric:landmark_count].

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The overall space is structured around a central composite public spine, with two wings forming six functional segments: the southern segment focuses on talent living and smart economy, the middle segment on near-school collaboration and urban services, and the northern segment on root technology research and transformation services. The central space is expressed by the park green code but is explicitly only a design suggestion; the wings' parcels are only defined by their functional interfaces, without specifying unverified Floor Area Ratios and heights. The land polygons fully cover the Provisional Boundary and do not overlap, with the zoning seams executed with zero overlap topology under the same projection coordinate system. The Building Footprints avoid the blue-green ridge, and the east-west stitching corridors reconnect the wings to the main line. The layer evidence includes [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001], [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001], and [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001], with depth controlled by [depth:land_use_layout], [depth:development_intensity_controls], and [depth:height_massing_character].

Update the design to follow the principle of "first the interface, then the mass; first reuse, then increment; first monitor, then expand." Prioritize the first floor for shared meeting rooms, exhibitions, dining, community services, learning, and enterprise services, forming a time composite that spans workdays and weekends; the second floor and above will be determined based on the current structure, ownership, and control plan for research and development, office, residential, or experimental functions. The 18 Building Footprints are mass interface prototypes, not corresponding to specific addresses or demolition targets. The road layer expresses a pedestrian mainline, two parallel cycling/greenway lines, and seven east-west stitching channels, without replacing the red line or engineering traffic organization. The Floor Area Ratio, total scale, and road land area remain unknown to avoid creating a sense of precision in the absence of sufficient data. [standard:MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING]

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

Zhongzhiyuan is positioned as the "Root-to-Stack Park": with open stack tower platforms serving as knowledge display and governance interfaces, envisioning low-disturbance blue-green edge experiments along the Qinghe direction, and forming an internal loop of basic research—toolchain—evaluation—standard—display. Prioritize renovation of shared first floors and idle spaces, setting up model safety red team sandbox, open-source toolchain workshop, and low-carbon edge computing stations; any outdoor testing to be conducted by appointment, geofencing, manual observation, and one-click shutdown. Riverfront, flood control, ecological, and external transportation conditions to be finalized only after professional verification. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001]

The Beijing AI Origin Community is positioned as the "Idea-to-Impact Origin Conversion Street": it connects campus achievements, open-source communities, incubators, intellectual property, investment and financing, and talent living through the original point phase. The architectural renewal emphasizes small units, low thresholds, short-term rentals, and nighttime safety. Shared learning stations, enterprise service Copilot, and achievement release halls are organized around a five-minute walkable public interface. Dazhongsi is positioned as the "Model-to-Life Model Entry Living Room": it arranges a queryable display of intelligent bodies, terminals, content, and public services around the rail connection and four-quadrant walkable seams. The model entry corridor is both a guide and a responsibility disclosure interface. The two areas are detailed in [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002] and [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003]. The three detailed designs adhere to the principles of preservation and modification prioritization, public interest prioritization, and governance visibility, as reviewed by [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design].

## AI Innovation Ecosystem, Personas, and AI+ Scenarios

Seven categories of personas are not population labels but task profiles: Research leaders require quiet spaces for focused study and scheduled validation; open-source developers need collaboration, release channels, and recognition for contributions; startup teams need low-cost spaces, access to computing resources, legal support, and their first customers; product engineers need realistic but controllable testing environments; residents and caregivers need barrier-free access, quiet periods, and public services with human assistance options; students and young people need learning opportunities, safe spaces for physical activities, and nighttime safety; international visitors and urban operators need multilingual guidance and auditable interfaces. These personas are used only for spatial programming and do not establish individual tracking profiles. [metric:persona_count]

Twelve scene stations are located at [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#SCENE-S01] to [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#SCENE-S12]: intelligent barrier-free navigation, low-speed robot co-road, Dazhongsi model entry, dynamic curb-line review, community public service navigation, Jing-Zhang cultural narrative, origin open-source release, talent shared learning, model safety red team, low-carbon edge-side computing, collective wisdom root stack collaboration, and urban operation observation. [metric:scenario_node_count] at least three categories belong to industrial testing: robot co-road adopts speed limits, geographic fencing, and on-site safety officers; model red team only uses authorized test sets and retains audit records; edge-side computing trials are subject to energy limits, thermal risk, and privacy isolation as entry conditions. Health, legal, safety, and other high-impact outputs can only provide navigation and alerts, with final decisions made by qualified personnel. Governance adopts the "minimum data—clear purpose—limited retention—Human Review—public appeal—voluntary exit" six gates.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

Land use structure is expressed according to the open call subset: research and development, education, commercial services, residential, community services, and park green spaces are mixed around a public spine; areas are recalculated from seven non-overlapping polygonal regions, but these zones are not determinants of land use nature. Building prototypes are divided into research and development experiments, incubation offices, mixed services, talent residences, community services, cultural displays, and connection facilities, all located outside the blue-green spine. Currently, only the conceptual base [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm] is calculated, with total building scale and Floor Area Ratio remaining unknown. Land use standards are based on [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE], with layer entries at [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-007] and [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001].

The Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy employs a four-tier evidence gate: Tier 1 verifies the building's year of construction, structural safety, ownership, and legality; Tier 2 evaluates historical and cultural significance, industrial memory, carbon emissions, and community use value; Tier 3 compares the full lifecycle costs of in-situ restoration, functional replacement, addition, and new construction; Tier 4 determines through public engagement and professional review. Before completing the four-tier evidence, no designation of "demolition" is made. This plan expresses 18 individual volume interfaces using `retain_and_activate`, `renovate_first`, and `infill_after_survey`, but does not map to specific buildings. Roofs, ground floors, street corners, and corridors are key areas for controlling publicness, with height, setback, fire safety distances, and parking awaiting formal conditions. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish] [depth:height_massing_character]

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

Traffic adopts a "1+2+7" slow-moving framework: one continuous pedestrian mainline, two parallel cycling/greenway lines, and seven east-west stitching channels. Priority is given to connecting tracks and key areas at three south, middle, and north connection nodes. The remaining channels serve the daily passage of communities, campuses, and enterprises. Evaluation does not focus on the number of roads but on continuous shadows, barrier-free slopes, crosswalk waiting, nighttime lighting, conflict points, and distances for human assistance. Robots only enter designated low-speed windows, dynamic curbs only use aggregated occupancy data, and retain human traffic management authority. The conceptual network length is recorded in [metric:road_centerline_length_m], spatial entrances are at [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001], and professional depth is managed by [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking].

Integrate Urban and New Infrastructure with "Subtle Integration": End-side computational power, perception, charging and swapping stations, rain and flood monitoring, lighting, and public Wi-Fi are shared within maintenance corridors and energy budgets. Public service nodes provide navigation for healthcare, education, legal services, and life services, but do not replace professional services. Each device must have a responsible party, a data list, a retention period, a maintenance window, and an end-of-life plan. Road right-of-way, underground utilities, power and distribution, thermal environment, fire safety, flood protection, and rail protection conditions are still lacking, so [data:geometry/constraints.geojson#CONSTRAINTS] remain an empty constraint set rather than fabricating control lines, to be overlaid once the data is available. The depth of the facilities is managed by [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure].

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green system translates the 112-meter conceptual buffer zone into a continuous learning landscape: shaded walkways, bike paths, rain gardens, seasonal lawns, low-intervention perception, and twelve shared public nodes, all under the same maintenance logic; the width is merely the current geometric generation parameter, not a statutory green line. Public Space is expressed through a narrower continuous living room and node squares, ensuring that innovative activities do not consume the ecological base. Green space area and ratio are [metric:green_space_area_sqm] and [metric:green_ratio], while public space area and ratio are [metric:public_space_area_sqm] and [metric:public_space_ratio]; the source layers are [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-001] and [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001]. [depth:blue_green_public_space]

The aesthetic follows "old trajectories, new interfaces, low energy consumption." Old trajectories are perceived through continuous lines, timber-scale paving, and narrative history nodes, without replicating historical elements; new interfaces integrate model descriptions, contributors, data boundaries, and human stewards directly into spatial information. Buildings feature adaptable skeletons, deep eaves, operable windows, and shared ground floors to reduce energy consumption. A color system uses deep blue as a base, open green to identify public and open-source spaces, and signal orange to mark experimental boundaries and human intervention. Public art prioritizes co-creation and copyright documentation by inviting community, research, and design teams. The overall aesthetic is constrained by [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES], with any heritage conclusions remaining open until verified in the official register and protected areas.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

The project library consists of ten decomposable actions: slow travel breakpoint audit, continuous tree shade pilot, origin release courtyard steps, model safety sandbox, community service navigation, Dazhongsi quadrants walking seam, co-intelligence ground floor update, low-carbon edge-side computing station, cultural narrative station, and urban operation observation station. Each must include location, public value, responsible party, data list, spatial dependency, budget level, trial period, exit conditions, and post-mortem conclusions. Policy-wise, it is recommended to establish "scene passes": low-risk, reversible, and data-minimized scenarios can first be piloted with a time limit; high-impact scenarios must complete professional reviews, public communications, and independent safety assessments. [depth:renewal_project_list]

Spatial phasing is not a commitment to specific years but rather a maturity gate. Phase one covers the southern segment with learning and lightweight pilots, with an area of [metric:phase_1_area_sqm]; it progresses to a networked update in phase two when accessibility, pedestrian conflicts, privacy, and operational metrics are met, covering an area of [metric:phase_2_area_sqm]; phase three addresses the northern segment with foundational technologies, standard collaboration, and regional network access, covering an area of [metric:phase_3_area_sqm]. The boundaries of the three phases fully cover the temporary overall scope, as shown in [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-001], which will be replaced by formal boundaries for recalculations. Hard conditions for progressing to the next phase include formal boundary verification, property rights validation, traffic and municipal feasibility, clear responsibility entities and funding sources, and the completion of the public assessment of the previous phase. ## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

The greatest risk is not in having insufficient detail in the drawings, but in treating temporary geometry as formal conditions. Formal overall boundaries, key area polygons, control plans, ownership, existing buildings, road rights-of-way, rail protection, municipal pipelines, flood control, fire safety, public services, and cultural heritage data are missing, leading to the need for Human Review of placement, area, intensity, Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy, and implementation subject. The second category of risk comes from AI: bias, hallucination, over-collection, automatic decision-making, system failure, and unclear responsibility; corresponding measures include limiting purposes, using public or authorized data, shortest retention, human review, significant disclosure, appeals, and opt-out. The third category of risk is the substitution of operational popularity for long-term public value; therefore, each pilot must assess accessibility, equity, nuisance, maintenance, energy, and opportunity costs. [depth:risk_missing_data] [standard:MOHURD-ARCH-DESIGN-DEPTH-2016]
