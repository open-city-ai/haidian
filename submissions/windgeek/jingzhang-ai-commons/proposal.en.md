# Jing-Zhang Smart Chain: One Spine Three Courtyards·Dual Wings Ten Landscapes

**JINGZHANG AI COMMONS — Centennial Track Spine, Intelligent Public Domain.** This scheme conceptualizes the "AI Innovation Belt" as a suite of public value infrastructure, rather than affixing a smart label to traditional parks. The track heritage provides a sense of continuity over time, open spaces facilitate human encounters, and trustworthy AI offers learnable urban services; collectively, they form a "city public model" that can be continuously calibrated by citizens, developers, businesses, schools, and professional teams. All spatial content is presented as Conceptual Recommendations, reference schemes, or topics for further research by professional teams, and does not replace formal planning, nor constitute a government endorsement, investment, or implementation commitment.

**A Single Sentence to Decide the Outcome: Transform the Jing-Zhang Railway from "a Centennial Heritage to Be Viewed" into "an Open Interface for Training Future Cities."** The uniqueness of the proposal does not lie in adding more screens, but in establishing three chains that can be closed loops, verifiable, and terminable: the spatial chain stitches fragmented campuses into continuous public domains, the innovation chain takes academic papers and models into real but controlled validation fields, and the governance chain embeds every use, challenge, human intervention, and exit into a cycle of urban learning. These three chains together form the "Urban Model Commons": cities contribute problems, scenarios, and rules, developers contribute models and tools, citizens contribute choices and feedback, operators bear human responsibility, and reviewers retain the right of veto.

### Five memorable answers are on the review table.

1. **Why Jing-Zhang?** Railway heritage is inherently a form of infrastructure that connects knowledge, industry, and people. Today, it is most suited to be upgraded into a public connector for the AI era, rather than a decorative backdrop for isolated industrial parks.
2. **Why are there three tiers?** Zhongzhiyuan validates "trustworthiness," AI Origin community validates "open source transformation," and Dazhongsi validates "integration into daily life"; the three tiers form a complete and mature staircase from technology to society.
3. **Where is AI Really?** Not hidden in large screens and slogans, but in ten urban test cases that have space, users, minimal data, Human Review, and an exit button.
4. **How to Avoid a Fleeting Success?** Maintain operations through annual operations with problem ledgers, reproducible releases, quarterly reviews, and the Global AI Commons weekly meetings, rather than relying on one-off festivals to sustain momentum.
5. **How to Truly Implement It?** Starting from a 100-day retractable prototype, progressively expand through four gates of "safety, public value, reproducibility, and operation," ensuring that no advancement is made without mature documentation.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

Overall structure is "one ridge, three courts, two wings, and four types of interfaces." One ridge is the Jing-Zhang Memory Green Ridge: it studies the coexistence of north-south pedestrian continuity, cultural narrative, and reversible AI services without touching unknown cultural heritage and implementation boundaries. Three courts are the Full Stack Validation Court, the Open Source Origin Court, and the Intelligent Living Court. Two wings organize Zhongguancun's technological services to the west and Xiao Yuehe scenario empowerment to the east. Four types of interfaces are rail transit connections, shared campus and park areas, community services, and blue-green connections. [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001] [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001]

Space is not a backdrop for hosting activities, but rather a "protocol" for where innovation loops occur. Each node on the track spine adopts a four-layer interface: the outermost layer is a public passage that is open 24/7 and barrier-free; the second layer is a city living room where one can stay and observe; the third layer is a reserved space for testing and co-creation; the innermost layer is controlled research and development. Moving from the outer to the inner layer, data permissions are progressively tightened; moving from the inner to the outer layer, the obligation to explain results is progressively strengthened. This approach both prevents closed campuses from consuming Public Space and avoids unregulated experiments intruding into daily life.

The zoning for land use adopts a complete coverage rather than fragmented strips: the southern segment as a mixed zone for urban-type AI industry and services, the middle and southern segment as a zone for talent living and community services, the middle segment as a continuous public domain along the Jing-Zhang, the middle northern segment as a zone for near-school technology transfer and open R&D, and the northern segment as a zone for full-stack independent innovation and governance validation. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001] These codes are used for structured expression and do not represent current land use or approved changes. Their value lies in providing a "task division map" for subsequent professional teams: which segment prioritizes ground-level vitality, which segment prioritizes commuting and living, which segment prioritizes protection and openness, and which segment prioritizes technology transfer from schools and enterprises.

Urban Renewal follows a four-tiered staircase approach: "Operational First—Light Renovation—Shared Performance—Necessary Update." The first tier involves validating needs through activity schedules, open rules, signage, and furniture. The second tier studies the shared ground floor, idle spaces, and rooftops after completing title, fire, and structural verifications. The third tier discusses energy efficiency, accessibility, equipment, and facade renovations. The fourth tier involves new construction or major adjustments, which must enter the statutory planning and engineering procedures. The 15 submitted Building Footprints are merely indicative of the block's capacity and facade [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001], not representing the current buildings, specific demolish–renovate–retain strategies, or construction scale. (Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy)

The aesthetic approach focuses on "low building bases, open ground floors, readable roofs, and restrained nightscapes": along the Track Ridge, maintain human scale and continuous shading; the three courts are designed to form identifiable public structures, but must not obscure historical resources or create strong light disturbances; new and old materials are layered through durable brick and stone, metal mesh, wood/renewable materials, and replaceable digital interfaces. Due to the lack of height, density, and Building Coverage Ratio controls, this plan does not set specific numerical values; [metric:floor_area_ratio], [metric:building_height_m], [metric:building_density], and [metric:total_floor_area_sqm] are all unknown and await official control plans, surveys, cultural heritage documentation, and specialized data.

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

The polygons for the three key areas are temporary rough constraints, expressing only the positioning, system relationships, and validation sequence. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001] [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002] [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003]

![Design Plans for Three Key Areas](assets/figures/key-areas.png)

**Zhongzhiyuan: Full-stack Validation Garden.** Organized as a garden-type innovation district with a short loop of "research and development—validation—governance—display." Conceptually, the trustworthy AI evaluation field, robot co-existence sandbox, and AI safety governance corridor are positioned between the research and development carrier and the Qinghe public interface, ensuring that test results can be understood by peers, managers, and the public. Building updates prioritize forming flexible experimental units, shared instrument lobbies, and semi-outdoor discussion spaces; traffic-wise, the focus is on studying the conflicts between the park entrance and public slow travel, without drawing the Fifth Ring Road alignment; green spaces adopt small-scale, reversible facilities, avoiding unknown blue lines, flood protection, and ecological conditions. Risk gates include official district boundaries, Qinghe/Water Conservation conditions, road safety, equipment certification, and data governance.

Its spatial prototype is the "**Verification Ring in the Garden**": an outer slow-moving ring allows the public to safely observe, a mid-circle modular test field supports time-switching operations, and an inner research courtyard remains quiet; the three circles are separated by transparent rule walls rather than solid high walls. The most recognizable moment is not the robot performance, but the weekly "Failure Open Day" — when teams publicly explain when models fail, who takes over, and how they are fixed, making trust visible in the city culture.

**AI Origin Community: Open Source Origin Courtyard.** Organize a proximal-type neighborhood around the themes of "source innovation—open source collaboration—result transformation—talent living." The open source release hall provides reproducible demonstrations and contribution records, while the result transformation living room connects researchers, product managers, legal, and standard services. The talent station connects short-term accommodations, childcare, sports, dining, and international services. Spatially, prioritize pedestrian connectivity and shared times between the research campus, park, and community. Around the rail station, form an integrated public interface for sheltering, waiting, cycling, and accessibility. Any campus openness, building sharing, or demolition–renovate–retain strategy must be confirmed by the property and safety owner, and cannot be unilaterally decided by this plan. (Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy)

Its spatial prototype is the "**Knowledge Transept and Open-Source Long Table**": a public corridor that is traversable, pausable, and suitable for hosting small-scale presentations, connecting the lab's entrance hall, transformation services, and talent living spaces; a continually expanding public table that serves both as furniture and as a contribution ceremony. Each displayed outcome must include a version, license, reproducibility status, and maintainer, avoiding the misconception that "first release" equals "completion."

**Dazhongsi: Smart Living Court.** Organize an urban-type innovative district around the themes of "intelligent entities—intelligent terminals—content co-creation—urban consumption." The multi-intelligent body living experiment court only handles service orchestration initiated by users, without conducting continuous identity tracking; the digital content co-creation theater provides small-scale public testing, copyright annotation, and manual editing; the smart clock public screen displays open-source contributions, scenario rules, and exit mechanisms, rather than advertisements. The four quadrants of the site are connected to address pedestrian issues: first, measure crosswalk wait times, continuous accessibility, non-motorized vehicle parking, and the ground floor interface, then propose engineering solutions by a traffic professional team. Focus on discussing public environments around key enterprises and universities, without pre-setting corporate renovations or land ownership arrangements.

Its spatial prototype is the "Station City Quadrant Living Theater": the arrival interface, originally divided by roads, is organized into four complementary living rooms—commuter services, family co-creation, digital content, and nighttime light consumption. All smart services follow a default process of "active invocation—single authorization—instant feedback—ready exit"; when not interacting with people, devices revert to a quiet, low-brightness, and non-interactive state.

Public Spaces should follow the principle of "rules first, then equipment; small-scale first, then expansion; manual fallback first, then automation; exit mechanisms first, then long-term operation." Any pilot project that encounters safety, privacy, fairness, or public space exclusion issues should be able to be downgraded or withdrawn immediately.

### Understand AI Innovation in a Day: From "Time to Work" to "Joint Training of Cities"

07:40, commuters at Dazhongsi see accessible routes and congestion information, but the system fails to recognize people; 09:30, the research team submits their model, license, and reproducibility script at the original point presentation hall; 11:00, the transformation living room routes technical issues to the product, standards, and legal counsel; 14:00, the model enters the Zhongzhiyuan trusted evaluation field, where failed samples are recorded rather than hidden; 17:30, the robot sandbox completes low-speed co-existence testing under the supervision of a security officer; 19:00, residents in the Jing-Zhang memory corridor choose whether to participate in the co-creation of oral history; 21:00, the smart clock public screen announces the day's energy consumption, human intervention, and appeal status; after closing, all temporary personal conversations are cleared according to the rules. ## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The land use structure ensures comprehensive zoning for machine verification, while a "mixed interface" guarantees urban vitality. The five polygons in `land_use.geojson` share tangents and cover the submission boundary, ensuring that parks, research and development, living, and services are not depicted as unconnected islands. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-005] The land use code only expresses conceptual primary directions; compatibility, boundaries, indicators, and approved uses must be confirmed by formal zoning plans.

Urban design strategies employ four action verbs instead of a rigid Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy: **Guard**, to prioritize the protection of carriers with high historical value, structural performance, and continuous usage value; **Integrate**, to improve the relationship between the park and the street through shared ground floors, corridors, courtyards, and operational hours; **Upgrade**, to perform performance updates to equipment, cladding, accessibility, and fire safety conditions; **Supplement**, to study small-scale additions when permitted by legal conditions and when public value is clear. The total Building Footprint area [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm] and the number of buildings [metric:building_count] submitted are indicative models, not current as-built surveys, buildable scale, or property rights conclusions.

Space supply is structured in a three-tier product system of "short-term shared—growth modules—stable carriers," and provides migration paths for different teams. Public value conditions that can be studied include shared meeting/experimental facilities, open activity hours, ground-level accessibility, barrier-free design, low-carbon disclosure, and community projects, but these must be determined through subsequent policy, contract, and public negotiations. Any specific site's retention, renovation, demolition, or new construction must be judged by a professional team after all surveying, ownership, structural, fire safety, cultural heritage, and planning conditions are in place.

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The transportation concept is the "Intelligent Mobility Spine": a continuous north-south spine for walking and cycling, three east-west stitching lines, fifteen-minute connectivity loops within the plazas, and a public interface at the transit stations. The concept slow-moving centerline within the layers totals [metric:road_centerline_length_m], used solely for network continuity comparison, not for road right-of-way or construction alignment. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-002] Recent work should begin with a pedestrian audit: crossing wait times, grades and curb ramps, shading and shelter from rain, nighttime lighting, parking for non-motorized vehicles, delivery conflicts, and emergency access; followed by further refinement by a transportation professional team considering the right-of-way, traffic volumes, and safety evaluations.

![Traffic Slow Zone and Blue-Green Public Space Composite System Diagram](assets/figures/mobility-bluegreen.png)

Integrated rail systems are not about pursuing large mixed-use complexes, but about making the first five minutes after exiting the station seamless: continuous barrier-free access, clear signage, shared waiting areas, bike-to-ride connections, safety at night, and services during rainy or snowy weather and at ground level.
