## 2. Three-Level Scope Work Framework

### 2.1 Coordinated Research Area: 43.6 square kilometers of the "interface layer"

Not to draw an overly deterministic master plan for a vast area, but to address how industry, talent, research, capital, public services, and Jing-Zhang collaboration can be integrated into the Jing-Zhang Innovation Belt. The output is an interface list, collaborative directions, and evaluation criteria, without replacing regional planning. [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]

### 2.2 Overall Design Area: Approximately 11.4 square kilometers of the "system layer"

Organize the space with "a verifiable spine, three verifiable cities, two wings in coordination, and six closed loops":

- **A Witness Spine:** A continuous corridor forming a walkway, bike path, public display, data annotation, appeal, and audit trail along the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001]
- **Three Validation Cities:** Zhongzhiyuan validates "whether it can operate safely"; AI Origin community validates "whether it can be publicly reused"; Dazhongsi validates "whether it can generate public and market value."
- **Wings:** The west wing, known as the Knowledge Ecosystem Wing, connects universities and research institutions, while the east wing, known as the Urban Application Wing, connects communities, business districts, and enterprise services; both are conceptual directions and are not referred to as existing agreements.
- **Six Closed Loops:** Brand, Ecology, Scenario, Space, Culture, Operations, corresponding to the complete delivery chain of `agent.1`—`agent.6`.

### 2.4 Rules for Transmission Between Levels

## 3. Coordinated Research Area Industry and Future City Research

### 3.1 Core Judgment

The competitiveness of AI industry spaces does not come solely from the number of companies, but from four rare capabilities: **high-quality problems, authentic test environments, credible evidence, and public authorization that allows for exit**. The Jing-Zhang Innovation Belt should upgrade from "park recruitment" to "city-level validation services," enabling companies to obtain verifiable evidence of safety, performance, inclusivity, and operational effectiveness before entering the market.

### 3.2 Five Types of Industrial Interfaces

1. **Model and Data Interface:** Red Team, Security Assessment, Data Card, Synthetic Data, Reproducible Baseline.
2. **Robots and Edge Interfaces:** Controlled Roads, Manual Takeover, Energy/Delay, Equipment Insurance, and Exit Mechanisms.
3. **Public Service Interfaces:** Professional responsibilities for high-impact scenarios such as education, medical guidance, legal diversion, and accessible navigation.
4. **Market and Capital Interface:** Trusted product experience, compliant clinics, pre-purchase validation, disclosure of results and complaints alongside.
5. **Regional Collaboration Interface:** Establish an open call relationship with Beixinwei Community, Future Science City, Huairou Science City, Economic and Technological Development Area, and the Jing-Jin-Ji region, based on the model of "problem—capacity—testing—transformation." This is a suggested interface and is not equivalent to a signed partnership.

### 3.3 Talent and Organizational Profile

The talent policy should not be limited to algorithm engineers but should cover eight categories of users/contributors: open-source developers, startup teams, leading enterprises and industry visitors, nearby residents, university students and faculty, children and teenagers, elderly and disabled individuals, as well as non-digital users and low-income service workers. Spatial and service performance must be evaluated separately for these groups to ensure that differences are not masked by average satisfaction levels. [metric:inclusive_persona_count]

### 3.4 Operations Organization

Suggest establishing a "Jing-Zhang Certified City Trustee Operation Body": Public sector sets the authorization and red lines, professional operation teams organize testing, independent audit institutions verify results, public representatives participate in the review of high-impact scenarios, and enterprises bear the costs of equipment, insurance, rectification, and exit. The operation body must not simultaneously monopolize data, testing, certification, and recruitment, to avoid conflicts of interest.


### 3.5 Criteria for Verifying Regional Value

Collaboration within the region is not evaluated solely by the number of signed agreements, but by whether problems span across zones, whether external validation of test evidence is possible, whether open-source outcomes are sustainably maintained, whether public services cover vulnerable users, and whether failed projects are exited according to established rules. Different participation entry points are set for research, industry, capital, and communities to prevent technical teams from defining user needs and to avoid short-term recruitment goals squeezing safety, inclusivity, and long-term maintenance. All regional relationships are expressed through recommended interfaces before formal agreements are signed. [source:CASE-MEDELLIN-RUTAN] [metric:inclusive_persona_count]

## 5. Detailed Design for Key Areas

### 5.1 Zhongzhiyuan: Model and Robot Validation City

**Location:** Position the "Innovation Showcase" as "Controlled Validation for Safety, Performance, Energy Consumption, and Co-Existence Capability." The space consists of a Model Red Team Validation Chamber, a Robot Pedestrian-Friendly Test Hall, an Edge Side Computing Energy Consumption Station, an Open Source Hardware Workshop, a Failure Museum, and a Centennial Validated Monument. The test area employs geofencing, time-limited operation, status transparency, manual take-over, liability insurance, and red-line shutdowns. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001] [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-001] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-003]

### 5.2 Beijing AI Origin Community: Open Source and Talent Verification City

**Location:** To establish a public reuse chain where code, data, models, services, and community feedback are openly shared and reused. The core components include an open-source release hall, a city smart audit room, a talent living service station, an education/medical trust service station, an open-source code wall, and an original public validation square. Digital services must provide offline signage, manned phone support, and paper entry points simultaneously. Non-digital users should not lose basic services due to a refusal to provide data. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-002] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-005]

### 5.3 Dazhongsi: Market and Public Value Validation City

**Location:** Verify that the technology addresses real problems, is worth purchasing, and is understandable to the general public. Establish a trustworthy product experience gallery, a multilingual city service desk, a policy compliance and financing and investment clinic, and an evidence starting point. Make the product effects, limitations, complaints, versions, and sponsorship relationships publicly available side by side, and prohibit turning the public verification space into a hidden advertisement. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-003] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001]

### 5.4 Four Pilgrimage/Audit Landmarks

1. **Evidence Starting Point:** All visitors first see "how the system works, who is responsible, and how to exit."
2. **Open Code Wall:** Records contributors, licenses, versions, reproducibility status, and maintenance responsibilities.
3. **Failed Museum:** Displaying closed, corrected, or replaced systems, transforming failure into public knowledge.
4. **Centennial Memorial Plaque:** Incorporate the spirit of the century-old Jing-Zhang Railway and annual public audits, as well as long-term contributions, into the city's memory.

There are concept nodes everywhere, and implementation will require review by site conditions, cultural heritage, landscape, public art, and operations. [metric:pilgrimage_landmark_count]


### 5.5 Collaboration Loop Among Three Cities

The northern segment generates reproducible reports through model testing, robotics, energy consumption, and synthetic data testing; the middle segment converts reviewed codes, data cards, model cards, and operation manuals into public assets and collects community corrections; the southern segment allows businesses, consumers, public service institutions, and visitors to test the value in real use. If accidents, deviations, complaints, or maintenance interruptions occur, the results revert along the same path to the testing segment, rather than being separately explained by the exhibition and promotion segments. The three cities share audit numbers, versions, and exit records, but each retains its own manual services and local responsibilities. [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design] [metric:key_area_count]

## 6. AI Innovation Ecosystem, Talent Profile, and AI-Enabled Scenario

### 6.1 Scene Admission Rules

Each scenario must include: clear purpose and prohibited uses, legal data sources, minimization of data collection, human point of responsibility, non-digital alternatives, public visibility status, appeal mechanism, threshold for de-activation, and exit/deletion mechanisms. High-impact scenarios must not shift professional responsibility with a single statement like "AI is for reference only."

### 6.2 Twelve Readable Scene Cards

#### SC-01｜Model Safety Red Team Sandbox (Validation Testing)

- **Location:** Zhongzhiyuan / BLDG-001; **Objective:** Validate hallucinations, biases, unauthorized  calls, and prompt injection risks before entering the public scene.
- **User:** Model team, research institutions, regulators, and independent testers; **Data:** de-identified test set, adversarial samples, model cards, event logs
- **Privacy:** Synthetic or anonymized data will be used by default; real personal data must be approved separately, collected minimally, and subject to a deletion deadline.
- **Human Review:** The test lead signs off; high-risk conclusions are re-verified by an independent review group, and the model must not self-determine that it is ready for deployment.
- **Operating Entity:** Park Validation Operating Body + Independent Safety Laboratory; **Publicly Visible:** Risk Level Light, Version Number, Applicable/Prohibited Boundaries, Re-verification Date
- **Main Risks:** treating the ranking score as the true measure of safety; test set leakage; supplier self-attestation.
- **Appeals:** On-site customer service desks, telephone/paper forms, and online entries coexist; complaints enter a trackable work order system, with major disputes subject to quarterly public audits.
- **Shutdown:** Immediately downgrade/shutdown the system when a personal safety incident, data overreach, continuous threshold deviation, inability to manually take over, or authorization expires.
- **Exit:** Form a version, conduct a cost-benefit and impact review; exit if not meeting the criteria, delete unnecessary data, and publicly record the exit.

#### SC-02｜Robot Pedestrian Friendly Validation Loop (Validation Test)

- **Location:** Zhongzhiyuan / ROAD-004 + BLDG-002; **Objective:** To verify the coexistence of delivery and inspection robots with pedestrians within a controlled time period and geofenced area.

- **Privacy:** No facial recognition; original image edge processing and short-term retention, retaining only event snippets.
- **Human Review:** Site safety officers can take over with a single click; accessibility testers have veto power.
- **Operating Entity:** Park Operators + Road/Public Safety Professional Departments (after Legal Approval); **Publicly Visible:** Test Period, Operating Range, Maximum Speed, Emergency Stop Button, Responsible Party

## 12. Risk, Copyright, and Compliance Statements

### 12.1 Main Risks

1. **Boundary Risks:** Temporary geometry misread as Official Planning Boundary; controlled through significant labels, low confidence indicators, and recalculation triggers.
2. **Technical Risks:** The scenario transitions from supportive expansion to automatic decision-making or monitoring; controlled through purpose limitations, human accountability, minimal data requirements, and downtime thresholds.
3. **Inclusivity in Risk Management:** Digital services exclude the elderly, people with disabilities, and those without smart devices; control is exercised through offline, telephonic, paper-based, and manual entry points.
4. **Operational Risks:** Testing, certification, and leasing are self-attested by the same entity; controlled through functional separation, independent auditing, and disclosure of conflicts of interest.
5. **Spatial Risks:** Solidifying buildings and investments without surveying/planning control; gating through phases with "unknown" metrics.
6. **Cultural Risks:** The Jing-Zhang, a century-old railway, is being superficially symbolized; it should be controlled through mechanisms of engineering ethics, open acknowledgment of failures, and long-term contributions.
