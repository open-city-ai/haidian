# Jing-Zhang Translation Field: Facilitating Mutual Understanding Between Technology, Cities, and People

**Let technology, cities, and people understand each other.** The Jing-Zhang Railway once translated distance into time; today, this innovation belt needs to translate research into products, models into understandable evidence, tests into professional decisions, and railway memories into public life. The proposal does not treat AI as a glowing city decoration, but rather makes "translation" into a set of spatial and governance infrastructure: **one core, three institutes, two wings, twelve stations**.

![Jing-Zhang Transliteration Brand, Three Layers of Evidence, and Core Commitment](assets/figures/site-overview.png)

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

The strategy for the sector is not to continue building office buildings, but to shorten five segments of "translation loss": from papers to prototypes, from prototypes to products, from models to evidence, from products to scenarios, and from scenarios to procurement. Each segment should be configured with space, serve people, and include stop rules.

| Translation Chain | Spatial Carrier | Minimum Institutional Action | Visible Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Research → Prototype | Origin Open Source Workshop | Publish Issue Lists, Computing Vouchers, and Replication Experiments | Reproducible Demo and Failure Records |
| Prototype → Product | Zhongzhiyuan Validation Yard | Sandbox Protocol, Standard Consultation, Security Red Team | Test Report Rather than Promotional Video |
| Model → Human | Explainable Evidence Kiosk | Output Source, Confidence, Human Review Entry | Service Explanation for the Layperson |
| Product→City | Twelve Translation Stations | Revealing Real Needs, Impact Assessment, Time-Limited Pilot | Three Conclusions: Approve/Rework/Exit |
| Urban→Market | Dazhongsi Release Yard | Scenarios Procurement Clinic, International Roadshow, User Feedback | First Order, Collaboration, or Clear Rejection |

Brand name "Jing-Zhang Translatability Hub" refers to both **yard (railway station/common workspace)** and **translation (cross-disciplinary understanding)**. The logo consists of two opposing trajectories, an orange "transfer beam," and twelve sleeper notches: the opposing trajectories represent bidirectional understanding between technology and the public, the transfer beam represents human confirmation, and the notches represent twelve translatability stations. All graphics are original creations by this intelligent entity, with no corporate trademarks or external materials.

The future urban form follows four design recommendations: "low threshold ground floor, flexible middle layer, shared roof, and continuous ground." They are component rules for the pending formal site and control plan conditions, not conclusions for height, Floor Area Ratio (), or setback.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

Overall structure as an "Evidence Chain walkable path": the northern end for system validation, the middle section for knowledge transformation, and the southern end for daily releases, all connected through the public interface of the archaeological park. Four land use strategy zones are designated using national land classification codes, but only express functional tendencies: `0802` for research translation, `1401` for park openness, `05` for industrial services, and `0702` for community services. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-01] [depth:land_use_layout]

Six smallest space components are reused repeatedly to avoid building anew for each scenario:

1. **Evidence Kiosk**: Display input sources, conclusion confidence, Human Review, and appeal entry; still provides static information when the network is down.
2. **Test Corridor**: Designed to accommodate mobile isolation, observation points, and emergency stop devices for robot/slow vehicle testing.
3. **Open Table**: Features an open topic wall, electrified workstations, and a small release surface, supporting non-consumptive stays.
4. **Transition Space**: AI suggests an area for manual confirmation before entering the actual service, including queuing and privacy buffer zones.
5. **Reversible Box**: Lightweight, modular short-term rental test units; removed if they do not meet performance criteria, with no pre-emptive permanent construction.
6. **Memory Scale**: Use continuous wayfinding formed by railway mileage, industrial components, and oral history, without creating faux-ancient landscapes.

Space actions are categorized into three types: immediate signage and operational activities; connectivity and renovation that require road, ownership, and engineering reviews; and construction volume and intensity that must wait for formal control plan approval. `geometry/buildings.geojson` only expresses twelve **suggested building/envelope packages**, and does not represent the current building survey or demolition conclusions. [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-01] [depth:retain_renovate_demolish]. The Floor Area Ratio, Building Height, density, green space ratio control values, and setback distances are **unknown**. [depth:development_intensity_controls] [depth:height_massing_character]

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

The Three Institutes are not three similar districts but rather each carry out different "translation processes." The three temporary rectangular spaces serve only as spatial indices, and the boundaries not provided in the announcement cannot be inferred from the graphics. [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001] [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design]

### A Zhongzhiyuan: System Translation Institute

Positioned as "transforming models, chips, robots, and standards into controlled protocols." The northern side features a Qinghe Ecological Observation Interface, with an internal layout comprising a Safety Governance Hall, Hardware Co-Testing Pavilion, Model Red Team Room, and Low-Carbon Computing Power Explanation Station, forming a validation loop. Four categories of users must enter through the same exchange door: teams submit test hypotheses, professionals verify data and safety boundaries, and the public only interacts with the publicly accessible demonstration layer. Failed results are recorded in the "Not Approved Archive" to avoid only showcasing successful cases. Key dependencies include the Official Boundary, river/channel/flood control conditions, Fifth Ring Road traffic interfaces, and energy capacity.

### B Beijing AI Origin: Knowledge Translation Institute

Positioned as "converting papers, open-source contributions, and academic achievements into reproducible prototypes." The five ground-floor interfaces include an open-source long table, a reproducible laboratory, a results clinic, a youth third space, and a campus-park pedestrian seam. Any achievement must first address four questions: can the code/data be traced back, are failures documented, is the intellectual property clear, and can ordinary users understand it. Key dependencies include campus-ground ownership, a census of existing buildings, interfaces with tracks and roads, and negotiations for nighttime operations.

### C Dazhongsi: Daily Translation Institute

Positioned as "transforming smart terminals, content, and corporate capabilities into daily services that residents can choose." The interface is composed of a station-city transition hall, a quadrants walking loop, a model street for living services, a data rights counter, and an international release venue, all forming a 24/7 interface. All AI services must provide an artificial window, non-digital pathways, and an exit button. Key dependencies include the integrated conditions of Dazhongsi station, traffic assessment at the intersection, planning of green spaces, commercial ownership, and activity safety.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The zoning divisions fully cover the temporary overall boundary, with four strategic bands validating the proportionality of functional relationships; it is not a statutory parcel. [data:geometry/site_boundary.geojson#SITE-001]. The building layer serves as the design envelope, incorporating types such as `ai_r_and_d/lab/incubator/mixed_use/community_service/cultural`, etc. Its area can be recalculated from the submitted geometry but does not represent the current condition or permitted buildable scale. [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm] [metric:proposed_building_count]

The Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy must be executed according to the same decision tree after obtaining data on building age, structural safety, ownership, value, tenant information, and carbon emissions: first, check safety and cultural heritage redlines; then, compare the retention of functional appropriateness with the lifecycle carbon; subsequently, conduct tenant negotiations; and finally, choose between retention, minor renovation, reuse, or demolition. Any "demolition" conclusion must be supported by independent engineering and social impact arguments. This stage does not make specific conclusions about existing buildings.

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The transportation backbone consists of a north-south "Evidence Greenway," three key horizontal stitching lines, and a station transition loop. [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-01] [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]. The layers only express the intent for pedestrian and transit use, without naming the lines as existing roads or road rights-of-way. Accessibility continuity takes precedence over spectacle: ramps, tactile paths, seating, drinking fountains, toilets, and nighttime lighting are completed first, before overlaying navigation services.

The city strategy adopts "plugging in rather than being an island": the evidence booth is plugged into the public service knowledge base; the low-carbon computing station is plugged into sub-metering and load reduction; the test corridor reserves emergency stops and manual takeovers; activity areas are equipped with reversible power supplies and space for rainwater retreat. Formal implementation must complete the necessary pipeline, flood control, fire safety, energy supply, communication, and maintenance boundaries. [data:geometry/constraints.geojson#CONSTRAINT-01] [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]

Public services are anchored by the principles of "one door, two paths": digital entrances are co-located with manned windows, ensuring that all services maintain an option for use without smart terminals. Business services follow a closed loop of "registration of issues—compliance diagnosis—pilot—review—procurement/exit," with quality of conversion taking precedence over the number of registrations.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The Jing-Zhang Heritage Park is the most important public interface, rather than a landscape strip behind an innovation district. The green space is suggested to consist of continuous pedestrian-friendly green corridors, three shared courtyards, and six public translation squares. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-01] [depth:blue_green_public_space]. When conditions for rainwater management, ecology, and cultural preservation are lacking, no proposal is made for channel realignment, hard riprap, or pseudo-precise sponge city indicators.

Culture narratives employ three grammatical elements: "tracks," "handovers," and "metrics." Tracks derive from the rail connections, handovers from the shift of responsibilities between humans and machines, and metrics from scientific measurements and open-source contributions. Four landmarks are all functional public facilities: the northern end "Protocol Gate," the origin "Open Source Signal House," the middle section "Evidence Metric Courtyard," and the southern end "Daily Handover Hall." The design does not replicate the locomotive form or commodify the industrial heritage theme.

Urban Character is recommended with a preference for reuse, transparent ground-level access, visible and explainable equipment, and low-disturbance nightscapes. Roof treatment and height are to be confirmed based on sightlines, cultural heritage, solar access, fire safety, and control plan conditions. Signage should be bilingual (English and Chinese) and provide high-contrast, tactile, and audio alternatives; activity spaces should retain at least one non-consumption seating option, quiet periods, and wheelchair turnaround spaces.
