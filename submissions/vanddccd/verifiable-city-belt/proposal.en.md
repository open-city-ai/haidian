# The Verifiable City — Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt。

Over a hundred years ago, Zhan Tianyou faced an unavoidable grade at Qinglong Bridge. He did not claim that the locomotives could climb it, but instead acknowledged this constraint and used the "person" (or "ren")-shaped reversal to convert the grade into spatial relief. The Jing-Zhang Railway became a line that was repeatedly told about not only because it was the first mainline railway designed by Chinese people, but also because it transformed an engineering judgment into something that could be seen, measured, and verified by future generations standing in the same place.

One hundred years from now, the questions that cities on either side of this line will need to answer have changed, but the structures are strikingly similar. Artificial intelligence is becoming an integral part of urban operations—suggesting traffic signal timing, scheduling deliveries, allocating services, and screening applications. The common feature of these decisions is that they occur rapidly, are rarely explained, and leave almost no trace that citizens can check themselves. When an AI system makes a decision in the city, ordinary people do not know what data it is based on, do not know who has verified it, and do not know who is responsible when it is wrong.

This scheme judges that: **Haidian's Innovation Belt is most lacking not in computing power, space, or policy, but in 'verifiability' as a form of urban infrastructure.** Therefore, we do not design this belt as a showcase for how strong AI is, but rather as a track where AI must undergo public verification. This both accurately inherits the spirit of the Jing-Zhang Railway — acknowledging constraints and leaving judgments in space for others to verify — and is Haidian's only unique differentiator in global AI competition: other cities can deploy AI faster, but only one sufficiently confident innovation district dares to inscribe AI's failure rates on the walls of Public Space.

This entire document is a **Conceptual Recommendation and reference proposal** for the open call, intended to provide guidance for professional teams to further study and develop. It does not replace statutory planning, nor does it constitute the government's review conclusion or implementation commitment.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The three levels are not three maps of different scales, but rather three different types of work. This proposal distinguishes them into three types of outcomes: "research—design—detailed planning," to avoid unnecessary detail at the broad scale and insufficient depth at the small scale [depth:three_level_scope_framework].

**Coordinated Research Area** covers approximately 43.6 square kilometers (north to the North Fifth Ring Road, east to the Jingzhang Expressway, south to West Straight Street, and west to Wanquan River Road) [source:PLANNING-LIMITS]. The nature of work at this level is **industrial and urban form research**, without making land use allocations. It aims to address the strategic focus of AI industry in Haidian, how the Three Zones and Two Wings can work together, what the future AI urban form will look like, and the innovative coordination relationships with the Beiwai Community, Future Science City, Huairou Science City, Economic Development Zone, and the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region. This package does not submit independent geometry at this level because the Provisional Boundary precision is not sufficient to support regional-level spatial conclusions; related judgments are expressed through textual arguments and structural relationship diagrams. This is an active convergence to the limits of available data, not an omission.

**Overall Design Area** is approximately **11.4 square kilometers** [metric:official_declared_overall_design_area_sqm], serving as the main subject of the spatial outcomes in this package, corresponding to the submitted **SITE_BOUNDARY[data:geometry/site_boundary.geojson#SITE-001]**. This layer aims to achieve the Urban Design depth required by the control plan [standard:MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING], outputting the Land-Use Layout, updating the framework, traffic and urban policy strategies, blue-green Public Space system, and guidelines for style and update projects. A key spatial fact to be noted is that the actual form of this area is a **north-south narrow strip, approximately 1.37 kilometers wide and 9.72 kilometers long** from east to west, with a width-to-length ratio of about 1:7. This is not a drafting oversight, but the inevitable result of the definition of "1-2 kilometers around the archaeological park," which directly determines the core strategy of this plan. In such a narrow and elongated area, the only structure that can permeate the entire scope must be linear. Any attempt to create a "central radiating" structure would conflict with the site's form. Therefore, we choose to organize the "verification track" along the archaeological park, turning the linear structure into an advantage rather than a flaw.

**The key-area detailed design covers approximately 368.4 hectares**, extending from north to south as follows: the Zhongzhiyuan AI Independent Innovation Acceleration Area covering about 192.1 hectares, the Beijing AI Origin Community covering about 104.3 hectares, and the Dazhongsi AI Industry Cluster Area covering about 72.0 hectares [data:geometry/key_areas.geojson#PROV-KEY-001]. At this level, the Urban Design should achieve the depth of the Integrated Planning Implementation Plan, outputting the functional mix, spatial structure, classification of update intentions, traffic organization, and connectivity of Public Spaces. The three areas together account for only 32.3% of the Overall Design Area [metric:key_area_total_area_sqm], indicating that the non-key areas between the key zones make up two-thirds. If only these three areas are addressed without resolving their connections, the entire strip will remain fragmented. This is why this plan includes the verification track as the primary structure and the three areas as stations on the track. (Key-Area Detailed Design Area)

The transmission relationship between the three layers is clear: the coordinating layer determines "what to verify" (strategic priorities and industrial directions), the overall layer determines "where to verify" (land use, green spaces, active transportation, and renewal frameworks), and the focus layer determines "how to verify" (specific spaces, functions, and implementation intentions). In terms of metrics, the coordinating layer corresponds to industrial indicators (currently kept as unknown due to data gaps), the overall layer corresponds to area and ratio metrics [metric:green_ratio][metric:public_space_ratio], and the focus layer corresponds to the area and number of clusters of key areas indicators [metric:building_cluster_count].

The limitations brought by the Provisional Boundary should be clarified most clearly in this layer: the provisional **SITE_BOUNDARY** is a rough range based on the announced text, **area magnitude is credible but boundary positions are not**. Therefore, all the 'segment positions' and 'group positions' in this package should be understood as relative order relationships (north segment—middle segment—south segment) rather than absolute coordinates [assumption:A-BOUNDARY-001]. Once the Official Boundary is released, the content that needs to be recalculated includes: the actual y-direction division positions of the five segments, the boundary and area of the 38 land uses, the actual proportion of green spaces and Public Spaces, the placement of the 14 building groups, and all area-related metrics [metric:land_use_coverage_gap_sqm].

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

### Overall Concept: The Verifiable City

**Main Name:** Verifiable City · Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt. **English Name:** The Verifiable City — Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt.

The logic of naming is not rhetorical but responds to a specific question: Why should this corridor be remembered in the global AI innovation district? If the answer is "strong computing power, many companies, good policies," then another park with greater investment could replace it at any time. But if the answer is "here's the AI must be publicly verifiable," this becomes a form of institutional asset—requiring long-term trust accumulation, public records, and citizen participation habits that cannot be replicated through short-term investments. This naming simultaneously responds to the task book's prohibition against "submitting slogan-like names" and "repeating city, park, or company names" [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]: "Verifiable" is neither a place name nor a company name but a city's testable commitment.

The naming system is divided into four levels to ensure scalability and international dissemination: Level one is named "Verifiable City"; Level two are the functional names of the Three Zones and Two Wings, namely "Zhongzhiyuan·Model Validation Field", "AI Origin Community·Social Validation Field", "Dazhongsi·Market Validation Field", "Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing·Capital Validation Network", and "Xiaoyue River Scenario Enablement Wing·Life Validation Network"; Level three are node names, uniformly adopting the "Validation + Type" word construction, such as "Developer Stroll Path", "Honor Wall Plaza", "Open Source Display Corridor", "Governance Deliberation Hall" [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-002]; Level four are event names, adopting the "Validation Week/Validation Day" system, as detailed in the operational chapter. The migratability of this naming system lies in: any newly added space or activity can find its position within the four-level system without the need to create new words.

### Visual Identity and Logo Orientation

Logo concept direction is for the **Fold Return Mark**: abstract geometry derived from the Jing-Zhang Railway Qinglong Bridge's 'person' character fold return. The choice of this motif has three reasons: it is the most recognizable and copyright-free public historical symbol of the Jing-Zhang Railway; its geometry itself expresses 'acknowledging constraints and advancing with detours', consistent with the meaning of 'verifiable'; and it is still discernible at extremely small sizes, making it suitable for use as a digital badge.

The extension system includes: main identifier (foldback symbol + Chinese and English lettering); **verified badge** (Verified Badge) —— validated scenarios, schemes, or contributors can obtain numbered badges, which can serve as both digital identifiers and physical space plaques; the color zoning of the Three Zones and Two Wings (it is recommended to adopt a low-saturation color system of engineering blue, heritage ochre, and ecological green, avoiding high-saturation, tech-oriented schemes); the font direction should adopt open-source fonts (such as the Source Han series) to ensure clear licensing [assumption:A-CASE-001]. **all fonts, images, trademarks, and portraits must be clearly licensed, and this plan does not provide any unlicensed materials; specific visual assets must be confirmed by a professional team during the deepening phase.** It is particularly important to distinguish between the overall logo system (foldback symbol) and the cultural identifier system (see the cultural narrative chapter on heritage signifiers), which are two parallel systems. The former serves brand recognition and international communication, while the latter serves the reading of the site's history, and they must not be mixed [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK].

### Three Key Orientations, Five Major Functions, and the Synergistic Loop of Three Zones and Two Wings

The three major positioning tasks given are the Centennial Jing-Zhang Cultural Belt, the Urban AI Living Experience Belt, and the AI Integration Innovation Belt; the five functional areas are the Full-Stack Independent AI Innovation System, the World-Class AI Innovation Ecosystem, AI-Enabled Scenario Empowerment New Paradigm, Intelligent AI Vibrant City, and AI Governance Global Discourse [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]. Most proposals present them in parallel, but this parallelism fails to explain how they mutually support each other. This proposal suggests using a **validation loop** to string them into a closed loop:

**Technical Validation (Zhongzhiyuan) → Social Validation (AI Origin Community) → Market Validation (Dazhongsi) → Capital Validation (Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing) → Life Validation (Xiaoyue River Scenario Enablement Wing) → Feedback to Technical Validation.** This loop has clear input and output for each segment: Zhongzhiyuan outputs models and full-stack capabilities that have passed safety and standard tests, corresponding to the "Full-Stack Independent AI Innovation System" and "Global AI Governance Discourse"; the Origin Community tests the social acceptability of the model within real campus, park, and community relationships, corresponding to the "World-Class AI Innovation Ecosystem"; Dazhongsi tests whether it can form sustainable consumption and business models, corresponding to the "Smart Nativized New Business Models"; the Zhongguancun Wing provides global factor configuration and capital empowerment to test its scalability; the Xiaoyue River Wing returns it to residents' daily life to test whether it truly improves their lives, corresponding to the "AI+Scenario Enablement New Paradigm" and "Smart AI Vital City".

The value of this loop lies in **it transforming the 'Three Zones and Two Wings' from spatial divisions into a functional sequence**. Any AI application seeking to establish itself on this loop must sequentially pass through five validations, each occurring in a specific space, with public records and Human Review at every step. This directly explains why the three positioning strategies can be unified: the cultural belt provides the spiritual source of 'acknowledged constraints', the life experience belt is the venue for social and life validation, and the integrated innovation belt is the venue for technological and market validation.

In terms of regional collaboration, this plan advocates for the loop not to be confined within the boundaries: the model validation results from Zhongzhiyuan should open up and connect with the major scientific and technological infrastructure of Future Science City and Huairou Science City, forming a division of labor between basic research and engineering validation; the market validation at Dazhongsi and the scaled manufacturing in Economic and Technological Development Zone form a "validation—mass production" channel; the Beiwěi Community focuses on community and content ecology, capable of receiving user-side feedback for life validation; at the regional level of Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei, "validation standard mutual recognition" should serve as the collaborative lever—should an AI scenario pass public validation in Haidian, its validation records and methods should be directly referenced by other cities within the region to avoid redundant testing costs. This mechanism is suggested for further study by relevant departments and professional teams, and does not constitute a determined regional cooperation arrangement [assumption:A-OPERATION-001].

### Adaptation for AI in Future Urban Forms

About how AI will change cities, this proposal does not make a technological prediction but instead puts forward an actionable form of judgment: **In the AI era, the urban space most needs to increase is not intelligent devices, but the density of 'explainable interfaces'.** As more and more city services are mediated by algorithms, the urban space must correspondingly increase the physical locations where people can understand and intervene in these algorithms—otherwise, the cost of efficiency gains is a loss of citizens' sense of control over the city.

Based on this, three morphological strategies are proposed. First, **adaptive and evolutionary spatial supply**: systematically retain 'blank spaces' within the land structure, with approximately 6% of each of the five segments designated as blank spaces [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-007], to accommodate yet-to-emerge AI industries, thus avoiding the premature fixation of future spatial configurations by current functional definitions; this ratio is a Conceptual Recommendation, and the specific scale must be confirmed by a professional team based on official land use plans. Second, **perceptible and interactive AI+ transportation system**: use the validation track as the backbone, organizing a continuous system that includes the pedestrian spine, validation segments for cycling, station connections, and open road testing segments, with a total length of approximately 19.5 kilometers [metric:road_network_length_m]. The low-speed connection and robot delivery road testing segments are physically separated from the pedestrian space but are visually visible, allowing citizens to 'see' how the automated systems operate [data:geometry/roads.geojson#RD-006]. Third, **Continuous Boundless Blue-Green Space System**: Green space accounts for 20.6% [metric:green_ratio], with the main green corridor of the relic park spanning north, middle, and south segments throughout the entire area [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GS-002], forming a horizontal connection with the Qinghe and Xiaoyuehe Blue-Green Spaces, achieving the north-south penetration and east-west connectivity as required by the announcement.

Regarding the definitions of AI culture, AI society, and AI city, this proposal states: **AI culture is a culture that acknowledges uncertainty and openly records it; AI society is a society that retains human final judgment rights; AI city is a city where algorithmic decisions can be reviewed by citizens.** These three statements are not slogans; they correspond to specific spatial carriers within this proposal—Honor Wall Square records contributions and failures, the Governance Deliberative Chamber bears Human Review, and the Open Source Exhibition Corridor publicly displays methods and data sources [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-007]. This section also addresses all the mandatory requirements outlined in task book agent.1, including comprehensive planning connotations, spatial industrial integration, and innovative ideas for land use and spatial planning: the innovation point of this proposal lies in treating 'verification capacity' as a planning, measurable, and allocable urban function, introducing a new metric, the number of verifiable nodes [metric:verification_node_count], into the metric system, rather than merely adding or subtracting from traditional land use and intensity dimensions.

Three horizontal conclusions can be drawn from the seven case studies. First, **no successful ecosystem is built on a single element**; it all depends on certain 'connective mechanisms'—therefore, this plan focuses on validating the feedback loop as a connective mechanism rather than listing elements. Second, **rejuvenation in heritage areas is most prone to failure due to displacement effects** (as seen in the lesson from C2), so this plan explicitly requires low-disturbance organic renewal and the configuration of affordable housing for young people in the original community. Third, **transparency in systems can itself become a competitive advantage** (as seen in C5 and C7), which is Haidian's relative strength compared to other AI parks and the most difficult to replicate. This is the direct basis for the concept of this plan.

### AI Innovation Ecosystem Map and Element Mechanisms

The ecological blueprint is organized around seven stages: "basic research → technological breakthrough → concept validation → pilot-scale incubation → productization → market scaling → capital exit and re-investment," corresponding to validation loops: basic research and technological breakthrough primarily occur in the platforms and facilities of universities and institutes supported by the original community and the Zhongzhiyuan; concept validation and pilot-scale incubation are the core functions of Zhongzhiyuan, also the weakest link in the current ecological chain; productization and market scaling take place in Dazhongsi; capital services are overseen by the Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing throughout the process; the Xiao Yuehe Wing provides the entry point for real user-side scenarios.

This proposal assesses that: **Haidian does not lack basic research or capital, but it lacks public facilities for the gap between concept validation and pilot testing.** This gap is difficult for the market to provide spontaneously because it requires expensive testing environments, clear regulatory boundaries, and open record-keeping systems, which are beyond the individual motivation of any single company to build independently. Therefore, this proposal advocates prioritizing this gap as a public investment focus, with specific spatial allocation corresponding to the full-stack validation and development cluster and the national-level AI platform validation and experimentation cluster in Zhongzhiyuan [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BD-002].

The following are the suggested mechanisms for ensuring the eight key elements, all of which are Conceptual Recommendations and do not constitute policy commitments [assumption:A-OPERATION-001]: **Land** —— Through Urban Renewal, release existing space and retain flexibility by preserving blank plots of land [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-038]; **Space** —— Allocate carriers based on 'verified functionality' rather than 'enterprise scale,' providing adaptable large-span spaces for the pilot phase; **Industry** —— Organize the supply and demand chain through a verification loop, clarifying entry and exit criteria for each segment; **Funding** —— Suggest that the Zhongguancun Wing provide research directions for patient capital tools covering the concept verification phase; **Talent** —— Use youth affordable housing and a 15-minute radius as core spatial indicators [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BD-006]; **Computing Power** —— Explore the integration of edge computing and distributed energy with traditional municipal facilities; **Data** —— Establish an open data directory and a minimum necessary authorization mechanism, prohibiting the use of non-public or personal privacy data. **Scenario** —— Implement a three-tier open mechanism (internal testing, controlled release, public operation), with clear Human Review requirements for each tier. The quantitative indicators for these mechanisms currently remain unknown [metric:ai_talent_density_per_sqkm] due to the lack of authoritative public statistics. This plan provides a guideline suggestion rather than a numerical conclusion.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

### Development Goals for Industry and Functional Layout

The industrial organization within the Overall Design Area is not categorized by industry but by **validation stage**—this is the primary distinction between this scheme and conventional industrial zoning. The reason is that the technological categories in the AI industry change much faster than the urban space update cycle. If categorized by sectors such as 'large model zones, smart terminal zones, embodied intelligence zones,' the classification would likely become invalid within five years. In contrast, the 'validation stage' is a stable structure; regardless of how technology evolves, any AI application must undergo a process of validation—incubation—scale-up.

The proposed industrial indicator system is suggested to include three groups: **Capacity Class** (number of verifiable scenarios, number of public validation records, Human Review coverage), **Density Class** (AI talent density, area of R&D carriers per square kilometer), and **Output Class** (scale of industrial output, number of enterprises). This plan can currently calculate and verify the verifiable nodes in the Capacity Class, which are 9 points [metric:verification_node_count]; for the Density Class and Output Class, due to the lack of publicly citable district-level statistical data, the statistical scope remains unknown [metric:ai_output_value_billion_cny]. This plan suggests that the statistical scope should be redefined based on the boundaries of the Overall Design Area as defined in the announcement, rather than continuing to use the current street-level scope, otherwise the indicators will not correspond to the planning area [assumption:A-INDUSTRY-001].

Functional proportions in terms of land use structure are as follows: Research and development land use (0802) accounts for approximately 22%, commercial and service land use (05) accounts for about 17%, green spaces and open spaces (1401/1402/1403) account for about 21%, residential land use (0701/0702) accounts for about 15%, educational land use (0804) accounts for about 8%, road land use (1207) accounts for about 10%, and blank land use (16) accounts for about 6%, with the remainder being cultural, medical, sports, and other public service land use [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001][metric:land_use_feature_count]. This proportion is a Conceptual Recommendation, meaning that research and development and green spaces together occupy more than 40%, reflecting the goal of treating environmental quality and research space equally in a high-quality urban area where talents aspire to live. The residential land use at 15% is relatively low, indicating that residential land use within the current scope is already limited. Therefore, this plan emphasizes improving the balance between work and residence in the original community and the Xiao Yuehe section rather than adding large-scale new residential areas [depth:land_use_layout]. All proportions must be recalculated after the Official Boundary is released.

Integrating with Haidian's "1+X+1" industrial system, this proposal advocates for the "AI+" integration focus in three directions: AI+ Education (relying on the dense university conditions, and with relatively controllable validation costs), AI+ Healthcare and Health (high social value but with the strictest requirements for Human Review, suitable as a test bed for validation systems), and AI+ Content Consumption (building on the market foundation at Dazhongsi). The spatial allocation for these three areas corresponds to the educational shared land use in the original community, the healthcare service land use along the Xiaoyuehe section, and the intelligent native consumption land use at Dazhongsi [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-028].

### Urban Renewal Overall Framework

Updated spatial structure as **one track, five segments, three stations, and multiple points**: one track is the north-south verification track; five segments are the Zhongzhiyuan segment, Qinghe seam segment, AI Origin community segment, Xiaoyuehe segment, and Dazhongsi segment; three stations are three key areas; multiple points are nine distributed Public Space verification nodes [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-001][depth:overall_spatial_structure].

The reason for choosing this structure over a conventional "double-axis multi-center" approach was explained in the three-layer scope chapter—due to the site's width-to-length ratio of approximately 1:7, the horizontal width is not sufficient to support a second parallel axis. This judgment is directly derived from geometric recalculations rather than compositional preferences, and it is the most credible conclusion in the spatial outcomes of this plan because it relies solely on area and span scales, independent of boundary precision.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The Land-Use Layout adopts a structure of five segments in the north-south direction, with east-west divisions within each segment, comprising 38 land-use faces. This structure achieves a seamless and gapless coverage of the Overall Design Area, with a coverage residual of 0 square meters [metric:land_use_coverage_gap_sqm][data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001]. This topological result is directly reviewable and verifiable: any two land-use faces do not overlap, and the union of all land-use faces equals the submitted boundary.

The functional logic and validation loops correspond to five segments: **Zhongzhiyuan Segment** (accounting for approximately 26% of the north-south length) is dominated by research and development land use, with configurations for research and development, talent housing, and ecological validation parks; **Qinghe Seam Segment** (14%) is dominated by green spaces and squares, fulfilling the function of seam integration, with the highest proportion of seam green spaces and squares on the ground [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-008]; **AI Origin Community Segment** (24%) is dominated by educational and research and development transformation land use, with configurations for youth communities and community services; **Xiao Yuehe Segment** (20%) is dominated by living services, healthcare, culture, and mixed residential areas, being the segment with the densest AI-Enabled Scenarios; **Dazhongsi Segment** (16%) is dominated by commercial services and intelligent body R&D, with configurations for station square and cultural display [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-032].

Each segment is configured with approximately 6% open space (coded as 16), totaling about 6% open space across the entire area [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-038]. This is the most significant active choice at the land use level in this plan: **reserving uncertainty with a defined proportion**. The reason is that the spatial requirements of AI industries are difficult to predict within five years, and if all land uses are assigned specific functions, the plan would become ineffective in the face of technological advancements; the specific scale, location, and management methods of the open spaces must be confirmed by a professional team according to the official control plan [assumption:A-CONTROLS-001].

Building level, the combined gross floor area of the 14 indicative functional groups is approximately 175.5 million square meters, with a coverage rate of about 15.4% [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm][metric:building_coverage_ratio]. **It must be emphasized again: this is the gross floor area of the indicative functional groups, not the result of a current building inventory, nor a control for building scale or density.** The total planned building scale, Floor Area Ratio, and Building Height in this package are all kept unknown [metric:total_floor_area_sqm][metric:floor_area_ratio], due to the lack of approved control conditions, and because the assignment prohibits the agent from making such statutory judgments [assumption:A-CONTROLS-001].

The update intentions are categorized into three types, all of which are **type intentions rather than site schemes** [assumption:A-BUILDING-001][depth:retain_renovate_demolish]: **retain** 3 sites, including the northern segment of the existing industrial buildings, the university shared platform, and the Tsinghua Garden Station cultural building, as their functions or cultural values are already established, with renovation benefits outweighed by risks; **renovate** 5 sites, including Zhongzhiyuan R&D cluster, the original point community incubation cluster, the original point community commercial interface, the Xiaoyuehe living service cluster, and the Dazhongsi consumption complex, as their structures are usable but their functions and interfaces require updates; **new build** 6 sites, concentrated on validating functional gaps (experimental cluster, governance office cluster), talent accommodation, and connection hubs. The ratio of the three categories (3:5:6) reflects the judgment that **renovation and gap-filling are the main focus, with no large-scale demolition** — this is both a pragmatic response to the complexity of the current ownership and aligns with the low-disturbance update orientation. Any judgment regarding the Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy at the plot level, disposition of ownership, and engineering feasibility conclusions must be completed by a qualified professional team based on the official control plan, site investigation, and ownership verification. This plan does not provide such conclusions nor imply them.

In terms of spatial supply and operational strategies, this proposal advocates for allocation based on the "validation phase" rather than "enterprise scale": the concept validation phase requires adaptable large-span spaces and shared testing facilities; the pilot production phase requires divisible and quickly reconfigurable medium-sized spaces; and the scaling-up phase requires standardized office and business spaces. This supply sequence naturally aligns with the spatial conditions of the three zones, which also explains why Zhongzhiyuan requires large-scale concentrated carriers while Dazhongsi is suitable for high-density urban interfaces [depth:land_use_layout].

### Transit-Station Integration with Static Traffic

Integrating functional layouts around existing rail stations is a requirement of the announcement. The spatial response of this plan is to concentrate the additional intensity and public functions around the station areas, specifically at the Dazhongsi Station-City Integration Transfer Hub [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BD-012] and the Station Forecourt Validation Plaza [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-005]. The design highlights of the four quadrants pedestrian connectivity at Dazhongsi Station include continuity, barrier-free access, and prioritizing the ground level: the rationale for prioritizing the ground level is that it is the most friendly to elderly and disabled groups, and it has the lowest implementation cost and engineering risk, aligning with the S12 Scenario Card's judgment that "technical solutions cannot replace physical barrier-free renovations."

The parking of non-motorized vehicles needs to be systematized to avoid occupying pedestrian spaces and fire lanes—this is the most common practical issue in the current vicinity of the station. It is recommended to establish centralized parking areas around the station and at the validation track nodes, and to incorporate smart scheduling research directions. However, this plan does not specify a technical supplier, nor does it make any particular equipment a necessary condition [assumption:A-SCENARIO-001].

### Integration of Municipal Infrastructure with New Infrastructure

The judgment for this proposal is that the municipal demands brought about by the AI industry mainly do not involve an increase in total quantity, but rather a **change in distribution form**—from centralized to distributed, and from stable load to fluctuating load. Therefore, three integrated directions are proposed, all of which are **Conceptual Recommendations** and **do not include energy load, municipal capacity, or engineering feasibility calculations** [assumption:A-CONTROLS-001][depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]:

Its first component, **Integrated Edge Computing with Architecture**: small-scale computing facilities should be placed within research clusters and community service stations, close to application scenarios, to reduce long-distance data transmission. The waste heat from these facilities could be considered for integration with building heating systems, though the specific feasibility would need to be assessed by a professional team. Its second component, **Distributed Energy and Three Major Facilities Coordination**: in new clusters, explore the integrated layout of photovoltaics, storage, and traditional power supply, with specific schemes to be determined by a specialized plan. Its third component, **Validation of Data Infrastructure**: this is a unique feature of this plan—public validation requires a set of public data release and retention facilities (public monitoring data, validation records, and audit logs). It is recommended that these be built as a public infrastructure to avoid inconsistencies in record formats across different scenarios, making it impossible to compare them.

### Public Services and Innovation Service Facilities System

The facility system is organized into four categories: **AI Industry Service Facilities** (testing and certification, pilot spaces, shared experimental conditions, concentrated in Zhongzhiyuan); **Innovation Service Platform Facilities** (technology transfer, intellectual property, compliance consulting, concentrated in the Origin Community and the Zhongguancun Wing direction); **Talent Life Service Facilities** (affordable housing for young people, community services, sports, and cultural facilities, distributed in the Origin Community segment and the Xiaoyuehe segment) [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-021]; **New Infrastructure** (edge-side computing power, distributed energy, validation data facilities).

The specific facility scale and standards must be determined by a professional team based on official configuration standards and population forecasts, and this plan does not provide metric values [metric:ai_talent_density_per_sqkm].

## Centuries of Jing-Zhang culture, Zhongguancun culture, and AI new culture integrated narrative

### Three shared structural elements of culture

Most "cultural fusion" approaches present the three cultures separately and then connect them with phrases like "inheritance and innovation." This plan believes that such a connection is hollow, therefore, it first identifies the actual shared structure among the three.

The core of Jing-Zhang Railway culture is not the result of being the "first autonomous railway," but rather **making verifiable judgments under the acknowledgment of constraints**—the turnaround at Qinglong Bridge was due to an unavoidable slope, and this judgment can still be verified by anyone standing in the same place. The core of Zhongguancun's innovative culture is not the title of "China's Silicon Valley," but **allowing failure and public post-mortems**—over four decades, numerous teams have experimented, failed, exited, and restarted, and this capacity for failure is its true asset. The new culture of AI is currently forming, and its most pressing need is precisely **explanation and re-examination** because opacity is the most significant social risk of current AI.

The shared structure is: **acknowledge uncertainty → make a judgment → leave a trace → accept scrutiny.** This structure forms the cultural narrative thread of this proposal, also explaining why a **"verifiable city"** is not just a technical concept but a cultural entity itself. **This proposal does not distort historical facts, with all historical statements based on public records; culture is not a decoration for technology but a source of concepts** [source:JINGZHANG-HERITAGE-PUBLIC].

### Space Cultural System and Guiding Routes

The cultural system is composed of three layers of carriers [depth:blue_green_public_space]: **Heritage Layer** —— the site of the old Tsinghua Garden Railway Station and the remaining railway relics along the line, bearing the function of historical reading [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BD-007]; **Innovation Layer** —— the record nodes of the innovation journey of Zhongguancun, suggested to be carried by the Open Source Exhibition Corridor, continuing the tradition of 'public review' into the contemporary era [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-003]; **Contemporary Layer** —— the Honor Wall and the Assembly Hall, recording the ongoing validation process happening now [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PS-002]. The three layers are spatially connected along the same validation track, forming a continuous reading from history to the present.

**Cultural Tour Route** (Conceptual Recommendation, approximately 9.7 kilometers in total, suitable for segmented experiences): Start at Qinghe Ecological Validation Park in the north → Zhongzhiyuan Governance Deliberation Academy (Contemporary Layer: see the standards being discussed) → Honor Wall Square (Contemporary Layer: see the recorded successes and failures) → Return Marker Square (Heritage Layer: see the spatial prototypes of geometric motifs) → Tsinghua Garden Station Cultural Forecourt (Heritage Layer: see historical judgments) → Open Source Results Display Corridor (Innovation Layer: see the public debriefing of traditions) → Xiao Yuehe Scene Experience Loop (Contemporary Layer: see validation in everyday life) → Dazhongsi South End Validation Gate (End Point: see market validation).

The narrative order of this route was intentional: **first see the current validation, then the historical judgment, and finally the results in people's lives**. The reason is that if the tour proceeds chronologically from history to the present, the visitors might interpret it as a nostalgic route. By starting with the current validation site, history becomes the context for understanding the present rather than just something to be visited as a relic.

### Signage, Identifiers, and Symbol Systems

The cultural sign system and the One Belt One Road overall logo system are **two parallel systems** that must be clearly distinguished [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]: the overall logo (turning point symbol) serves brand recognition and international communication, while the cultural sign system serves the reading of the site's historical context. These two systems are layered on the signposts—superior layers for verification track signage (logo system, node directions, scene states), and inferior layers for historical information (historical documentation, location of remains, time markers). Visually, they are distinguished by material and color rather than being mixed together.

The three components of the symbolic system: **mile markers** — continuous markers set along the validation track, serving as both historical mile markers and contemporary node numbers; **validation status indicators** — uniform graphical representations of the openness level of the scene (internal testing / controlled open / public operation) and Human Review methods, allowing citizens to know which level the system is at without needing to ask; **historical record plaques** — public citations and source annotations for historical records.

All fonts, images, portraits, trademarks, and paper images **must be used with clear authorization**. This proposal does not provide any unlicensed materials. It is recommended to use open-source fonts and to individually confirm the authorization for all historical images; the use of images of real individuals (including historical figures such as Zhan Tianyou) must be in compliance with laws and regulations and confirmed by the relevant parties. This proposal only cites public historical facts in the text and does not design for the application of any person's image or portrait [assumption:A-CASE-001].

### Characteristics of the City and International Communication Narrative

The urban character is positioned as **modest confidence**: not claiming to be ahead, but showing how it is being tested. The core narrative for international communication is expressed uniformly as: "A century ago, it acknowledged the gradient with a switchback; a century hence, it acknowledges uncertainty with public verification." (English direction: *A century ago it acknowledged the gradient with a switchback; today it acknowledges uncertainty with public verification.*) The accompanying communication structure is **"one assertion + a set of verifiable data + a visitable route,"** where the data comes from the publicly available metrics [metric:verification_node_count] [metric:green_ratio].

The international dissemination of this narrative leverages its **capacity for external independent verification**: any overseas visitor or media outlet can verify the authenticity of the failures recorded on the honor wall and the presence of the Human Review entry. The effectiveness of the dissemination does not come from promotional efforts but from verifiability—this is also the understanding of 'global AI governance discourse' in this scheme: discourse power comes from trusted records, not volume.
