# Open-Source Ground: Public Transformation of Knowledge-Production Space along the Jing-Zhang Railway

The Jing-Zhang Railway once carried knowledge, people and industry into Beijing. Today its corridor concentrates universities, research institutes, AI companies, transit stations, established communities and urban parks. This density already supports a world-class innovation corridor, yet walls, inaccessible ground floors, transverse breaks and fragmented services divide it into separate internal systems. Open-Source Ground takes the Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park as a continuous public base and weaves adjacent ground floors, institutional forecourts, transverse streets, stations, riverbanks and community entrances into an accessible, continuously learning and reprogrammable urban system. Universities, companies, residents and AI systems can then produce knowledge, space and public service together.

A continuous north–south public ground is stitched to campuses, parks, communities and transit through five transverse accesses. Fifteen-Minute Open Units distribute everyday services, while Ground Protocol Units govern entrances, opening hours, permissions, maintenance and recovery. Physical, digital, generative and knowledge grounds share the same plan, parcel and ground floor. The northern section becomes environmental-learning ground, the central section knowledge-compilation ground, and the southern section public-service-orchestration ground. Zhongzhiyuan, Beijing AI Origin and Dazhongsi each receive an independent plan, key section and representative public-life scene.

The design makes “open source” tangible through six urban rights: to see what is open, reach a public interface, use space and facilities, contribute a problem or knowledge, participate in rule revision, and exit a data or technical system. Space, knowledge, data and models, facilities, urban problems, public services and governance interfaces all become resources that people can access.

## Design Basis and Material Register

The proposal integrates three consecutive bodies of work. Site research establishes spatial facts and problems; comparative case study identifies transferable transformation mechanisms; the final design turns those findings into an overall structure, key-area plans, sections, scenes, projects and operating rules. The two internal studies are navigation layers within the design process. Every formal reference below traces back to an original official, public-institution, academic or open-data source.

The public qualification announcement and Agent Taskbook define a Coordinated Research Area of about 43.6 km², an Overall Design Area of about 11.4 km² and three Key-Area Detailed Design Areas. Site analysis uses an OpenStreetMap Beijing extract retrieved on 12 August 2026, EPSG:4548, a 250 m output grid and a 400 m activity observation window. Twenty-two maps examine location, transit, walking and cycling, barriers, urban fabric, vitality, innovation and education, public service, culture, commerce, blue-green space, renewal clues and key-area differences. [source:SRC-OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [source:SRC-AGENT-TASKBOOK] [source:SRC-OSM-BBBIKE-20260812]

The dataset supports corridor-scale comparison and priority selection. The analysis grid covers about 43.25 km² and identifies 3,975 points of interest. Estimated green space is 13.2%, water 0.7%, and building coverage 17.8%; median straight-line distance to a transit station is about 606 m. Walking and cycling catchments are adjusted for network supply and road/rail barriers. Real entrances, crossing delay, slope, opening hours and lived experience require field calibration.

![Site-research evidence chain](assets/figures/site-overview.png)

*The evidence chain moves from site reading to problem discovery, core contradiction and spatial action. The twenty-two analytical maps below provide the detailed evidence.*

Twelve in-depth cases were selected from 27 candidates. They address continuous public space, rail stitching, public interfaces of knowledge institutions, blue-green testing, heritage reuse, phased delivery and multi-actor operation. King’s Cross, Paris Rive Gauche and the southern Yangpu Riverfront form the core set. KIC, 22@ Barcelona, Kendall Square, Gyeongui Line Forest Park, The Bentway, Stockholm Royal Seaport, Shougang Park, Guiwan Park and Hong Kong Science Park add specialist mechanisms. Every reproduced image is tied to an original page and explicit reuse status.

The current design uses provisional extents. Official key-area boundaries, station exits, road red lines, ownership, building condition, river and flood controls, municipal capacity and rail-crossing conditions remain unknown. Dazhongsi is therefore represented only as a four-direction potential access framework.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and the Future City

### 3.1 Reading a Knowledge City through a Railway Corridor

The corridor extends from Xiaoyue River, Xuezhiyuan and research parks in the north, through Liudaokou, Xueyuan Bridge and university/research clusters, to Zhichun Road, Dazhongsi, Jimen Bridge and Beijing North Station. Three urban fabrics coexist: northern parks beside blue-green resources; central university and institute superblocks interwoven with compact neighbourhoods; and southern communities, commerce, transit and railway gateways. A single renewal template cannot fit all three.

The corridor’s potential comes from these systems coexisting; its difficulty comes from their separate rules and entrances. Knowledge production, technical testing, community service, rapid transit movement and park life sit close together geographically but remain out of phase operationally.

### 3.5 The Core Contradiction

Five findings reinforce one another: innovation and education resources are highly concentrated; activity and public service form local high values; transit and cycling provide broad potential coverage; transverse barriers and superblocks create real detours; and public ground floors and continuous blue-green interfaces remain thin. The core contradiction is therefore: **innovation resources are intensely concentrated, while interfaces for public transformation remain broken.**

The design focuses on interfaces. Knowledge enters public ground floors through courses, models and explanation; technology enters public space through bounded testing and human service; urban problems enter research systems through community points and open lists; parks, rivers and streets enter daily life through continuous paths. Each opening has a time, permission, accountable operator and maintenance commitment.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Urban Design

### 4.1 The Spatial Prototype of Open-Source Ground

Open-Source Ground extends open-source principles from code to urban space, knowledge, data and models, facilities, urban problems, public services and governance interfaces. Each public interface answers six questions: what is open, how it is reached, when it is usable, how one contributes, who revises it, and how one exits. Entrance, time, permission, responsibility, version and recovery form a Ground Protocol Unit. [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

![Open-Source Ground planning structure](assets/figures/design/open-source-ground-zoning-v1.webp)

*The continuous base, five transverse accesses, three north–central–south sections and three key areas form the overall spatial structure.*

| Public right | Spatial action | Everyday result |
|---|---|---|
| See | Make entrances, open content, status and responsibility legible | People understand conditions before arrival |
| Reach | Continuous accessibility, primary/backup routes and station links | Communities, transit, parks and institutions form complete paths |
| Use | Tier basic access, time-sharing, collaboration and controlled contribution | Everyday and specialised access have clear thresholds |
| Contribute | Urban-problem entry points, courses, models, tools and local experience | Real problems enter research and public service |
| Modify | Public evaluation, appeal, correction and version records | Rules adjust using evidence from use |
| Exit | Data deletion, equipment shutdown, activity closure and spatial recovery | Public routes and essential services survive technical change |

Five operations make these rights spatial: open institutional edges; thicken the public ground through park, forecourt and ground floor; establish transverse access; embed human service, tools, models and infrastructure; and manage temporary layouts, hours and technical modules by version.

### 4.2 Four Grounds in the Same Space

Physical ground provides walking, cycling, public ground floors, landscape, shade, sanitation, storage and logistics. Digital ground discloses status, environment, capacity, energy, sources and error. Generative ground assists bounded adjustments to shade, irrigation, lighting, activity layouts and service. Knowledge ground turns courses, research, community experience, maintenance and failure into public resources.

## AI Innovation Ecosystem, User Profiles and AI-Enabled Scenarios

Open-Source Ground organises a continuous loop: problem proposal → research and model → testing → public translation → adoption → feedback. Universities open problem statements, courses and shareable results; companies provide compliant testing and maintenance; communities and public bodies provide real problems and participate in evaluation; public space provides low-threshold experience, human service and exit channels. [source:SRC-AGENT-TASKBOOK]

### 6.3 Cultural Identity and Long-Term Appeal

Five functions structure the ecosystem: full-stack independent innovation, enterprise services, urban scenario access, talent/community service, and responsible governance. Three pilgrimage and honour-display landmarks — the Northern Environmental Learning Gate, the AI Origin Public Model Hall and the Southern Railway Knowledge Portal — connect railway history, Zhongguancun innovation and public AI ethics. A four-season programme publishes problems in spring, operates river learning in summer, hosts a Jing-Zhang Open Knowledge Week in autumn, and publishes model, fault, repair and exit records in winter.
