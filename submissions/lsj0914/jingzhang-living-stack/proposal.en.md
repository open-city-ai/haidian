## Three-Level Scope Framework

The Coordinated Research Area, Overall Design Area, and key regions are not three separate visions, but an Evidence Chain from ecological positioning to urban structure and then to implementable prototypes. The coordinated research area, covering approximately 43.6 square kilometers, discusses industry, talent, culture, and global collaboration; the overall design area, forming approximately 11.4 square kilometers, shapes the updated framework, land organization, transportation and utilities, blue-green spaces, and urban appearance; and the three key regions, spanning approximately 368.4 hectares, carry functional, spatial, and operational validation. These numbers are cited from the announcement, and the submitted geometry remains a provisional expression. [standard:PROJECT-OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [depth:three_level_scope_framework]

The overall concept is **"One Ridge, Three Courtyards, Two Wings, and Twelve Walkways"**. The one ridge is the Jing-Zhang Biodiversity Ridge, which integrates the railway culture timeline, blue-green water storage and thermal comfort, barrier-free slow travel, AI scenarios, and public feedback in one location. The three courtyards are the Mass Intelligence Full-Stack Validation Courtyard, the AI Origin Co-Learning Transformation Courtyard, and the Dazhongsi Urban Application Courtyard. The two wings are the Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing and the Xiaoyue River Scenario Enablement Wing. The twelve stacks bind space, users, AI, data, responsibility, KPI, stop/restart, and manual alternative paths into a single review unit. [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

This framework allows for different evidence to be accepted at different scales for the same judgment: the strategic layer uses case studies and innovation chains to explain "why"; the master plan layer uses land, roads, green spaces, and Public Spaces to illustrate "where"; the focus areas use scene cards, building envelopes, character journeys, and phased responses to address "who does what, how to stop, and how to review." Spatial relationships are seen in [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001], [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-SPINE], and [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-01], but their accuracy is limited by provisional boundaries.

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future City Research

Jing-Zhang does not need a traditional park with an AI label added on. Rather, it requires a complete ecological cycle of "research—talent—computing power—validation—transformation—public value—international collaboration." The habitat stack divides this chain into four layers: AI/computing power/data provision capacity, ecology/climate provision of urban resilience, public life/Accessibility provision of social legitimacy, and railway heritage/culture provision of long-term identity. Each layer must be constrained by another: technology must not bypass public value, ecology must not become mere decoration, culture must not be reduced to symbols, and operations must not rely solely on one-off events. [depth:overall_spatial_structure]

Six cases adopt the approach of "mechanism—restriction—transformation" rather than image mimicry:

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

After a comprehensive comparison, four Jing-Zhang strategies have been identified: first, to reduce the cost of innovation validation through authentic urban scenarios while protecting the public through data minimization and human intervention; second, to shorten the transformation chain by the adjacency of universities, companies, communities, and public institutions without excluding everyday life through closed campuses; third, to replace one-time large system implementations with small-scale, exitable experiments; fourth, to integrate responsible AI, accessibility, and resident benefits into competitiveness, rather than treating them as post-compliance requirements. The industrial space is zoned not by a list of companies but by their capabilities and risks, forming a shared stack: basic research and open learning, edge-side computing power and New Infrastructure, models and data governance, robotics and urban equipment testing, industrial services and technology transfer, public experience and feedback. The Zhongguancun Technology Services Wing provides capital, legal services, intellectual property, international collaboration, and computational services; the Xiaoyue River Scenario Enablement Wing provides ecological monitoring, Public Space experimentation, resident feedback, and cross-zone experiences. Before entering the scenario, companies must clarify data, responsibilities, stop thresholds, and asset disposal upon exit.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The habitat spine organizes the continuous relationship along the Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park from north to south, but does not treat the tentative alignment as an engineering control line. It is composed of four overlapping systems: a continuous and accessible system for pedestrians and cyclists, a blue-green system for rain and heat comfort, a narrative system for heritage and innovation, and a versioning system for scenario testing and public oversight. The four systems intersect at key nodes and maintain low intervention in ordinary sections to avoid the entire corridor being dominated by high-maintenance infrastructure. Attracting talent is not equivalent to office space. Researchers need open experimentation and social value, entrepreneurs need real validation and predictable rules, families need housing, education, health, and care accessibility, international visitors need multilingual support and long-term collaboration, and residents need a daily life that is not excluded by technology. All roles have feedback, appeal, and non-digital pathways; this is also the foundation for transforming "global talent attraction" into observable experiences.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

Land-Use Plan divides the tentative scope into functional stacks such as research and development innovation, industrial services, community amenities, green spaces, and public facilities, and ensures complete topological coverage as a baseline for machine validation. [data:geometry/land_use.geojson#LU-001] Each polygon shares boundary lines to avoid overlaps and gaps caused by independent drawing; classification is based on [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE]. The layers only illustrate conceptual functional proportions and scenarios, without implying land ownership, current legal uses, or statutory adjustment pathways.

The architectural strategy employs a "light touch to the ground, shared ground floor, flexible internal spaces, maintainable mechanical systems, and openable interfaces." The conceptual building sits within the participant-generated developable zone, avoiding the concept blue-green and Public Spaces; the Building Footprint only serves the internal recalculated area of [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm]. [data:geometry/buildings.geojson#BLDG-ZZ-01] The Floor Area Ratio, total gross floor area, number of floors, Building Height, and underground space remain unknown due to official conditions being missing, and no assumptions are made to manufacture precision. [depth:development_intensity_controls] [depth:height_massing_character]

The Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy must be approached with a survey-first classification: prioritize retention for railway heritage and objects with historical, community, or structural value; prioritize renovation for underperforming but clearly valuable objects; and prioritize rehabilitation for objects that can be improved through ground-level openness, equipment updates, or facade treatments. Temporary scenarios should use reversible supplements; only after assessing ownership, structure, cultural heritage, carbon emissions, and public interest should new construction or demolition be considered. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish] Since there is no qualified individual building inventory at present, no output demolition list at the building level is generated.

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

Blue-Green Space is the operational foundation of the habitat stack, not a background fill. The Habitat Spine translates the ecological relationships between Qinghe, Xiaoyuehe, and the parks along the river into conceptual water management, shading, thermal comfort, and biodiversity networks; specific river blue lines, flood control standards, soil, groundwater, and plant configurations still await official and specialized documentation. [data:geometry/green_space.geojson#GREEN-SPINE] [metric:green_ratio] only indicates the ratio of participant-generated green areas relative to the provisional boundaries, with a moderate level of confidence, and does not represent the statutory green space ratio.

Public Space is composed of three scales: continuous habitat spines responsible for passage and everyday stays, three courts for higher intensity interactions and scene validation, and twelve stack nodes for lightweight, reversible, and deactivatable service interfaces. [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-FORUM] [metric:public_space_ratio] Accessible pathways, shade, seating, drinking fountains, restrooms, care, and nighttime safety precede interactive screens; the space should still be usable even if equipment fails. [depth:blue_green_public_space]

The aesthetic system is based on deep indigo, habitat green, waterway blue, signal amber, and railway warm red, with track pillow rhythm, four-layer habitat platforms, and leaf/water wave signals forming the identifying elements. Building control only provides principles such as open interfaces, volumetric segmentation, integrated roof equipment, ground-level accessibility, and durable materials, without specifying unsupported height or aesthetic control lines. [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES] All three honor nodes adopt low-energy, reversible, and maintainable solutions and are subject to review by cultural heritage and community stakeholders.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

Implement the initiative not with a one-time "big platform" approach, but with three low-risk prototypes to establish a baseline: Public AI literacy and model cards, inclusive heritage tours, and smart maintenance of rain gardens. After safety, data, and responsibility conditions are clearly defined, three types of industrial tests will enter the second phase; cross-area spatial renovations will wait for control plans, ownership, traffic and municipal infrastructure, and cultural heritage conditions. The project list consists of four categories: scenario stacks, three-court Public Spaces, habitat spine discontinuities, and governance capabilities. [depth:renewal_project_list]

| Stage | Action | Threshold to Proceed to the Next Stage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–6 Months | Official Data Verification, Consensus Map, Three Low-Risk Rapid Deployment Scenarios, and Baseline Monitoring | Boundary, Ownership, Responsibility Subject, and Data Impact Assessment Completed |
| 6-18 Months | Testing of Three Categories of Industries, Phase One Public Space and Model Card Kiosks | Safety, Accessibility, Public Acceptance, and Operations Costs Evaluated in Phases |
| 18–36 months | Cross-Zone Habitat Corridors, Scenario Replication, Talent Services, and International Cooperation | Pilot Evidence Supports Replication and Clear Professional Planning Conditions |
| 36 months later | Annual Audit, Expansion/Pause/Exit and Long-term Operation with Living Stack Week | Continuous Compliance with Public Value, Ecological Benefits, and Governance Capacity |

Phasing Geometry [data:geometry/phasing.geojson#PHASE-01] expresses relative sequence rather than government timelines. [depth:phasing_implementation] Each phase has three outcomes: continuation, suspension, and exit. Continuation occurs when public value, ecological, technical, and governance thresholds are met; suspension occurs when evidence is insufficient, leading to a pilot phase; and exit occurs when risks exceed boundaries, resulting in cessation and rollback. RACI roles include government/owner responsibility for public interest and compliance, professional teams for planning and engineering review, enterprises for product and data responsibility, operations teams for site responsibility, universities for research and evaluation, and public engagement in problem definition and result oversight.

Living Stack Week Forms an Annual Public Route Along the Biome Spine: showcasing reviewed scenario versions, lessons learned, model cards, community contributions, and global partner issues. The event does not serve as a rationale for permanent infrastructure development; sponsorship, issues, and review conflicts of interest are transparent, and open outcomes are reusable within the scope of copyright, with problem closures announced for the following year. This transforms the annual call into a long-term community and global collaborative mechanism.
