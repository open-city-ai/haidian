04 / Land and lifespans

Short-lived devices serve long-lived public space

A 30-plus-year public substrate, five-to-ten-year reversible yards, 90-day pilots, and per-shift status cards form four lifespans. Retention, light adaptation, and removability lead.

06 / Mobility and blue-green

The public-first line precedes the service companion

A failed device may not turn a public route into a waiting room. Loss of connection, boundary breach, thermal anomaly, or damage triggers clear-route, human takeover, short isolation, and professional referral.

07 / Renewal and delivery

Survey first; build one removable 90-day prototype

Implementation follows stoppable milestones rather than corridor-wide deployment. Labour participation, accessibility, fire, electrical, insurance, and restoration are all gates.

P1 / baseline

After-shift census

Devices, shifts, parking, charging, faults, maintainers, and complaints.

P2 / reversible

90-day dock pocket

Human handoff, printed status, offline removal, and end-of-term restoration.

P3-P5 / tests

Three independent prototypes

Inspection, interoperability, and fair charging receive separate Go/No-Go decisions.

P6-P7 / commons

Retirement directory and Safe Dock Week

Make failure, maintenance, and destinations public; never substitute event traffic for safety.
