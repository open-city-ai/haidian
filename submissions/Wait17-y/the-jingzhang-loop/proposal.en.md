## 0 Executive Summary

### 0.1 Project Positioning

The **Jingzhang AI Innovation Belt** is neither a science park nor a scenic boulevard. It is the spatial carrier for global AI innovation infrastructure — an urban spatial system that enables AI innovation to move from "happening" to "happening continuously."

Haidian has the highest density of AI talent, enterprises, and research institutions in the world. Yet these resources are physically divided by 9 walled campuses, constrained by building stock from the 1980s–2000s, and limited by the absence of urban testing environments and international exchange facilities. The urban design of the Jingzhang AI Innovation Belt answers one core question: **How can spatial design upgrade the AI innovation ecosystem already clustered in Haidian from "happening in isolation" to "operating as a system"?**

### 0.2 Core Issues

| Diagnostic Dimension | Finding |
|---------|------|
| **Global Benchmarking** | Haidian AI talent density 9/10, enterprise density 9/10, research output 9/10 — but spatial quality only 3/10 (Kendall Square 7, Station F 8, Shibuya 9) |
| **Wall Effect** | 9 walled campuses physically enclose ~11 km² of innovation land; knowledge exchange is degraded to low-frequency chance events |
| **Broken Innovation Chain** | 6 spatial gaps exist across the full chain from basic research → technology development → experimental validation → product application |
| **Regulatory Plan Constraints** | All 5 regulatory plan conditions are missing (FAR/height/density/green ratio/setback); the proposal must put forward discussable spatial directions under zero regulatory plan conditions |

The core framework of this proposal is **The Loop** — a three-layer model that transforms urban renewal from a "land development logic" into an "innovation infrastructure construction logic":

```

```

## 1 Project Overview

### 1.1 Background

Beijing's Haidian District is one of the regions with the highest density of AI

**Three Districts**: Zhongzhi Park (Research Core, North), AI Origin Community (Collaboration Hub, Central), and Dazhongsi (Application District, South). These three are not independent parks located adjacent to one another, but rather three density nodes along a continuous innovation corridor, connected to each other by a developer corridor and rail transit systems.

**Five Nodes**: Knowledge Origin → Open Source Engine → Collision Hub → Experimental Platform → Market Interface. Each node corresponds to specific innovation functions, spatial typologies, and public interfaces.

### 5.2 Spatial Decision Matrix

All spatial proposals in Phase D derive from the analytical diagnosis of Phase B. The following matrix establishes the complete derivation chain from "diagnosed problems" to "spatial response principles" to "spatial strategies":

| B5 Gap | Spatial Response Principle | Phase D Spatial Strategy | Spatial Location |
|---------|------------|-------------|---------|
| ① Absence of cross-institutional sharing | Establish accessible innovation interfaces at campus edges; activate edges rather than demolish walls | Boundary Interface Model (three layers) + Innovation Display Windows + Cross-University Joint Laboratory Cluster | Zhongzhi Park |
| ② Absence of flexible R&D | Provide modular, monthly-scalable flexible spatial systems | Flexible R&D Space Cluster (50/150/500/1500 m² modules) | Origin Community |
| ③ Absence of urban sandbox | Embed time-segmented, tiered, regulatable testing environments within real urban blocks | Urban Sandbox Tier 1/2/3 + Experimental Platform | Dazhongsi |
| ④ Absence of launch destinations | Provide professional, rentable launch facilities to lower the launch threshold for small and medium AI enterprises | AI Product Launch Pavilion + Interactive Experience Zone | Dazhongsi |
| ⑤ Absence of capital interface | Mix VC-in-residence, roadshow halls, and startup workstations within the same building | Collision Hub · VC-in-Residence + Roadshow Hall | Origin Community |
| ⑥ Absence of international exchange | Provide a complete international visitor reception spatial chain from venue to residence | International Conference Center (2,000-person capacity) + Developer Flexible Residences (4 floors) + 24h Zone | Origin Community |

**Not a single spatial strategy exists independently of Phase B analysis. The spatial proposals of Phase D are products of that analysis.**

### 5.3 Developer Corridor

**(Conceptual Proposal)** Along the linear space of the Jingzhang Heritage Park, three spatial measures are conceptually proposed: East-West Stitching (enhancing pedestrian- and bicycle-priority crossing connections at approximately 10 major road intersections); North-South Continuity (forming a continuous "Developer Corridor" walking/cycling system with spatial amplification at nodes); and a Programmable Public Space System (establishing reconfigurable public spaces at the five nodes and key intersections—flat hardscape paving, movable furniture, modular power/network interfaces, and temporary installation capacity).

**What will not be done**: No new vehicular roads traversing the heritage park are proposed; no bridge or tunnel engineering or underground space development is proposed; no changes to the approved conservation planning boundaries of the heritage park are proposed.

## 6 Zhongzhi Park — Research Core

### 6.1 Core Contradiction and Strategy

Zhongzhi Park faces the greatest spatial constraint: nine walled campuses create the physical enclosure of approximately 11 km² of innovation land. Yet the spatial proposal cannot and should not demolish campus walls. The solution is not to eliminate boundaries, but to **translate boundaries**—transforming closed physical boundaries into layered innovation interaction interfaces.

### 6.2 Boundary Interface Model

A three-layer structure, from inside to outside:

**Layer 1: Institutional Core — Maintain status quo.** The academic and research core areas within campus walls. No spatial intervention whatsoever. High-quality fundamental research requires spatial conditions of "deep focus"—the enclosed nature of the Institutional Core is itself a guarantee of research quality.

**Layer 2: Shared Innovation Interface — Conceptually proposed edge transformation.** The transitional zone along the inner side of campus walls. The conceptual proposal uses "Innovation Display Windows" (ground-floor functional adjustment along streets + glass facade renovation) to transform campus ground floors that originally faced away from the street into open innovation display surfaces facing the heritage park. Campuses retain spatial property rights—the realization of open interfaces depends on the conceptually proposed "Campus-City Cooperation Agreement" governance model.

**Layer 3: Public Innovation Edge — Conceptually proposed city-side response.** Urban public space outside campus walls, immediately adjacent to the heritage park. The conceptual proposal establishes the Knowledge Origin node (heritage exhibitions + academic annual conference venue), programmable public spaces, and the "viewing side of the Innovation Display Windows"—allowing the city side to "actively approach" the campus boundary, rather than requiring campuses to "open up."

### 6.3 Core Spatial Strategies

**Strategy 1: Campus Innovation Display Windows.** The conceptual proposal transforms campus ground-floor spaces along the eastern campus edge facing the heritage park (approximately 1-2 km) into open innovation interfaces facing the street—ground-floor laboratories (glass facades making research activities visible to pedestrians), public lecture halls (50-150 persons), and achievement display galleries (real-time updated research posters and prototype displays).

**Strategy 2: Cross-University Joint Laboratory Cluster.** The conceptual proposal establishes 3-5 medium-scale laboratory buildings (conceptually proposed at 3-5 floors each, 5,000-15,000 m² per building) in the transitional zone between the two universities at the southern end of Zhongzhi Park, jointly used by two or more universities and enterprises. High-adaptability floor load capacity, centralized cooling, shared high-performance computing access, and modular laboratory units (reconfigurable within 1-6 months).

**Strategy 3: Shared Computing Infrastructure Reserve Zone.** The conceptual proposal reserves 2-4 ha of land at the northern end of Zhongzhi Park to accommodate a regional-level shared AI computing center (long-term, 7-15 years). Operated under a "public utility" model—serving university researchers, small and medium AI enterprises, and independent developers, lowering the computing access threshold for AI innovation.

## 7 AI Origin Community — Collaboration Hub

### 7.1 Core Contradiction and Strategy

The building stock of the Origin Community serves the institutional office and residential needs of the 1980s-2000s—long-term leases, fixed layouts, nine-to-five schedules. It cannot accommodate the cross-timezone, counter-rhythmic, highly flexible AI innovation activities of 2026. The core of the spatial strategy is **flexibility** and **mixing**—enabling spaces to be reconfigured according to the needs of innovation activities, and enabling different functions to mix vertically within the same space.

### 7.2 Spatial Structure: One Core, Three Zones, One Ring

**One Core**: The Collision Hub—the spatial and functional center of the Origin Community. **Three Zones**: Flexible R&D Space Zone (West) + 24h Zone (East) + Developer Residence Zone (South). **One Ring**: The "Innovation Exchange Ring" surrounding the Collision Hub—a functional mixed-use belt within a 5-minute walk.

### 7.3 Collision Hub

**(Conceptual Proposal)** The Collision Hub is the heart of the Origin Community, with the following functional configuration:

| Sub-space | Conceptual Scale | Function |
|--------|---------|------|
| International Conference Center | 2,000-seat main hall + 500-seat sub-venues ×3 + 50-person meeting rooms ×10 | Hosting NeurIPS/ICML-level international AI academic conferences |
| Permanent Hackathon Venue | 3,000-5,000 m² open large space | Year-round available hackathon venue |
| VC-in-Residence Space | 2,000-3,000 m², 5-10 VCs | In the same building as startup workstations—"Investors on the 5th floor, entrepreneurs on the 3rd floor" |
| Grand Conference Plaza | ~0.5 ha outdoor programmable plaza | Opening ceremonies, outdoor exhibitions, community days |

**Spatial Organization Principle**: The four sub-functions are mutually accessible within a 3-minute walk—a researcher should be able to walk from the conference venue to the hackathon space, see a roadshow, and encounter investors within the 15 minutes of a conference coffee break.

### 7.4 Flexible R&D Space Cluster

**(Conceptual Proposal)** Approximately 10 ha of land on the western side of the Origin Community. Modular units (four standard modules: 50/150/500/1500 m²), flexibly expandable or contractible on a monthly basis. Shared meeting rooms and laboratory infrastructure (fume hoods, cooling water pipes, high-speed networks). The conceptual proposal adopts a "Space-as-a-Service" model—enterprises pay by area used and time, without needing to sign 3-5 year leases all at once.

### 7.5 24h Zone — Global AI Collaboration Infrastructure

**(Conceptual Proposal)** The 24h Zone is not about all spaces being open around the clock, is not a night-time economy, and is not a night-time consumption district. It is **spatial infrastructure supporting cross-timezone AI collaboration**.

| Space Type | Operating Hours | Rationale |
|---------|---------|------|
| Late-night shared workstations | 24h | A rigid need for cross-timezone collaboration—2:00 AM Beijing time is peak collaboration hours with Silicon Valley/London |
| Late-night café | 24h | Supporting amenity for late-night workstations |
| Convenience store + pharmacy | 24h | Basic physiological needs of cross-timezone workers |
| Shared workstations in Flexible R&D Zone | 06:00-23:00 | No need for full-time operation but covers most cross-timezone windows |
| Open Source Ark | 24h | The collaborative rhythm of open-source development is not limited to any timezone |

The 24h Zone is equipped with a night-time safety management system (designated entrances after 22:00, dual security staffing, noise monitoring, monthly community forums) and operational boundary mechanisms (adjustment of operational scope if utilization remains below 30% for three consecutive months).

### 7.6 Developer Flexible Residences

**(Conceptual Proposal)** A four-tier stay system: 1-3 days (capsule hotel, 50-100 beds) → 1 week-1 month (serviced apartments, 100-200 units) → 1-3 months (serviced apartments, 50-100 units) → permanent (market supply). Core logic: lowering the threshold for "exploratory mobility"—an MIT PhD considering working in Haidian for three months and an entrepreneur deciding to relocate their company to Beijing require completely different levels of spatial commitment.

## 8 Dazhongsi — Application District

### 8.1 Core Contradiction and Strategy

The core contradiction of Dazhongsi: AI products lack validation channels in real urban environments, and citizens as "end users" are completely absent from the R&D process. The core of the spatial strategy is **embedding** and **participation**—embedding AI testing into real urban blocks, and transforming citizens from passive consumers into innovation participants.

### 8.2 Spatial Structure: One Axis, Two Zones, Three Nodes

**One Axis**: The southern section of the Jingzhang Heritage Park—a continuous innovation display axis from the Experimental Platform to the Market Interface. **Two Zones**: Experimental Platform Zone (North/Waterfront) + Market Interface Zone (South/Commercial District). **Three Nodes**: Experimental Platform (Testing) → Urban Sandbox (Validation) → Market Interface (Launch + Feedback).

### 8.3 Citizen AI Participation Model

Citizens are not test subjects of AI products, but innovation participants. A four-layer participation model:

| Layer | Objective | Core Spatial Carrier |
|:--:|------|------------|
| **Layer 1: Exposure** | Enable any citizen to encounter AI at the lowest threshold | Turing Market (weekend outdoor AI experiences) |
| **Layer 2: Experience** | Enable citizens to deeply experience AI products in real environments | AI Product Launch Pavilion Interactive Experience Zone |
| **Layer 3: Feedback** | Enable citizens' usage experiences to be systematically collected and fed back | Citizen Tester Center (recruitment/training/collection/compensation) |
| **Layer 4: Co-creation** | Enable citizens to upgrade from giving feedback on products to defining needs | Demand Co-creation Workshops |

**Ethical Infrastructure**: Citizen testers enjoy the right to informed consent (in-person signing of informed consent forms), the right to compensation (conceptually proposed at 100 RMB per test), the right to data control (ability to withdraw data at any time), and the right to exit (stop at any time without providing reasons). All test and feedback data involving citizens must be de-identified. The Citizen Tester Center establishes an ethics committee—2 citizen representatives + 1 academic ethics expert + 1 enterprise representative + 1 operations representative.

### 8.4 Core Spatial Strategies

**Strategy 1: Urban Sandbox Testing Zone.** The conceptual proposal establishes a regulatable city-scale AI testing environment in an approximately 8 ha area in central Dazhongsi—including street segments available for closed-off testing (2-3 segments × 200-400m) and testing plazas. Tiered access management: Tier 1 (all-weather, fully autonomous) → Tier 2 (designated time periods, human monitoring) → Tier 3 (closed time periods, engineer accompaniment). Streets function as normal urban streets during weekday daytime hours, and are closed as testing zones during designated nighttime/weekend periods.

**Strategy 2: Experimental Platform (Xiaoyue River Section).** The conceptual proposal transforms the Xiaoyue River waterfront space into an outdoor testing field for robotics/autonomous driving—multi-material ground testing segments, small obstacle courses, and low-speed autonomous shuttle experimental routes. Public observation areas and real-time test result display screens are established.

**Strategy 3: AI Product Launch Pavilion.** The conceptual proposal establishes a 4,000-8,000 m² professional launch space in the core of the Dazhongsi commercial district—a flagship launch hall (500-800 seats) + interactive experience zone + media center + pop-up concept stores. A "Venue-as-a-Service" model—AI enterprises rent by the day/week, lowering the launch threshold for small and medium AI enterprises.

## 9 Innovation Flow Spatial Support System

### 9.1 Core Positioning

The five innovation flows between the three districts are not abstract economic concepts—they need to be physically carried, accelerated, and visualized in physical space. However, what carries these flows is **spatial strategies and spatial conditions**, not fixed facilities built through government investment. This proposal does not provide "construction content for the five flows," but rather a system of spatial carrying strategies that supports the occurrence of these flows.

### 9.2 Knowledge Flow (Zhongzhi Park ↔ Origin Community · Bidirectional)

What is carried: research outputs (papers/datasets/model prototypes/open-source code) transmitted from the research end to the development end; industrial frontier directions and technical challenges fed back from the application end to the research end. **Knowledge Flow is bidirectional—not a one-way street of "academia outputs, industry receives."**

| Spatial Carrying Element | Space Type | Carrying Location | Carrying Direction |
|-------------|---------|---------|---------|
| Innovation Display Windows | Street-facing display galleries | Eastern edge of Zhongzhi Park | Research → City |
| Research Poster Walls | Linear display surfaces | Along the Developer Corridor | Research → Industry |
| Research Translation Center | Translation space | Origin Community/Collision Hub | Research → Application |
| Industrial Problem Wall | Display interface | Interior of Collision Hub | Industry → Research (reverse) |
| Enterprise-Academia Matching Space | Exchange space | Collision Hub | Industry → Research (reverse) |
| Joint Mentorship Space | Educational space | Joint Laboratory Cluster | Industry → Research (embedded) |
| Demand Insight Laboratory | Research space | Zhongzhi Park | Application → Basic Research (feedback) |

### 9.3 Talent Flow (Zhongzhi Park ↔ Origin Community ↔ Dazhongsi · Bidirectional High-Frequency)

What is carried: graduate employment/job-hopping/entrepreneurship/internships/visiting scholars/international stays—the multi-directional, multi-frequency, multi-duration physical movement of talent among the three districts.

Core spatial carrying strategies: **Developer Corridor** (transforming commuting paths into "mobile social networks"), **Developer Flexible Residences** (four-tier stays of 1 day/1 week/1 month/3 months, lowering the threshold for exploratory mobility), **Talent Passport Spatial Nodes** (seamless spatial experience of the same identity across the three districts), **Rail Station-Workspace Connectivity** (high-density workstations within a 5-minute walk of station exits).

### 9.4 Data Flow (Dazhongsi → Origin Community → Zhongzhi Park · South to North)

**This is an independent flow newly identified in Phase B analysis.** The distinction between Data Flow and Knowledge Flow: Knowledge Flow carries "knowledge understood and discussed by people," while Data Flow carries "structured information streams that need to be systematically processed and analyzed"—testing data, computing usage data, spatial usage data, and open datasets.

| Spatial Carrying Element | Carrying Location | Function |
|-------------|---------|------|
| Testing Zone Data Collection Nodes | Urban Sandbox + Xiaoyue River Experimental Platform | Physical collection points for AI product testing data in real environments (conceptual proposal) |
| Data Aggregation and Processing Center | Origin Community | Physical location for aggregation and standardized processing of multi-source data (conceptual proposal) |
| Data Feedback Nodes | 2-3 locations along the Developer Corridor | Real-time visualization of de-identified data as public information |
| Open Dataset Nodes | Open Source Engine | Physical access terminals for government/academic open datasets |
| Demand Insight Laboratory | Zhongzhi Park | Transforming structured data into research direction adjustments |

**Privacy and Ethical Constraints**: The spatial carrying strategies of Data Flow involve data collection and processing. The conceptual proposal describes only the conceptual locations of spatial nodes and the conceptual directions of data flows, and does not involve the design of specific data collection systems. Any collection and processing of citizen data must undergo independent ethical review and statutory procedures.

### 9.5 Capital Flow (Origin Community → Dazhongsi · North to South)

What is carried: seed → Series A → Series B investment, enterprise-startup matching, international VC introduction—the physical path of capital from opportunity discovery (Origin Community) to achievement validation (Dazhongsi).

Core spatial carrying strategies: **VC-in-Residence Vertical Mixing** (VC offices and startup workstations in the same building), **Roadshow Hall** (standardized roadshow space for monthly Demo Days), **Investment Story Corridor** (displaying growth narratives of portfolio companies along the southern section of the Developer Corridor), **Launch Pavilion Closed Loop** (portfolio company products launch at Dazhongsi, forming a physical closed loop of "financing → validation → showcase").

### 9.6 Public Feedback Flow (Dazhongsi → Origin Community → Zhongzhi Park · South to North)

**The only flow from south to north**—from the end-user side of AI products back to the basic research end. This is the most difficult to spatialize yet most critical flow in the entire Innovation Flow system—if the public's voice cannot be "heard" by the research end, AI innovation will become detached from social needs.

| Spatial Carrying Element | Carrying Location | Function |
|-------------|---------|------|
| Citizen Tester Center | Dazhongsi | Physical collection of citizen feedback, de-identification processing, and participant training |
| Feedback Processing Node | Origin Community | Structured processing and quality control of citizen feedback data |
| Data Feedback Nodes | Along the Developer Corridor | Real-time display of de-identified citizen feedback data |
| Feedback Gallery | Along the Heritage Park | Physical display of case stories "from citizen feedback to research direction adjustment" |
| Demand Insight Laboratory | Zhongzhi Park | The physical terminus where citizen demand signals are "heard" by the academic end |

## 10 Infrastructure Loop — Spatial Operating Mechanisms

The spatialization in Phase D of the five Infrastructure Loop sub-cycles defined in C1:

### 10.1 Spatial Openness Mechanism

**Core Question**: Are innovation resources closed or shared?

Spatial carriers: Innovation Display Windows (glass facades of ground-floor laboratories on campus street frontages) → Open Source Ark (24h open code collaboration space) → Programmable Public Spaces (no permanently fixed functions; designed as reconfigurable "spatial platforms") → Shared Laboratories (equipment and spaces open to multiple institutions and user types).

Capacity-type indicators (conceptual benchmarks): proportion of open interfaces on street-facing ground floors >60% (conceptually proposed target); shared facility area as a proportion of total innovation functional area >15%.

### 10.2 Talent Mobility Mechanism

**Core Question**: Does talent pass through or stay?

Spatial carriers: Flexible Residence System (four-tier stays from 1 day to 3 months, lowering the threshold for exploratory mobility) → 24h Zone (meeting basic life services for cross-timezone workers) → Developer Corridor (making daily commuting a social act) → Seamless Rail Station-Workspace Connectivity (within a 5-minute walk of station exits).

### 10.3 Data Feedback Mechanism

**Core Question**: Does the city "know" that innovation is happening?

Spatial carriers: Testing Zone Data Collection Nodes → Citizen Feedback Collection Points → Data Aggregation and Processing Center → Data Feedback Nodes → Demand Insight Laboratory. All data collection must operate within strict privacy protection and ethical compliance frameworks.

### 10.4 Public Sharing Mechanism

**Core Question**: Can innovation resources be used by non-specific users?

Spatial carriers: Shared Laboratories → Public Computing Access Points (conceptually proposed to operate under a "public utility" model) → Open Dataset Nodes → Open Source Ark → Turing Market.

**Core Proposition**: The most valuable innovation breakthroughs often come from "people who shouldn't be here"—a biologist who occasionally walks into an AI lab, a citizen who raises a need at a weekend market. The spatial design of the public sharing mechanism makes such "encounters that shouldn't happen but are extremely important" systematically possible.

### 10.5 Operational Cycle Mechanism

**Core Question**: After construction, who operates, and how is continuity ensured?

Spatial carriers: Mixed Governance Spaces (BID offices + community deliberation halls + foundation management offices in the same building/block) → Spaces with flexibly adjustable uses (adjusted based on operational data feedback) → Shared Facility Management Nodes (booking, billing, maintenance management).

The value of infrastructure is realized in operation, not at completion. The operational cycle mechanism requires physical places where operations teams can be permanently present, collaborate, and make decisions—not "build and hand over," but "remain present after construction."

## 12 Implementation Path — Implementation Loop

### 12.1 Three-Phase Overview

The Implementation Loop divides spatial implementation across the three zones into three progressive phases, each with clearly defined spatial actions, capability thresholds, and milestones. The three phases are not a linear waterfall, but a cyclical feedback loop — operational data from each phase feeds back into adjustments to the scale and timing of the next phase.

```
Phase 1: Connection (Years 1-5)    Phase 2: Capability (Years 5-12)    Phase 3: Global Network (Years 12-20)
Low cost · High visibility · Reversible    Medium investment · Structural transformation    Visionary investment · International competition
```

### 12.2 Phase 1: Connection (Connection Period · Years 1-5)

**Strategic Objective**: Break down the physical and information barriers between existing innovation resources. Not about building new things, but enabling what already exists to "see" each other.

| Zone | Conceptually Proposed Spatial Actions |
|------|---------------------------------------|
| **Zhongzhi Park** | Innovation Showcase pilot segment (initial 300-500m street-facing interface); Knowledge Origin node (heritage exhibition hall + small academic venue) |
