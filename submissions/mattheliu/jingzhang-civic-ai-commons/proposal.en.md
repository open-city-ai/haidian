### The site is not a blank canvas.

This deepening first corrects the misconception of "building a new innovation belt from scratch." The Jing-Zhang Railway Heritage Park Phase I has already been constructed and opened to the public, with an official announced area ranging from Qinghua East Road to Zhi Chun Road, a total length of 2.5 kilometers, and a total area of 16.8 hectares; it should be regarded as a public asset that already carries commuting, recreation, and railway memory, rather than additional green spaces added by this plan. [source:BJ-JINGZHANG-PARK-PHASE1] The 2021 planning interpretation also revealed long-standing issues of multiple parallel railway lines, east-west discontinuities, multiple ownerships, and segmented implementation in the linear space, [source:BJ-JINGZHANG-PLANNING] thus the main task of this plan is not to draw another ribbon but to supplement horizontal connections, public operations, and scenario agreements with accountability.

The same applies to three existing or under-construction initiatives serving as collaborative baselines: the Qinghe-Haidian waterfront has approved projects; the Xiaoyuehe waterfront is in construction push context; and the renovation of approximately 6.87 hectares of Public Space on the south side of Xueyuanlu, including seven nodes from the Jing-Zhang Heritage Park to the Xiaoyuehe Greenway, has been approved. [source:BJ-QINGHE-APPROVAL] [source:BJ-XIAOYUEHE-WATERFRONT] [source:BJ-XUEYUANLU-SEVEN-NODES] These projects are only marked as "existing/synergistic interfaces" and are not included in the additional construction volume, area, or performance metrics of this plan. The open status of Phase II is rapidly changing in public reports and must be refreshed with on-site and latest official documentation before formal deepening, with no single reported date treated as a fixed fact.

The former Qinghua Yuan Railway Station site has been designated with a cultural heritage protection range and construction control requirements, but there is currently no official GIS layer for verification. [source:BJ-QINGHUAYUAN-HERITAGE] Therefore, this plan only sets up the "Heritage Layer Import and Professional Verification Gate," without relying on the textual boundary descriptions to manually draw the red lines. Any nearby buildings, installations, underground works, and pedestrian organization must be evaluated after the formal protection range, construction control zone, and archaeological requirements are imported.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The three-level scope adopts the transmission relationship of "area coordination—overall structure—three-harbors validation." [depth:three_level_scope_framework] The Coordinated Research Area covers approximately 43.6 square kilometers, focusing on how universities, research, industry, capital, talent, computing power, data, and scenarios can coordinate; the Overall Design Area covers approximately 11.4 square kilometers, focusing on land use, updates, transportation, utilities, blue-green spaces, public services, and urban appearance around the Jing-Zhang heritage Public Space; the key areas cover approximately 368.4 hectares, separately validating the differentiated mechanisms of Zhongzhiyuan, AI Origin, and Dazhongsi. The three areas are approximate values, and the submitted polygons are still provisional, and cannot be used to cover the authoritative levels of the announcement and formal attachments with graphical precision.

The overall spatial structure is "one vein, three ports, five halls, and twelve nodes." "One vein" is a composite corridor of the Jing-Zhang historical narrative, pedestrian and bicycle paths, blue and green spaces, and public smart services; "three ports" are the northern full-stack validation port, the central open-source transformation port, and the southern intelligent experience port; "five halls" are public living rooms that are accessible, placeable, and for shared decision-making; "twelve nodes" integrate AI scenarios into specific Public Spaces and record privacy, ethics, Human Review, and operational boundaries. [depth:overall_spatial_structure] [data:geometry/roads.geojson#ROAD-001] [data:geometry/public_space.geojson#PUBLIC-001]

![Three levels of scope and land structure](assets/figures/land-use-structure.png)

Conceptual Recommendation: The three layers are connected through four feedback loops: the regional ecology proposes knowledge, talent, and scenario needs; the overall design translates these needs into space types and public interfaces; the three ports validate these through small-scale, reversible, and auditable prototypes; and the validation results are fed back to the professional team, community, and management to decide on continued implementation, adjustments, or exit. Any "placement" here is a conceptual recommendation and reference solution, available for the professional team to deepen their research, and does not constitute a government approval, land ownership, investment, or construction commitment.

To ensure that "One Vein" truly possesses urban depth, the overall design employs three interchangeable horizontal sections rather than a uniform green belt:

1. The segment parallel to the railway and park is organized around "operational safety interface, railway memory, continuous active transport, rain and flood garden, and community daily use." Additional reviews for lighting, noise, structure, fire safety, and night-time management will be conducted in the bridge underpass and elevated space.
2. **Campus and Park Boundary Segments** are organized around the concept of "controlled R&D, shared walkways, visible but desensitized work processes, public living rooms, and quiet boundaries at night," to facilitate open access in a managed manner, rather than assuming unconditional open access for the campus and laboratories.
3. **Community and Waterfront Segment** is organized around "flood/operations management, ecological buffer, pedestrian and bicycle movement, all-age stay, non-digital alternative services, and building interface." AI is responsible for information disclosure, maintenance reporting, and accessibility alerts but does not substitute for water monitoring or add unreviewed water-related structures.

All three profiles adhere to the principle of "originality first, daily use first, and maintenance first." Heritage is not a design element, blue and green spaces are not technological exhibition backdrops, and Public Spaces are not containers for equipment deployment. Only after the original use, management responsibilities, and exit conditions are clearly defined does the scenario proceed to the next step. [source:BJ-INNOVATION-CENTER-REGULATION]

Update the logic to prioritize "public interface first, test nodes first, and architectural decisions last." The first layer involves establishing a low-cost public interface using signages, activities, movable facilities, public code honors, and scheduled tests; the second layer connects universities, parks, communities, and transit nodes through pedestrian-friendly pathways and green spaces; the third layer only conducts professional judgments on the preservation, restoration, renovation, or new construction after obtaining the Official Planning Boundary, current buildings, ownership, control plans, and municipal data. [depth:development_intensity_controls] Total gross floor area [metric:total_floor_area_sqm] and Floor Area Ratio [metric:floor_area_ratio] should be maintained, avoiding the creation of precise impressions through conceptual massing.

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

The Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy employs a four-step process: the first step involves establishing a record of the current rights, status, architecture, era, purpose, structure, energy consumption, ownership, and users of the buildings; the second step identifies historical and cultural value, public benefits, and the potential for low-carbon reuse; the third step involves an assessment by structural, fire safety, cultural heritage, traffic, municipal services, and property rights professionals; the fourth step involves deciding through public participation and legal procedures whether to retain, repair, renovate, demolish, or construct new structures. Currently, there is a lack of data for the first step, so all conceptual volumes are marked as `massing_envelope_only`, without any specific promises regarding the demolition or retention of buildings. [depth:retain_renovate_demolish]

Building scale is suggested to adopt "along the vein leave white space, port aggregation, first-floor sharing, and reversible roof": continuous Public Spaces should prioritize retaining views, canopies, and pedestrian interfaces on both sides; the three port nodes can be organized with shared halls and atriums to accommodate composite functions; the first floor should reserve switchable spaces for launches, testing, services, and community activities; roof equipment, energy, and public activities must meet fire safety, noise, structural, and ownership conditions. Building Height, number of floors, Floor Area Ratio, and setbacks will be determined by official development intensity controls and professional design, and this scheme does not provide pseudo-precise numerical values. The northern "Open Verification Lighthouse" showcases testing methods, failure cases, and safety governance, without displaying unauthorized models and corporate brands; the central "Code Switching Plaza" features a public honor system for releases and contributions, scaled with open-source brackets and tie plates, allowing contributors to correct, retract, and supplement; the southern "Station for the Next Century" weaves a bilingual public course from the railway timeline, the innovation history of Zhongguancun, and AI ethical issues. All must be refined after heritage, green space, transportation, and accessibility reviews to avoid entertainment and internet-fame tendencies.

The cultural narrative unfolds in three segments: "tracks—switches—open-source protocol." Tracks correspond to the engineering and public memory of the Jing-Zhang Railway; switches correspond to the innovative culture of continuous selection of new technology paths in Zhongguancun; open-source protocol corresponds to the common commitment to knowledge sharing, responsibility, and human dignity in the AI era. The wayfinding system distinguishes between brand, historical cultural identifiers, and scene operation identifiers: the brand uses the J/Z negative shape, historical identifiers use time and source, and operation identifiers use a unified legend for whether data is collected, who is responsible, and how to make manual appeals. Urban Character adopts evidence-based colors such as "deep Haidian blue, Qinghe green, signal orange, and paper mill white." Eight critical data gaps have been written into `assumptions.json`: [depth:risk_missing_data] official overall and key area polygons, control plans and four lines, road and rail interfaces, parcel and ownership, current buildings, cultural heritage, pipelines and fire flood prevention, and public service baseline. (Demolish–Renovate–Retain Strategy)

AI risk control is based on five lines of defense: do not collect data unrelated to the issue; do not use black-box models to replace public decision-making; always provide human services, appeals, and exit options; test sites are small-scale, reversible, and logged; and provide equivalent services for children, people with disabilities, the elderly, and non-digital users. In public safety scenarios, AI is only used to assist in organizing public information and workflows, not to replace law enforcement, medical, fire, or emergency professional judgment. Any expansion of scenarios must be preceded by privacy impact, algorithmic impact, accessibility, safety, and public acceptability assessments.
