Land use, buildings and renewal

Seven conceptual land-use units organize relationships around the civic spine. They do not replace statutory land-use controls, ownership evidence or surveyed building conditions.

Retain first

Retain railway heritage, serviceable buildings, affordable innovation space, mature trees and active community uses.

Renovate selectively

Open ground floors, repair accessibility, improve energy, fire safety, shade, drainage and adaptable interiors.

Demolish only after verification

Demolition and new permanent buildings require ownership, structure, heritage, planning and lifecycle funding review. FAR and total floor area remain unknown.

Transport, Walking and Cycling Network, and Blue-Green Space

Accessibility, rail interchange, walking/cycling continuity, park ecology and public-service nodes are designed as one system.

Engineering boundary: cross-rail structures and major road interventions remain conceptual recommendations pending dedicated rail, road, municipal, heritage and safety studies.

Renewal projects and implementation

Stage 1 · Open verification

Surveys, public rules, staffed services, reversible furnishings and one 100-day pilot at each platform.

Stage 2 · Corridor co-construction

Repair successful interfaces, extend open ground floors, Walking and Cycling Network continuity, and blue-green links.

Stage 3 · Embedded iteration

Adaptive reuse and long-term infrastructure only after all permanence gates pass.

Permanence gates

Verified spatial, rail, road, heritage, ownership and fire-safety conditions.
Continuous accessibility and equivalent non-digital access.
Lawful minimum data, visible AI disclosure, complaint and deletion rights.
Public benefit demonstrated without hiding minority harm.
Named accountable operator and sustainable lifecycle funding.
No demonstrated displacement of residents, small businesses, low-cost space or free public access.
