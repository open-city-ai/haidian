Jing-Zhang AI Spine: Urban Design Proposal for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt

The Jing-Zhang green corridor that runs through the country's densest cluster of universities and research institutes is translated into a trustable public innovation interface, with three delivery disciplines — Recalculable, Stoppable, Accountable — running through the design: every spatial conclusion is recalculable, every AI scenario is stoppable, and every renewal project is accountable. This formal package is generated on a provisional boundary; the main text is written entirely as design decisions rather than brief restatement, preserving precision caveats and recalculation requirements.

Jing-Zhang does not lack compute — it lacks a public interface that makes innovation trustable: every figure is recalculable, every AI is stoppable, every project is answerable.

One-line test standard (anyone can verify it themselves): every AI on this belt must answer three questions at once — recalculable? (every figure has a source and a formula), stoppable? (with the AI off, a staffed counter still completes the same task), answerable? (when something goes wrong you can find a name, an appeal and a fallback). If any one of the three cannot be answered, it does not go live. This is the engineering wisdom of the Jing-Zhang Railway's herringbone ("人"-shaped) switchback, translated into the AI era: the switchback exists so the train can calculate the gradient, come to a stop, and have every switchback signed and accountable — the "Spine" of this proposal is not a formal metaphor but this public innovation interface itself; recalculable, stoppable and accountable are the three disciplines it must pass.

A Century in Dialogue: From Self-Strengthening in the First Industrial Revolution to Leadership in the Fourth

This roughly nine-kilometre Jing-Zhang green corridor is where two of China's self-strengthening stories — from two industrial revolutions — overlap on the same ground Source.

In 1909, a poor and weakened China was told that "the engineer who could build the Beijing–Zhangjiakou Railway had not yet been born." Zhan Tianyou (Jeme Tien-yow), leading an all-Chinese team, independently designed and built the country's first trunk railway. His herringbone switchback translated the steep 28‰ grade at Qinglongqiao into engineering wisdom a train could climb, stop on, and be held accountable for at every switchback — a self-strengthening project in which China learned from and caught up with the advanced technology of the First Industrial Revolution Source. The Qinghuayuan Station, opened the same year, lies within today's provisional extent of the Beijing AI Origin Community; in 1949 it became the first stop of the leadership's "journey to the capital for the examination," and after closing in 2016 it rests southwest of Chengfu Road. One small station anchors three identities at once: the 1909 origin of self-strengthening, the 1949 starting point of the "examination," and the 2026 origin of innovation.

One hundred and seventeen years later, on the same ground, China has become one of the leaders in artificial intelligence. With an AI core-industry scale of 350 billion yuan, 130 registered large models and 26 unicorns, Haidian carries a core share of China's role in global intelligent competition Source. The centenary Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt is not a fresh start on a blank slate, but the continuation and elevation of this self-strengthening thread into the Fourth Industrial Revolution — translating the herringbone railway's recalculability, stoppability and accountability on a grade into this proposal's three delivery disciplines for AI systems: Recalculable, Stoppable, Accountable.

This is the conception of the proposal: to make the Jing-Zhang AI Spine a public innovation interface that runs "from historical self-strengthening to intelligent leadership." As the flagship detailed-design anchor (see "Key-Area Detailed Design"), the Beijing AI Origin Community overlays the triple memory of Qinghuayuan Station, the engineering wisdom of the herringbone and the contemporary discipline of the Accountability Corridor in a single place — letting a passerby, beside the rail relics, read a nation's two self-strengthenings: the first time, we learned and caught up with the world's advanced technology; this time, we co-define the rules of the intelligent age with the world Source Depth.

The three levels are mapped line-by-line in the task-coverage matrix, so that every mandatory task in announcement clauses 1.3, 1.4, 1.5 and agent taskbook items 1–6 has a section, layer, metric, drawing and HTML evidence. The three levels are not a disjointed set of drawings: strategic research determines the industrial chain and urban-form judgment, overall design turns that judgment into renewal projects, spatial structure and facility support, and key-area detailed design verifies the implementability of specific parcels, buildings, transport, public space and AI application scenarios. The overall concept of this proposal is "Jing-Zhang AI Spine" (京张智脊): translating the Jing-Zhang green corridor that runs through the country's densest cluster of universities and research institutes into a trustable public innovation interface. Three delivery disciplines run through this design — Recalculable — Stoppable — Accountable — not as a design theme, but as self-check requirements on every design landing point.

  • Recalculable: every spatial conclusion (area, ratio, layer count, scenario node) is independently recalculable from the spatial layers and the indicator-recalculation table; the main text cites only recalculable metrics and locatable layers, never pseudo-precise numbers that cannot be reproduced from structured data Spatial data Metric. Commuting base and scenario-KPI anchors (public data): Beijing rail transit averaged ~9.9 million daily trips in 2024 (weekday average ~11.19 million); Metro Line 13, which runs through the innovation belt, has a peak daily ridership of ~1.6 million, currently the most crowded commuting corridor in north Beijing, and is being split into Lines 13A/13B (about 19 new stations) to add capacity Source. Meanwhile, the Jing-Zhang HSR has carried ~57.42 million passenger trips since opening on 30 December 2019 (as of January 2025); the ground-level old line from Beijing North to Qinghe has been released as Heritage Park space, and Qinghe Station has become a three-in-one hub of HSR, Metro Line 13 and the Changping Line (single-day peak ~44,500) Source. These ridership data support two design judgments: station integration and slow-mobility breakpoint repair must absorb high-intensity commuting flows, and the KPI baselines of scenarios such as "AI slow-mobility navigation" and "global AI activity-week route" (e.g. slow-mobility continuity rate, activity participation) are set against this commuting base, not invented.

Municipal and public services adopt a "chassis—increment—shutdown" topology Depth: the chassis layer is lighting, accessibility, emergency contact, water/sanitation, manual or telephone, static wayfinding and safety power-off; the increment layer is computing, robotics, booking, digital wayfinding and activities; the shutdown layer is local isolation, manual takeover, closure information, clearing/reset and event logging. Every layer states its supply entry, daily operation, fault isolation, manual substitution and reopening evidence. Energy, water supply/drainage, fire safety, communications, waste, noise, thermal environment and edge-computing capacity remain UNKNOWN, so the topology does not represent pipelines, loads, equipment or engineering feasibility; where budget, positions, substitution, maintenance windows, manual alternatives or appeal responsibility are missing, the corresponding increment stays not_activated [assumption:A-CONTROLS-001].

AI Planning Method Workflow (Diagnose — Generate — Simulate — Review — Iterate)

This proposal's AI planning method is a reproducible five-step workflow, not a one-shot generation Depth:

  1. Diagnose: identify existing conditions, breakpoints and constraints from the provisional boundary, constraint layers and the missing-data checklist Spatial data.
  2. Generate: generate land-use, building, road, green-space, public-space and phasing layers from the three-level scope and three-core positioning.
  3. Simulate: recalculate area, green ratio, public-space ratio and building footprints under EPSG:4548.
  4. Review: run the deterministic / spatial / visual / professional four-gate self-check and manually spot-check drawings.
  5. Iterate: after official polygons replace provisional ones or review comments arrive, re-run the scaffold, self-check and drawing/HTML generation.

This workflow guarantees that every step of "diagnose—generate—simulate—review—iterate" is traceable to a layer, metric or self-check evidence Standard.
