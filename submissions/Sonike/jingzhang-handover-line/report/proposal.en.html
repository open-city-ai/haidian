JING-ZHANG HANDOVER LINE

Intelligence moves. Responsibility stays visible.

The proposal translates a durable railway practice—shift handover—into a civic protocol for the AI era. Research hands a capability to validation; validation hands it to an open community; the community hands a service to residents. When the system is uncertain, offline, outside its boundary or challenged by a person, control must move back to an accountable human. “One line, three yards, two support wings and twelve reversible scenarios” is therefore both a spatial structure and a public-duty structure.

Executive Brief

One test decides everything here: for any AI service on this belt, the same task must also be completable without AI. If it cannot, the service does not open.

This is not a supplementary clause; it is the whole scheme's criterion. It replaces "smart city" metrics that no ordinary person can check — device counts, automation rates — with a question anyone can answer for themselves: without a smartphone, without reading Chinese, without sight of a screen, or simply not wanting to use AI, can I still get this done? If no, the service should not open; if yes, a smart layer has earned the right to sit on top of it. The railway shift-handover became the most reliable institution on this line precisely because it never assumed the next shift would catch what was thrown — it assumed the opposite, and required someone to sign in person.

Dual-Control Shift Ledger: outgoing and incoming are independent decisions

To make "handover" more than a field count, v1.6 adds the original Dual-Control Shift Ledger 0.3. The outgoing shift records scope, version, inputs, known failures and the no-AI service floor. The incoming shift must independently reproduce the packet and explicitly accept or refuse it; an unresolved item cannot vanish between shifts. The machine-readable contract is Spatial data. Its minimum example uses the low-risk SCN-05 accessible-route copilot with synthetic obstacle cards only: no personal data, no live service connection, deployment_mode=sandbox_only, human roles unassigned and performance results null Spatial data. The instance passes JSON Schema structural validation Metric Metric Metric, recorded in Spatial data. Zero structural errors proves only that the synthetic record is machine-readable; it does not prove route correctness, service availability, legal compliance or readiness for field use.

This version instantiates the protocol across all twelve scenarios and puts it through defect injection. Until now only SCN-05 had an instance, so the protocol could not be checked scenario by scenario — a rule set exercised on a single example says nothing about whether it holds elsewhere. Each of the twelve scenarios now carries one ledger Spatial data, with every field derived from the scenario attributes already registered in geometry/public_space.geojson (human_fallback, exit_condition, accountable_role, acceptance_gate, rollback_state) — no new design claim is introduced. The one design decision is how the control pair is split: the protocol requires release and receipt to be judged by different roles, and where a scenario's accountable_role names only one party the receiving side is identified from that scenario's exit_condition (for SCN-09, the community care roster, the first shutdown condition is "the person concerned refuses algorithmic allocation", so the receiving role is an advocate for the person receiving care, not the service provider). All twelve validate against the JSON Schema with zero errors Metric.

Seven protocol rules were each tested by injection. The rules are not newly written; they make existing constraints explicit: release and receipt must be judged by distinct roles (R1); a human-equivalent service must exist before the smart layer, with at least one channel available while the smart layer is off (R2); an open blocking item keeps the smart layer off (R3); no limited trial before a rollback drill has been observed to pass (R4); synthetic records must not touch live services or contain personal data (R5); a refusal must carry checkable reasons (R6); and a pair state cannot read as jointly attested while either role is unappointed (R7). Each ledger was first run as a baseline and then had each defect injected in turn — 96 checks in total (12 baselines plus 12 scenarios x 7 rules) Metric. Every baseline triggers no rule, all 84 injected defects were intercepted, and nothing was missed Metric; the per-check results are in Spatial data.

This proves rule closure only — not field performance, safety, compliance or approval. In all twelve instances receiver_disposition reads refused and smart_layer_state_after_decision reads off, because the three acceptance gates (site rights and accountable party; human-takeover and no-AI equivalent drills; data minimisation and public-interest review) are unmet in every scenario. On paper the protocol correctly refuses all twelve of its own scenarios — which is the behaviour it should have: prove the refusal path works before discussing release.

The three phases are not a timetable but three merge gates—data, ownership and professional assessment must pass before a merge; if they do not, the state rolls back to the last working one. Being reversible is the precondition for being evolvable, which is exactly why every component in the library must be removable and why "reversible insertion" precedes "investigate" in the four-step building decision. A city that cannot undo its own decisions cannot adapt.

The perceivable, interactive "AI+" mobility system is defined by two boundaries. Perceivable is not surveillance: only device status, aggregate crowding and surface obstacles are sensed — never faces, identities or individual traces — and every sensing device posts on site what it collects. Interactive means each handover node offers the five-step interface of arrive, slow, recognise, ask for help, continue, layered with the accessible-route copilot and tactile wayfinding so the system works for people who do not look at a screen. Three things are explicitly not done: no identity-based movement priority, no dynamic pricing, and no app installation as a precondition for passage or entry. The continuous green space system is carried by the Handover Line itself, where walking continuity outranks any smart function.

Multi-agent collaboration, required by the fourth co-creation principle, lands as a governance structure built on separation rather than headcount: a generating agent produces the proposal and geometry, a validating agent independently recomputes metrics and topology, a reviewing agent checks evidence references and compliance boundaries, and a dissenting agent looks specifically for overlooked groups and failure modes. No single party may hold two of these roles, and the generator may not certify itself; validation must be reproducible by a third party from the same data; any role may trigger a shutdown; final judgement rests with people and professional teams. This submission is itself a product of that process — geometry, metrics, figures and matrices generated from one model, then checked separately by four deterministic gates and human review.

What "integrated" and "fusion" actually mean here: integrated-planning substance and spatial–industrial fusion

Integrated-planning substance, in this proposal, is not a stack of sector chapters but different professions working on one recomputable data package. The traditional "integrated" output is an album that binds transport, municipal, green space and industry chapters together, with the chapters reconciled by hand. In this package the geometry, metrics, figures, drawings and three matrices are generated from one source, so a change in any chapter changes the number every other chapter cites, and anything that cannot move surfaces immediately as a contradiction. The claim is therefore: what is integrated is the evidence surface, not the album surface — whether an integrated plan is genuinely integrated is tested by whether all its sector conclusions can be falsified by one recomputation.

Spatial–industrial fusion lands on interfaces, not on a tenant list. This proposal names no company and promises no output value. The relation between industry and space is expressed as the interface specification of eight shared stacks — land, space, industry, finance, talent, compute, data, scenario — across the three areas Depth: each stack states the smallest exchangeable result, the intake condition and the refusal condition. The reason is falsifiability: a tenant list expires, an interface does not. Five years on the companies will have changed, yet the interface specification can still be checked for whether it holds. Industry is therefore not "placed into" a parcel; it connects through a gate that can refuse and can be exited, and the degree of fusion is measured by how many can actually connect and actually leave, not by occupancy rate.

These two share one premise with the territorial-planning claims above: the credibility of a planning deliverable comes not from the certainty of its conclusions but from who can overturn them, with what, and how soon. All three claims can be checked independently by a professional team without contacting the design side Depth.

A 1:1 prototype: the public handover table — the whole protocol shrunk to one table

The three sections above work at district scale. But a line named after handover ought to have one thing small enough to build first, cheap enough to remove the same day if it is wrong, and simple enough for others to copy if it is right. The public handover table is that thing: the smallest physical carrier of the whole dual-control protocol — one table and four items that must all be present at once.

Why this before the handover grounds. A handover ground is an area, entangled with tenure, fire safety, transport and sustained budget; any one of them unresolved and nothing moves. A table can sit inside existing public space on movable furniture and a short-term use permit.
